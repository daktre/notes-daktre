Thank you for the opportunity to review this paper. The paper seeks to explore OneHealth based strategies in an urban setting in western India based on quantitative data and qualitative interactions with frontline health workers in a large city in Western India. Overall the paper is well written and is useful contribution to the scarce literature in this domain. I have indicated comments based on a critical reading of the paper and categorise these into major and optional/suggestions.

Major
Abstract: the conclusions presented in the abstract appear to be too broad in scope and need to be framed sharper based on the findings reported in this paper. For example, The conclusions are currently framed as being at the level of demotivation of community health workers, where as the crux of the results examines the feasibility of whether these workers could be one health activist. Indeed while motivation does matter, the framing will still need sharper grounding within the results reported in this paper.

A relatively major concern is the framing/conceptualising of an OHA (see p.8 lines 173-178). When the ASHA (village-level CHW in India) was conceived, then too the idea of an “activist” was a part of the designation of this worker. And in line with this the incentive structure too was established based on specific outputs. However, the ASHA’s evolution has been far from being an activist and there is a body of literature building up exploring this aspect. Here the authors conceive a OneHealth activist; this requires some conceptualisation/description as to what did the authors have in mind when they explored feasibility of this role to be played by CHWs. I believe some of this might have influenced their tool development and thematic/framework analysis and hence is useful to include in a section. This will also allow the reader to form a clearer opinion on what was included in the remit of the OHA as conceived by the authors (or corresponding to a standard framework/definition). 

Just over half of the FHWs approached to participate, actually participated in the study (58%; p.6 study sample). This may have had implications on the perceptions generated herein and will need to be critically discussed or reported as a limitation. For instance, there could be a systematic reason why a certain “kind of” respondent is not well represented here (low-literacy respondents, less confident respondents, far-away/distant/absentee respondents?) in which case the results may overemphasise on some aspects that may not be generally shared? 

Regarding the FGDs (p.7), please include a few more details on the place they were conducted, how people were invited (voluntary or compulsory?) and how the process of FGD invitation & space may have influenced degree of comfort/participation. 

In their results reported in section 3.1.1 (p.9), the gendered nature of the results is stark. This merits a bit more reflection/discussion. FHWs and MHWs have rather different payment systems, roles & responsibilities yet demonstrate a rather different position vis-a-vis OHA. The role of gender and the identity beyond payment/incentives jumps out as being important (see for eg. results reported in section 3.1.3 as well including both motivation score & self-efficacy scores). However this is in contract to narratives in section 3.34 where authors report that an incentive package is the most important driver for FHWs. It is quite likely that incentives are being reported as an important driver but given stark differences in self-efficacy and motivation scores, there is a need to discuss this in more detail in my assessment. Could incentives be “necessary but not sufficient” overall in the OHA role? This is pertinent to the Discussion section (p.17) where the case study recommends considering FHWs as OHAs…..the idea of necessary vs sufficient may be helpful. 

Overall, the effect of wider systemic factors outside of OneHealth appear quite significant in shaping the OHA role and the framing of the conclusions needs to acknowledge this and ought to restrict to the findings with respect to OHA presented in the results only. This is also because there are other factors outside of the OHA scope that were not studied in this case study and hence this ought to be discussed either in a separate limitations section or as a point under discussion (along with few other limtiations poined above). 


Minor/optional 
The expansion for OHA is not provided at the first instance that it appears (page 4).

While the authors discuss the feasibility of community health workers playing the role of OHA, there is currently no structural requirement for the same, nor have there been policy moves in this direction (their framing of the current paper building upon previous work under RICOHA is duly noted but still…). It may be useful to discuss where this idea comes from in relation to the current policy context in India.￼ ￼￼￼￼ Currently, there appears to be an underlying assumption that the OHA role may be/ought to be situated among CHWs, but it is quite possible that there may be policy challenges to this. Perhaps this ought to be brought into discussion?
