I appreciate the extensive revisions, but two important issues remain, in addition to one relatively minor one. 

1. **Inconsistent citations and mixing up of anecdotal/experiential knowledge on the topic**
Below are list of several instances. Due to the time taken, I have only indicated the important ones, but there are several other instances where the language and writing needs to be tightened to clearly represent information emerging from a review of the literature and not from anecdotes and experiences. If the latter is being presented as well (which is not at all unwelcome), then they have to be separated and systematically presented, and not mixed up along with the review of literature. 

1. **Need for  copy-editing for language and grammar**
Extensive copy-editing for grammar, language and style is needed. (See for example usage of numerals for numbers less than 10 at several places in the manuscript). Also several unsourced assertions (for example, “It was noteworthy that great depth of understanding of the 2 diseases led the nation to launch the two national programmes.”) and use of superlatives that may not be emerging from the papers reviewed. Although these appear minor, they may significantly hamper the quality of the information presented in the review. 

1. **Precisely characterising the evidence in favour of fluoride causing goitre**
I appreciate the careful revisions made by the author following the review. However, one major issue still remains. The link between fluoride excess and thyroid function is being put across as a very clear, direct and causal link in the abstract and several places in the manuscript. However, the evidence-base presented to make a strong direct causal link between the two still has gaps (as the authors acknowledge at several instances) due to the following reason:
1. Link has been shown in limited animal studies
2. Association has been shown in epidemiological papers from the 70s and 80s (refs 22 & 24 from 1941 and 1972 respectively) 
I would advice reformulating sentences to convey the current evidence base. Author themselves summarise this “probability” rather than clear and direct causality on p.6 “Fluoride excess may be a probable cause of persistence of goiter even in iodine sufficiency”. I would suggest to harmonise all instances in the paper where direct causal link is implied to be brought in line with the high probabilistic pattern indicated by the largely epidemiological and animal studies till date. See the following instances:
- In the abstract where author states “The major issue is that both F ̅ excess and iodine deficiency caused goiter”
- ”The results cited provide ample evidence to prove that F ̅ causes enzyme inhibitions resulting in non-production of thyroid hormones, in spite of the fact iodine intake was adequate.” (p.19) (For eg. just a bit later on p.19, the authors themselves insert “possibly” to bring in the probabilistic nature of the causality)
