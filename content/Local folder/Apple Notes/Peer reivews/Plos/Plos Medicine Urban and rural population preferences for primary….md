Authors report on the results of a discrete choice experiment (DCE) to explore the probable drivers of choices for care for Hypertension. They present a strong justification for undertaking this study in southern Karnataka in the context of ongoing reforms & policies related to non-communicable diseases (NCD) in the Karnataka & India context. Their findings are discussed in the context of specific ongoing reforms related to health and wellness centres which has potential for improving care for hypertension and hence the gaps they report have policy and practice relevance in addition to their scientific contribution.

On p.5 lines 125-127, in justifying district choice, have the authors overlooked the possibility of proximity of Kolar to Bangalore as being a driver as well? If yes, consider stating that as well. 

Again on p. 5, lines 131-135, authors mention use of block maps from immunization micro-plans. Are these the same as administrative “biocks” (district-block-village admin hierarchy that is often followed?). If yes, clarify. Note also the additional confusion with the use of “blocks” to represent set of choices (p.6, lines 172-175). Consider minor revision in terminologies to avoid this. 

Can you clarify how villages were chosen within a given block (for eg. 2 out of 343 villages have been chosen). Perhaps also discuss implications of such choice in terms of the social, geographical & demographic characteristics of the chosen villages: which kind of perspectives/settings might be over/under-represented?

Process of instrument development is very well described and the effort at comprehensively attribute

On p.7, authors report use of “previous diagnosis of hypertension or elevated BP at the time of survey”. Often in the southern Karnataka rural context, medical documentation is absent/unavailable. Was this assessed based on self-reporting? Given that “self-reporting” is a wider use terminology, why not use that? If not, clarify/justify. 

A relatively minor point related to the DIscussion on AYUSH doctors (p.14, line 344) which I am not sure how it could be resolved. From the methods description it appears that the study’s attribute related to care provision by doctor or nurse never distinguished between MBBS/AYUSH doctor or nurse (although another paper that the authors cite for a DIFFERENT purpose than this does seem to indicate acceptable standards of care from AYUSH doctors as well). Given that the treatment for hypertension that the study examines is largely pharmaceutical treatment and assessment based on modern medicine, the introduction of the possibility of AYUSH doctors in the Discussion poses a challenge and if retained, merits a more detailed discussion. Suggest to revisit this. 

Reviewer signed: Prashanth N Srinivas, Institute of Public Health, Bengaluru, India
