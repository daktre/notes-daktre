Thank you for the opportunity to review this paper. The paper aims to identify implications for nursing and midwifery practice in India through qualitative inquiry that analyses perceptions on the governance of these sectors among nursing and midwifery “leaders” across five states of India. The background makes a strong case for their inquiry in terms of outlining key issues pertaining to nursing and/or midwifery education as well as its governance. Authors need to address relatively minor issues with (a) framing of the objectives, (b) clarify their position with respect to characterising certain perspectives as “global”, (c) providing more details on the tools (document analysis and interviews), (d) details of the handing of data from respondents from Switzerland and UK. 

The conclusions are concisely stated and well presented. 

Objectives
They identify clear objectives nested within their inquiry into nursing/midwifery education and governance, of which the second one is very pertinent and draws from body of literature building up on this both in-country and elsewhere. The third objective - in my opinion - ought to be included as discussion rather than specifically stated as an objective. The reason for saying this is two-fold: firstly, the purported purpose of such an inquiry into governance is not purely academic but indeed to contribute to the wider discourse on public policy and its reform; and secondly, framing recommendations for this entire sector may require wider consultative processes beyond the scope of this paper. If this is not acceptable, then a statement of limitations ought to be considered which allow the reader to set a boundary upon the study recommendations. 

Global perspective as HIC?
Authors choose to present “global perspectives” from nursing and midwifery leaders in UK and Switzerland. While a case is made by them to justify Switzerland as representing somewhat a global hub through its playing host to a slew of inter-governmental and international organisations, the characterisation of these perspectives as “global” is problematic. Apart from excluding large parts of the world in these perspectives, increasing literature on the need to decolonise global health has demonstrated the limitations of uncritically embracing these views as global (enough). At the very least a strong limitation is needed and at best a revision of this characterisation itself. 

Tools Document analysis as a method: It appears that the authors undertook a document analysis that contributed to their analytical lens as well as to their recommendations/discussion. Please consider clearly describing the methods used to identify documents, what was extracted and how it helped with the analysis of hte interviews. Some portions f the results (for eg. 3.1) are results of a review process which has not been described in sufficient detail in terms of what data was extracted and what kind of framework/lens was used to summarise results. 

Interview tool: Was a tool used and is it available? Provide more details

Respondents from Switzerland and UK
Lines 550 onwards, view of respondents from Switzerland and UK are shared. However, being presented under Discussion, it is unclear if these were interviews as part of this paper? Suggest to include further details about these respondents (in line with section above on characterising this as Global…) under methods. Was the same interview tool used? Analysis appears to be different: where they “check”/contrast perspectives from state and national interviews with the “global” ones? Please clarify with more details.
