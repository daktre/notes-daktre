Thanks for the opportunity to review this manuscript. This is an important contribution to the growing body of knowledge on PAR within health systems strengthening literature. The paper begins with a well-conceived research question and proceeds to use appropriate tools to scan literature and present a synthesis of this using critical interpretive synthesis. I have gone through the paper and do not have any major comments on the results and discussion, both of which are very well written. A few remarks/observations are made below to strengthen the earlier sections of the paper.  

The manuscript PDF has 2 numbering schemes for the lines. I have used below the continuing numbering scheme starting from 1 and ending in 975 (the other numbering scheme started afresh each page). 

Major comments

1. In lines 98-101, authors explain their position on the role of health manager vis-a-vis the components of the health system. I have 2 minor comments, possibly related to the way the sentences are framed rather than challenging the explanation of the authors: (1) The explanation related to how health management functions by “…brokering human relations…linkages” positions itself as the ONLY way in which health management functions. I would moderate this by using words such as “supposed to function” or “could function” or “has been posited…”; (2) When the authors say that the manager brings “…bringing together work carried in the six different components of the health system “, it appears as if the work related to the “different components of the health system” are happening at different places/people in isolation, which the manager brings together. Suggest reframing to avoid such mechanistic interpretation, which I am sure the authors may not wish for. 
2. Line 148: Authors state that “To the best of our knowledge, no such synthesis had been undertaken before.” Was there no attempt to search/scan literature for such studies? IF yes, it may be useful to state that as well, even if the scan was limited to recent literature. Later on from the methods it is evident that the authors engaged with a variety of PAR literature both from scanning literature or through contacting other researchers/experts. 


Minor/optional comments

1. Lines 32-35: Consider simplifying the first two sentences in the abstract, given that abstract may be much more widely read than the full paper
2. Line 40: First line under Abstract-Methods section: “eight selected articles that met….” (“that” is missing)
3. Line 98: Remove apostrophe for functions
4. Line 121: Replace is with as (define as)

5. Line 167: Open quotes is left open. 
6. Line 177 has an extra comma after “determining”
7. Line 248: Author name (Atun) has been spelt wrongly
8. Line 662: Authors possibly mean “contacted” instead of “conducted”
