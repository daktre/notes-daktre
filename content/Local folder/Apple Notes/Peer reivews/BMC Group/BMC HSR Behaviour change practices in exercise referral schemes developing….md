Thank you for the opportunity to review this paper. The paper describes the process of and results of development of a program theory for an evaluation of how behaviour change referral schemes to improve physical activity in sedentary patients could work, specifically focusing on how these programs could lead to change. The paper is consistent with the principles of realist evaluation which require the development of propositions in the form of program theories that could be evaluated using data to understand the process through which change occurs. 

Concise background making a case for the building up of program theories focusing on how change could occur in ERSs. The overall justification and approach for program theory building is clearly and well explained. 

Minor revisions suggested

1. The first paragraph in Study setting describes the position/positionality of the lead researcher vis-a-vis the research topic and possibly belongs elsewhere. On the other hand, lines 10-18 in the sampling section on p.6 may belong in study setting. For an international reader, it may be useful to better situate the setting geographically in terms of where it is, what geography and demographic were covered etc. Some bit of reorganisation may be needed across these sections. 
2. Authors do not seem to provide any references to the assertion that Swedish ERSs have achieved superior outcomes. Please cite relevant evidence for this assertion. 
3. The PT construction is well explained in the results particularly for PT 1 and 2. However, my overall impression is that the authors must try to improve the linking of empirical procedures that resulted in particular propositions in the results. For example, when they say “Practitioners explained that integration with GPs….influences their motivation and capability”, my understanding is that this is an emergent insight after analysis of multiple types of data (IDI, observations…others?). I struggled sometimes linking the results to the analytical procedures used. Although this is not straightforward in RE, better illustration of this if needed in a new section “Data analysis” may improve the paper. 
4. In PT 3, what is meant by supportive leadership is unclear. It is of course likely that respondents too meant different things when they characterised leadership as supportive (or not). How does the reader engage with this construct? Perhaps unpacking (to the extent possible the elements of this supportiveness may be important. 

Discretionary/optional revisions
1. last line of methods may be simplified to make it understandable to a wider audience beyond those who may be familiar with realist evaluation and qualitative frameworks
2. Authors assumption in p.3 (background) that ERS schemes are “used worldwide….” may not be true. Please check if there is some scoping/review on integration of ERSs in national health systems worldwide especially in several LMICs where this may not be the case. 
3. Although the assertion on p.4 with respect to the realist worldview on mechanisms is broadly ok, I believe situating the mechanisms in human action/people rather than the “world” could be considered (“realism suggests that the world possesses hidden mechanisms….)
4. On p.5, line 21: “routed in positivisim”. Review use of word “routed”
