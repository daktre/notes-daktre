Thank you for the opportunity to review this paper. This is a review of reviews, a synthesis of systematic reviews on what kind of professional-led parenting interventions could address social inequalities in health (SIH) that have origins in perinatal and early childhood. The authors have described the review process in accordance with reporting standards and the article is a contribution to the evidence-base for interventions that could address SIH. The background, methods and results section are particularly written well (especially the background which is concise and clear in building up from SDH to the specific aim of the article), while the Discussion section could benefit from editing for clearer language and for length. 


Major comments
1. In the abstract, as well as in the Discussion (p.18), the authors report that “…only a fifth had integrated them somewhat patchily”.  Both in the abstract as well as in the Discussion, it is important to describe why the authors come to this conclusion (of having integrated SIH patchily). Perhaps a description of what they would have considered a comprehensive inclusion of SIH in the analysis will help the readers understand what they mean better. 
2. In the section on “Literature analysis”, the authors say “The exactitude and the exhaustive nature of the data was validated by the experts”. This sentence needs to be rewritten for clarity. How did experts “validate” exhaustive nature of data?
3. On p.12, under the section on “Three distinct levels of evidence”, the authors report that references number 39 and 40 were evaluated to be of poor quality evidence and give reasons. The reasons supplied appear to qualify them for exclusion at an earlier stage. Clarify. 
4. On p.12, in the section on “The most effective programmes”, the authors cite one reference (ref number 42) in support of their conclusion on cost-effectiveness. They appear to be summarising the result of a single review, whereas the wording appears as if the authors have reported this conclusion (as if it were part of their synthesis). Clarify.  
5. On p.15, in the section on “A fragmented view of the SIH issue in the reviews studied”, authors report that 10 reviews addressed the issue of SIH, of which 4 incorporated them into their analyses. Later when they provide citations in support of the reviews that tackled the SIH question in their results and conclusions they cite 6 papers. Please clarify. The 4 that included SIH in their analysis are part of the 6 cited?
6. On p.18, authors note that “all the reviews shoed either proven or promising evidence…” whereas earlier on p.12 while discussing levels of evidence, they report otherwise. Clarify. 
7. All sub-section headings under the Discussion section titled “Taking action on teh SD of perinatal health: a methodological challenge in terms of effectively tackling SIH” need revision. Firstly, all the section headings are too long and unwieldy. And secondly, the sub-section title bears little connection to the narrative presented under the sub-section. For eg. “Take action early”, “Take action based on families’ needs” do not fully summarise the narrative presented under these sub-section heads. Please review carefully. 

Minor comments
1. Authors’ use of the word “thematic” in multiple places as a noun. I am not sure if this is an issue with my familiarity with how this word is used, but I am used to seeing it as an adjective. For eg. thematic analysis. Do the authors mean “themes”, or rather topics (in “…six reviews on the thematic.”)
2. Under aim of the reviews section, authors note that a majority of the reviews aimed to assess effectiveness. Later on they identify three reviews that do not deal with effectiveness and describe each one in detail. If all but three reviews dealt with effectiveness, it is perhaps useful to number the reviews instead of saying “majority”. Again in the section on “Principal prorgrammes studied”, the authors say “Generally, the reviews were centred on the practices of the professionals…” and identify three that looked at parental needs (and describe them). Is it safe to assume that all but these three were on the practices of professionals? IF yes, good to report numbers. 
3. On p.13 and elsewhere in the manuscript, review the use of the word “problematics”. Is this different from problems?
4. On p.14, authors report that “the hypothesis of the lack of parental skills is *a priori* assumed”. By whom? Clarify.
5. A general stylistic issue is the use of long phrases as section headings under Results and Discussions. Consider shortening them for better reading
6. On p.17, please explain/clarify the line “…parenting does not involve just the parents”
7. On p.17, the use of neighbourhood “organisations” needs clarifying. DO the authors mean the act of organising communities at neighbourhood level, or organisations/entities at the neighbourhood level. This is prone for misunderstanding. 
8. The reason for reiterating professional-led services at the beginning of Discussion is unclear. Is it because there is a scarcity of evidence on community/patient led interventions? Clarify. 
9. On p.18, while alluding to results of reviews, the authors use the word “…authors believed…”. Review use of belief in the context of reporting results. 
10. Under limitations, last line, authors mention “these programmes”. They are possibly talking of programmes that they studied, but it is unclear. 

Optional/discretionary comments
1. A sentence in conclusion “Acting on the determinants of health will lead to a reduction in inequalities” appears superfluous. 
2. On p.6, last line, consider replacing “withdrawn” with “removed”. 
3. The finding that there is an Anglo-American bias is also a function of searching databases that primarily index English language articles. Authors could consider noting this as a limitation as well. 
4. In the first line on p.11, consider using a better word for “parents’ story”. (oral history?)
5. On p.14, while citing ref. 30, they ascribe the paper’s findings to the “Cochrane database”. Rather consider saying “…a paper included in the Cochrane database reports…”
6. On p.17, results related to effective educational programmes at the beginning of pregnancy are mentioned. This is mentioned as being effective only in Europe. Is it because they were not effective elsewhere or else they were never studied/reported elsewhere? Consider clarifying. 
7. Consider mentioning high-income country bias (possibly because of limited research/systems capacity or limited resources in LMICs for parenting interventions despite greater need). 
8. Consider mentioning English language coverage as a limitation.
