Thank you for the opportunity to review this paper. I share my review from the lens of looking at the analysis, results and discussion from the perspective of this being a realist evaluation, as included in the title. The paper evaluates how an enhanced service embedded within the justice system targeting individuals with ND could improve positive outcomes for them. Evaluations of such programs are important contributors to improving our understanding of how to build more inclusive health and social care systems for people with ND, and may perhaps help overall inclusive health systems as well. However, there are two major issues with the paper which I describe below. Other relatively minor issues are listed separately. 

Firstly, on the framing of the paper as a realist inquiry. The authors begin with an IPT (with some problems in their framing but acceptable), identify CMOs and move towards (well articulated) refined PTs. However, their results and conclusion (both in the abstract and in the full paper) appear somewhat disconnected from the results of the thematic analysis. In a sense, I find that the thematic analysis was conducted in a classical qualitative analysis manner but does not attempt clear alignment with the realist inquiry. The results section especially does not clearly build on the figure and table (which are grounded in RE terminology). This is reflected in the reporting of the results in the abstract for example which do not appear to help the reader understand “what worked, for whom, and under what conditions and why”, typically the starting (and end-point) in an RE. For example the column “Evaluation” in Table 1, where I expect would be the demonstration of the link between the thematic analysis and the RE does not appear to be so. I am unsure if this is due to a problem of navigating the reader through the analysis or whether there is a different understanding of the method of moving from the analysis to results that is not sufficiently clarified? If indeed authors wish to assert this as an RE then this needs to be remedied. That said, I find the results of the thematic analysis well presented and the issue appears only in situating/framing within an RE framework (specifically unpacking lines 33-40 on p.20). 

Secondly, there is a need for clarity on what the authors have considered to be “context” and what they identify as “mechanism”. This is not standardisable and this is in some sense a nature of the realist inquiry method but an *a priori* enunciation of this is useful for the reader to understand this. I find some inconsistencies in fully understanding this. To some extent it may be due to my own unfamiliarity with the specific judicial context in English courts that the authors evaluate. However, given international readership and given that the principles and definitions of these in the realist universe are available the authors I feel there is a need for greater clarity. For example, I find the mechanisms in the refined PT (Table 1) to be clearly in line with RE mechanisms in that they appear to be elements of human agency that rest within all, but are triggered by the program in relation to given context. However, what they identify as context in their IPT is difficult to understand. Some of their C is part of the program itself or is closely related to the program’s inputs. Some of the refined PTs too are ambiguous in their identification of context (see for eg. CMO2 and CMO3 where defendants with suspected NDs and defendants who self-identify for NDs are included under context). 

Other issues to address:
1. Consider including country in the title for international readership. This may not apply, for example in several LMIC contexts which do not yet have these services within their judicial systems. For eg. on p.5 CMHLDS is described in a line as if it would be a system that exists in any country. Better to ground this within the English system/setting. 
2. C in court has been capitalised in multiple places (see for eg. p.3)
3. Results and conclusion need to be rewritten “closer” to either their thematic analysis results and/or their RE (see also comments above on this). Results must also identify clearly “for whom” they apply and demonstrate greater contextual familiarity in line with RE
4. “Complex co-morbidities”: complex is undefined (p.5). Perhaps suffices to just say co-morbidities?
5. Consider reformulating the PT in a way that captures why the enhanced CMHLDS program is likely to work for ND individuals. All the elements of the PT are available. This is just an optional suggestion to the authors. 
6. On p.8 the method is termed “critical realist evaluation”. In the interest of consistency, suggest to stick to “realist evaluation” (if needed the links of RE with critical realism could be discussed - optional suggestion). 
7. On p.11 towards the end (line 59), “either” is repeated twice in the same sentence
8. Inconsistent C, M and O operational definitions (see comment earlier). 
9. Coding framework and its relationship to IPT could be an important navigational element that may help reader establish the connections between the thematic analysis outputs and the refined PT (Table 1) (Also see comments above on this). 
10. Thematic analysis is overall well presented and carries substantial and useful results that are very relevant to the evaluation. However, some sub-headings do not adequately connect to the theme. See for eg. “Values and attitudes” (p.17) which is not well reflected in the two verbatim shared. How was value conceptualised is unclear. Some of this may be contextual information that has not been included in the paper (this could potentially address the two major issues mentioned above). 
11. Strengths and limitations section needs to be more reflexive and outline the various limitations in unpacking the specific micro- and meso-contexts which do not figure much in the paper. Further, authors could also address some of the issues raised above through a more comprehensive section discussing the limitations.
