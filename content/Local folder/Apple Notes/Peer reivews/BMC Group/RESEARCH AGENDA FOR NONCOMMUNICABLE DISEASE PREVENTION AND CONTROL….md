Thank you for the opportunity to review this paper. Given the rising burden and current status of health systems, contributing towards shaping the research agenda for NCD prevention and control is an important topic. Firstly a general comment: While the overall consultative and consensus-building process described is appreciable, a greater involvement of “local” researchers and communities in framing the agenda itself, as well as its reporting in the paper would have added value to the paper. This is a general comment and not for addressing in the revision. 

The PDF had two numbering schemes, one of which begins afresh from 1 onwards each page, and another one which is a serial numbering that starts at the very beginning of the manuscript and proceeds till end of document. The latter are used whenever line numbers are referred to. 

Major observations/comments
1. On p.4 lines 49-42 presents activities overlooked by the NPCDCS NCD cells. It is unclear how the authors identified these activities. Arguably, many more can be identified and this depends either on the framework we use to critically look at the program, the wider/comprehensive the framework, the more the gaps and at various levels. A systematic effort will  be needed to do such a gap identification/analysis and its methodology will need to be reported. If these are being reported anecdotally, that must be mentioned. 
2. While the justification presented for the NCD research agenda presented is well formulated, it is unclear if any attempt was made to see if such an effort has/is made/being made. This will be important and if none exists will also add value to this contribution. 
3. Table 1 provides stakeholder profile. There are some inconsistencies in identifying nature of the organisation. (a) non-profit status is mentioned for some and not for others (Medanta is a for-profit for eg.; while the NGOs are identified by their non-profit status, the for-profit ones are not), (b) there is overlap between the category “Academic” and “Research” and their distinctions unclear
4. Methods mentions the effort at trying to “ensure diversity”. However, the authors’ position on the axes along which diversity was sought is not mentioned and hence difficult to assess. For eg. did diversity across regions (geographical), location (urban vs rural), sex and gender, caste…to name a few of the participating individuals/institutions has not been mentioned. Suggest to specify the axes of diversity sought and to show data on what was achieved. 
5. On pages 4 & 5, the membership of CCB and SAG of a proposed(?) RCRE for prevention and control of NCDs in India is mentioned. The nature of this body is unclear. Is it a publicly funded/government body or a civil society/private sector initiative? This is important to mention to ensure that the positionally of the participating researchers and organisations may be suitably assessed given that NCD research agenda-setting is a contested space with several industrial and corporate interests which ought to be disclosed. 
6. I find the assertion that the actors group represented here (being on expert committees of one particular notional level body) as “broad range of stakeholders” problematic. While there is certainly diversity and great value in representing their views given their positions in the RCRE, a more modest framing is suggested. Important perspectives may not be represented in this membership and such a membership is also a function of how these committees themselves are put together which the researchers do not critically analyse in this submission (understandably outside the scope of this paper). However, when I read further down and see that 142 “experts” are mentioned I wonder if the authors have gone beyond the RCRE membership of one institution to a wider consultative process? If that is the case, more details on how that was done may be added to bring clarity on how people were chosen to provide these inputs. For example, details on how the 30 participants in the first online survey were chosen are lacking. Similarly, it is unclear who was/was not invited to the in-person meeting alluded to in Step 2 (was this a sub-set of the 30/140?). 
7. The statistical analysis presented is appropriate and clearly shown (but see limitations)
8. An important limitation of the exercise is that the agenda is also shaped by the reserchers’ own categorisation and conceptualisation of the problem that is reflected in their choice of “health issues”. The list reflects a certain framework at approaching the NCD agenda and could miss other ways of framing this. A critical reflection on how these were problematised in the first place and a discussion on the limitations that this presents in terms of framing the problem would add value to the paper and provide a clearer boundary within which to view the results. A further limitation of the exercise is the possible prioritising of more “tangible” interventions versus more “intangible” (yet important!) ones because of the nature of the exercise. For instance, interventions focusing on empowering vulnerable groups, social action at the community/village level etc may fall out merely because of the nature of the framework chosen to problematise, as well as the methodology and hence a more robust discussion of the limitations of this way of identifying NCD research agenda is crucial before discussing how to translate the said agenda.
