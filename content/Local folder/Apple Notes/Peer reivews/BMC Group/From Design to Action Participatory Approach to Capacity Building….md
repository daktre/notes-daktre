Thank you for the opportunity to review this paper. The paper outlines the process of co-designing an intervention to address the need to build community capacities in managing increasing drug overdose. The paper The paper is a useful contribution to the evidence-base on co-production of interventions with communities for public health problems. 

The background provides a strong justification for involving affected communities in co-producing interventions. The results describe the process and outputs of the co-production workshop that they conducted to design an intervention. The discussion presents insights from the co-design process, identifies enablers and challenges of doing the workshop in this manner. The results are detailed and well described and suitably illustrated with schematics and referenced to supplements. However, there are two critical areas which need to be substantively addressed:

1. Despite their accomplishments in doing participatory workshops, this is still the beginning in a longer process of learning how such co-designed interventions pan out. The intervention being described and implemented is a socially complex intervention with many aspects far outside the control of the designers (and hence their focus on participatory methods is well intentioned). However, the title, abstract and conclusion somewhat overstate/over-frame the current accomplishment. I feel editing these three to bring into context that this is a preliminary step in a long process is useful. What has been accomplished and described while substantive is only foundational to the ensuing interventions that the team plans to roll out and the title, abstract & discussion ought to reflect that. 
2. There are several aspects of co-design that need deeper reflection with respect to (a) position-laity of the team vis-a-vis the participants, (b) power dynamics. Both these are important elements in the co-design process and need to be described. 
3. Finally, a little more substantive discussion placing this work in context of various other co-production approaches may be useful. There is a paragraph currently which asserts agreement/alignment with current literature but perhaps a little more critical one citing other experiences where similar approaches may have been tried, even if not done specifically for overdose (This is an optional suggestion for the authors’ consideration and will not affect the quality of this manuscript).
