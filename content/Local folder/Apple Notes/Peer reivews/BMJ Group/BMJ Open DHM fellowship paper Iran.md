Thank you for the opportunity to review this manuscript. The study is a straight-forward pre-post study of a fellowship training for district health managers in Iran. The objective of the study and its methodology do not leave scope for results beyond the fellowship programme. 

There appear to be major issues that have to be addressed before considering this manuscript for publication, with respect to the description of the methods and tools, the reporting of the results and the points raised in Discussion. 

Major comments
1. One of the author’s objective to “design” the program does not appear to be dealt with in this manuscript. 
2. **Problems with the abstract**
	1. The data collection/extraction methods section of the abstract lacks important and essential details for the abstract to be understood properly. I would suggest to rewrite this section with a few more details on the type of study, tools used rather than make a statement about the validity of the tool (which can be covered in the manuscript)
	2. The “Principal findings” section of the abstract requires to be re-written for greater clarity. I am not sure if this is a problem with the analysis itself or in the language, but in its current form, it is difficult to understand the results. For example, the authors refer to “learning by practice” and “access to publications” and provide numbers in brackets for both of these as being the “most useful methods” (for what?). It is neither clear what these two are (learning by practice and access to pulications), nor what the numbers in brackets are.  Many of the findings reported do not have corresponding methods/tools associated with them (for eg. the manager’s self-assessment was a pre or a post-test). 
	3. The conclusion contains assertions that are not fully supported by the results. For example, continuous supervision and managerial support (which are in the conclusion) are not dealt with under principal findings. So, it is not clear how the authors have arrived at this conclusion. 
1. Strengths and limitations of the study appear to deal much more with the fellowship program rather than the “study” itself. The authors need to relook at this section in relation to their “study” (the methods, tools, results and conclusions) beyond the fellowship itself. 
2. p.5, line 3-10: Needs rewriting for clarity. 
3. Authors mention two cohorts of 89 participants. Does this make the total number of participants 178? This is not clearly stated. 
4. Data collection instrument: Authors say that they used “a valid and reliable questionnaire” and cite a paper which purportedly tests the validity and reliability. The paper they cite does not report on the validity and reliability of the tool, but rather in turn cites a report that is not publicly accessible as being the original from which their tool was used (with permission). The authors of that study say “It was based, with the kind permission of its main author, on one used in a study to evaluate the "Effects of Postgrad- uate Certificates in Teaching and Learning in Higher Edu- cation" [22]. The questionnaire was developed in Leeds, discussed and adapted with NPMC staff, and pilot-tested.” The authors need to carefully rewrite this section to reflect their position on the tool and how it was used/adapted and provide permissions (if needed). 
5. Authors mention a rating scale (Likert) of 0-20. This appears to be an error. 
6. Important details of the number of people it was totally administered to, at which stage in their fellowship. When was the pre-test done and when the post etc are missing
7. Results section requires significant rewriting. Some numbers are written in letters (p.9, line 16) whereas others are in numbers. There is little consistency in capitalising/sentence cases for sent. More importantly, the communication of results appears very ad-hoc. The whole section needs to be better organised to report all the results. The tables need to be carefully edited for clarity. For example, in Table 4, the last three column header says “Attitude, No (%)”. This is not understandable. Is it a number or a percentage? Again the total numbers reported in the tables does not match the earlier assertion of “two cohorts of 89 managers”. Similarly in table 5, the column headers seem to have different scales (some have 3-point and others 5-point scale, while the last one is a categorical Yes/No
8. Discussion sections has several assertions not supported by the results. For e.g.., “Moreover, due to the positive impact of training program, the organization and process of district health system would be changed.” Authors have not demonstrated “positive impact of the training”. Many other assertions about which was the most useful method or satisfactory method of “learning” are rather results than discussion. Many terms such as “working with tenures” are not understandable to a global audience. There are other inconsistencies: authors say “Also, based on the results, health managers’ satisfaction through online training was low.” This appears to be something that ought to be reported already under results in detail and discussed here. I was unable to find a detailed result reporting on satisfaction with online training. Similarly, several pre-post comparisons are made in Discussion but do not turn up in the results. There is a need for total rewriting of the discussion taking on board such inconsistencies.  
9. The study has several limitations and this needs to be included.
