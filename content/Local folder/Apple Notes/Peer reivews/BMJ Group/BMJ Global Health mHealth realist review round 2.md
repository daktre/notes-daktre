Thank you for the opportunity to review the revised version of this paper. The paper has been carefully revised to incorporate the comments I had submitted, as well as the comments of the other two viewers. My compliments to the authors on this careful revision of the paper. The clarity has improved. I also note the extensive revisions and improvements to the Discussion and strengths and limitations. 

Major comments
On page 8. line 192, the authors mention “…advice from experts”. If possible, please consider including more information on who they were and how they were consulted. Was the funder involved in suggesting experts (for eg.) Perhaps a little more information on the role of the funder (GE healthcare) in the funding statement could also help, as well as clarifying the nature of expertise and process. 

On p.19, lines 441-445 require rephrasing for clarity. Authors mention “…various contextual social and physical factors” and note “people who lack health education” as one of the “social and physical factors”(?). The next two lines too require to be rephrased for better clarity. For eg., is it the case that the interventions that they reviewed did not include detail about whether patients who had another chronic disease, did not did not have duplicate messages? 

I have noted below suggestions that may be considered as minor revisions/suggestions. I do not have any major comments to make in the paper.

1. On page 2, in the section titled abbreviations and definitions, the authors have included numbered references. I was not sure if these preliminary sections before the abstract can have numbered references. I believe these references add to the clarity and readability, but I am unsure if these are typically allowed in this section. 
2. On page 2 line number 48," what works **for** whom”. The word “doe” has to be replaced with for.
3. On page 5, in the summary box, line 85, the authors state that the burden of chronic diseases is an escalating problem in low and middle income countries. I wonder if it's useful to qualify the statement by highlighting that the burden of chronic diseases is a problem globally, but perhaps the response is insufficient in LMICs. 
4. On page 6, line 120, the authors say that in LMIC's mHealth mostly applies to the use of mobile phones. I wonder if it is useful provide references to this assertion. I am aware from grey literature that there are several tablet-based interventions that are being studied in several countries in Africa and South Asia, but I could be wrong. 
5. On page 7 line 131, the phrase"… Even when the effects were short-terk" caught my attention. Do the authors expect that short-term effects are easier to understand, or is it because many interventions/studies have short follow-up period? This may need some clarification for the reader. 
6. The focus on LMIC-rooted understanding of theoretical foundations of how/why mHealth interventions ought to/do work is perhaps good to address in the Discussions (or in limitations?). Do we have a good understanding of how/why they work in HICs? Why do we expect it to be different in LMICs? Is this perhaps because of specific country/macro contextual features of LMICs? Or is this due to a general lack of theorising on interventions in LMICs that we are in such a situation. These are some thoughts that occurred to me when I was trying to understand the entry-point of researchers into this question. I leave it to their best judgement on whether this is within/not the scope of their paper. 
7. On page 8, line 170, you could consider adding “the year 2000…”
8. On page 19, line 437, authors indicate that “…it is likely not all did have sufficient descriptions…” when they talk of studies included in their review. I wonder if they may want to drop “likely”, as they are talking about studies that they have extensively reviewed already and the know whether these studies have (or dont have) sufficient descriptions of the variety of settings in which they were conducted. Or rather, are they making a different point about why interventions tend to only be studied via RCTs (which is also a valid discussion point). 






**BMJ Global Health mHealth realist review round 1**

Thank you for the opportunity to review this paper. The paper seeks to review the theoretical foundations for particular kind of mHelath interventions to improve chronic diseases management in LMICs by identifying mHealth intervention elements that tend to work in a given context, using the realist synthesis approach. The detailing out of the program theory from the four papers is very well done and the careful adherence to RAMESES publication standard for realist syntheses is noted. 


Major comments 
- Justification for the review could be clarified a bit more. In the abstract, at least, the framing of the arguments offered appear a bit circular (reviews ask for greater understanding, so we did a review?). Or perhaps this is my mis-reading? Please clarify. This could be due to the ambiguity in this sentence: “Reviews suggest a greater understanding of the theoretical constructs that mHealth interventions target.” Do the reviews suggest that there ought to be a greater understanding, or do they suggest that there already is. 
- The framing of the conclusion lacks acknowledgement of the settings/context elements needed for the purported mechanisms that the authors identify to work. In the framing of a realist synthesis, acknowledgement and/or detailing out of these are vital. While the word-limit restrictions of the abstract format may not allow for this to be detailed out, the framing of the conclusion needs to acknowledge how their realist synthesis has/has not done this. See “Analytic purchase comes from the ability to describe and understand the many contingencies that affect the likelihood of such interventions generating their intended outcomes. This in turn provides guidance about what policy makers or practitioners might put in place to change the context or provide resources in such a way as to most likely trigger the right mechanism(s) to produce the desired outcome.” (From RAMESES publication standards 2013 by Wang et. al.)
- In lines 127-128 under Methods, the authors begin by saying “This realist review was used to guide our understanding of whether mHealth monitoring interventions are likely to work or not”. This sentence appears out of place. Authors are yet to describe the method and the results of the review and the sentence appears to already indicate how the review was “used”.  
- The limitations section does not sufficiently discuss the difficulty in looking within the diverse implementation contexts of the interventions. Like the authors acknowledge earlier, the papers being RCTs, it is likely that many of them (save for one which had a detailed process evaluation and other supplementary documents) did not have sufficient descriptions of the variety of settings within which their interventions were implemented. This certainly limits the ability to iteratively and critically examine the program theory elements that the authors bring up, and could weaken the explanatory/emperical power of their conclusion. I would suggest to briefly reflect upon this, and perhaps even discuss possible “hints” to varying contexts that may be apparent (or not) in their reading of the four papers. 


Minor/Optional remarks

Please treat these as suggestions or optional comments to the authors. 

- Consider dropping “patients’” in the title: “…monitoring of chronic diseases” vs “…monitoring of patient’s chronic disease…”. 
- In the summary box (what is already known about this subject?), re-look at this line for clarity: “ There is a greater need to understand the theoretical constructs in the intervention design **inclusive of** understanding the mechanisms that determine what contexts can influence outcomes.” (Emphasis mine)
- p.4, line 101, the authors state that “In LMICs, mHealth mostly applies to the use of mobile phones.” It’s useful to clarify in a line what all mHealth encompasses for a general reader of the journal who may not be fully aware of all the technologies covered under mHealth. 
- Consider asserting that realist review and realist synthesis are synonyms for readers who may not be familiar with realist approaches. 
- In lines 210-211, the mixing up of continents and countries in the sentence could be avoided. (Asia and South America listed along with Pakistan, Honduras and Mexico; confuses the reader).
