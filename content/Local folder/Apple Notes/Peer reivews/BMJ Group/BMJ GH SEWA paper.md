Thank you for the opportunity to review this paper. The paper is an important contribution to understanding the role of community engagement and the need for careful navigation in order to achieve equity in publicly funded health insurance programs. Overall, the manuscript is written well, is concise and is a useful addition to the evidence-base on how to improve publicly funded insurance programs and move towards universal health coverage. I have several minor/optional comments and a few more substantive ones which the authors could consider integrating in a revision.

Comments
- On p.5, the authors introduce SSK. Clarify SSK itself was envisioned as an “intervention”/program, possibly independent of AB and had its own long-term objectives pre-dating AB? It was also possibly designed & implemented (currently says only designed) by SEWA? This section could benefit from some sharp editing and clarirfication. 
- On p.7, the author's refer to “Population Council Institute” whereas the author affiliations point to an organization called “Population Council of India”. It is unclear whether these two are the same or this is a different one? If yes, explain relationship and role. 
- Perhaps the most confusing aspect for me in this paper was the use of the term mixed methods design in the data collection section on p.7. Mixed methods have evolved over a period of time as an entire methodology or approach to the use of qualitative and quantitative data in specific ways so that they may interact and together help in advancing scientific research. In the case of this study, the authors have already clearly specified in the previous section titled “approach”, that they use implementation research methods which already allows them to benefit from the use of qualitative and quantitative data collection methods as appropriate. The appearance of mixed methods as a study design is confusing in this sense, both due to its one time appearance here and more so, due to its location under the data collection section. 
- On p.8, the author's report ethics approval from an entity which is not affiliated with any of the author institutions. One of the essential requirements for effective ethics oversight is the ability of the review board or the committee to have a reporting relationship with the institute that employs them. Authors could consider clarifying how effective ethical oversight was secured through an approval from an entity that does not appear to be any of their affiliations. 
- On p.8, the authors describe the design as intended in present tense so the phrasing is unclear whether they speak of program intentions which they probably do. I wonder if the authors would consider framing it as program intentions “It was foreseen…the program sought to..etc” to clarify that these were program intentions and are not empirically verified results of what they observed. 
- On p.9, on the processes as implemented, the authors talk about the observation of three specific ways in which Sevika were engaging women and they present it in figure 4. Were these not intended already in the program? The current phrasing appears to indicate that the authors are observing and reporting what the SSK were doing in practice…but was it not intended in this way to have these specific platforms? 
- On p.10, and several other pages subsequently, several verbatim quotations are provided which are not cross referenced to any specific respondent. A better idea of whether these are all from one person or different people and wherever available or possible with a few more respondent characteristics that allow a better understanding and appreciation of these quotations may be useful. 
- On p.10, authors could consider re-examining whether the function performed by the Sevikas is a health service navigation or a health system navigation. There is extensive literature which pushes for a system or a health system to be conceptualised as being wider than the healthcare services. In that sense, the function being described appears to be a healthcare navigation or a health services navigation. 
- On p.13, the characterisation of the Sevika as the bridge is puzzling because the ASHAS indeed were supposed to be such a “bridge” between communities more so women and the health services. Thus the need of this new cadre which has been very clearly demonstrated by the authors for a very specific health service function, which is public insurance coverage to some extent, point out toward overburdened or poorly conceived slash implemented community health worker program? This is just a thought that occurred to me and need not be specifically addressed, but I offer it as a comment/reflection to the authors. 
- On p.16, the section titled “Trust in SEWA” has been described under the inner context. This was a bit confusing for me because the section under trust clearly demonstrates that trust has been an important pathway or even a social mechanism that explains their findings. From what I understand “Context” are composed of elements in the environment of the program which are not a part of the program and yet have an influence on the intended outcome of the program. In that sense, trust certainly seems to have an important influence on the outcome. Probably because it's one of the pathways through which this entire program appears to be working. In that sense, a careful and nuanced distinction between context and probable programmatic mechanism or pathway may be useful. The current phrasing of inner & outer context in that sense and the location of trust in the inner context - to me - is confusing and if retained will need robust backing from methods related literature on identifying context elements (inner & outer) and their characterisation/differentiation from pathways/mechanisms. 
- On the other hand, digital literacy clearly is an important context element. But I struggled a bit to understand why it is categorized as an “inner setting” (see earlier comment above on methods literature on this characterisation). Given that it is a contextual element outside the control of the program itself, which in turn influences the outcome. 
- The discussion section reads well but for me, with two relatively weakly developed components. (1) the characterization of some of the approaches as being empowering. I can see that there is a lot of empowerment components involved in what the author's record, but it does not come across strong enough for it to be claimed and demonstrated. So a suggestion to the authors to examine how perhaps they could explain/deepen the empowerment element that they assert and discuss. (2) A second element that does not come through strongly enough yet asserted both in the beginning (below the abstract) and the discussion is the strong government partnerships. There appears to be relatively scarce findings that locate the importance of such partnerships in the pathway/process of the change that they see. Perhaps this has not been clearly enough illustrated in the findings? Indeed, some of the elements in the discussion for example, in the last paragraph on page 18 are not specifically reported in the findings, and the author's could consider rewording such that the discussion develops and deepens the findings reported rather than bringing new explanatory elements.  

Discretionary comments/observations/
- Consider adding contextual/setting information in the abstract on the specifics of the study setting, the socio-demography etc to make it a bit more self contained. Many only read the abstract so such information may be useful.  
- On p.4, the authors use a phrase”the flagship scheme Ayushmann Bharat was introduced in 2018”. This appears after a semicolon. Please review for grammar (some editing needed here) 
- On p.4, the author's characterize the scheme Ayushman Bharat (AB) as adopting a continuum of care and cite its two major components as evidence of an adoption of continuum of care. This is not  clear how these two components of the scheme demonstrate an adoption of continuum of care
- On p.4, the authors state ”…dedicated staff to support beneficiaries in the hospital”. My reading and knowledge of AB indicates that one person is made available at “most” (if not all) hospitals and the person facilitates documentation, essentially an entry requirement/barrier for benefit from the scheme. My suggestion to the authors to clarify specifically what is meant here by “dedicated staff to support beneficiaries” beyond this. 
- On p.8, the author's could consider a paragraph that helps navigate the reader to the findings. A paragraph perhaps that explains how their findings have been organized and will be described. 
- On p.9, authors could provide more information on the sevika: whether many or all or some of the Sevika were also potential beneficiaries and whether they also belong to similar socio economic positions as their beneficiaries? 
- On p.10, the currency conversion of US dollar to Indian Rupees can benefit from additional information of the date or month during which this was the exchange rate. 
- Authors could consider making some of the subsections in the Findings in bold.
