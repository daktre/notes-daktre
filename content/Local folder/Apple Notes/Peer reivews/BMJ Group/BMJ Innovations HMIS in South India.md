Major

Authors use the PRISM framework and refer to its prior use in Kerala for HMIS evaluation. The reference is to a PhD dissertation thesis which is not available in peer-reviewed format. Hence, without presenting the details of the framework in this paper, it is difficult to review its use, assess its fit for this purpose and examine how it was applied. (The first reference to the framework itself (of the 2009 HPP paper is cited in a line which says piloting was done, so it’s unclear why the original paper presenting the framework is cited here instead of having a separate section on the background/introduction on the reason for its choice and its application)

A key gap that has to be acknowledged is that the assessment/evaluation of the HMIS is restricted to its direct users. People who exercise an oversight and/or are involved in decision-making based on its implementation appear to not be involved. This has to be reflected in the text where such a sharp definition of the overall sampling universe is unclear (see for eg. lines in participant selection and recruitment section which indicate sampling to ensure representation across various roles and capacity and different levels). 

In pages 6 & 7, many sentences begin with “It is recommended…”. It is unclear as to who is making this recommendation. Given the quotes cited in parentheses, I am assuming this is comping from the respondents. On examining the interview tool (which appears to be a structured tool), I see that there are no specific questions asked on “recommendations”. However, from the very transparent way of citing quotes that the authors use, it is indeed evident that respondents did make comments about their perspectives on the issues discussed. Please reconsider framing these as “recommendations”; in some instances, they dont appear to be: for eg. in the case of quote 21 which is summarised in the result was “It was recommended….”, on reading the quote, it could also be interpreted as the views of the respondent on what a good training is like and his/her critique on the current approach (rather than a recommendation). There are few other quotes summarised as “recommendation” which do not appear to be. I would suggest to re-look at how the thematic summaries are presented and reconsider framing respondent perceptions as “recommendations”. Another reason for this is because this section could be easily confused with being recommendations for HMIS arising out of the critical assessment carried out by the research team (which it appears not to be). Further confusing is that in some places, indeed the authors’ recommendations also appear; see for eg. p.8, line 25-25. 

On p.8, quote 24 is cited in support of the feeling of “resentment and a negative work culture”. This is not evident from the quote. Work culture is a fairly complex construct that has not been examined explicitly by the researchers and hence there is a need to critically examine if this is emerging from their codes and provide more theoretical support for this as well, if called for. Furthermore, please carefully examine quotes cited in favour of some results in this section. For eg. quote 29 is cited in favour of a “recommendation” (see above again on framing this as a recommendation) for “incentivising performance based on data entry quality” but that does not appear in this quote. 

Last sentence in the section “The role of Private Enterprise” needs rewriting/clarification. It begins with reporting on differing view of participants and rather reports a recommendation/observation of participants. Authors could consider categorising the nature of observations about hte private sector and listing the cateogories here. On examining the three quotes cited (32, 33, 34), there appears to be an overall three functions suggested in terms of private sector engagement: coordination, enforcement, education. 

The final section on change management in discussion needs better integration with the findings. The first line in this section mentions that “migrating from one form of data handling to another”. I presume the authors are mentioning about the transition/change from paper-based systems to digital systems? Or are they talking about specific HMIS models being implemented in their district? In either case, this section needs to be rewritten to better connect with the findings reported, given that change management did not appear in the background or has not emerged as a direct result within their quotes, albeit the overall framework of change management does fit within the dynamic nature of HMIS implementation in the district health system. 



Minor

P.4, line 3): Authors assert that “To the best of our knowledge, there have not been any prior qualitative evaluations….”. In my opinion, there is a need to qualify this. Why do they think qualitative evaluation in “our” setting is uniquely absent. Do they expect something drastically different in southern Karnataka than with evaluations say in Kerala or other states/districts? Or is it that quantitative evaluations reveal something different which they hope to refine/refulte using qualitative methods? It is unclear why this assertion is being made. Further, “our setting” is not properly defined (coastal Karnataka/Karnataka state/southern India/India). Depending on this definition, this assertion might need to be changed. And finally, intend of “to the best of our knowledge”, please consider doing a simple systematic scan of literature to back up such assertions instead of reliance on knowledge alone. If this has indeed been done, then this can be mentioned to strengthen the empirical grounding of such assertions. 

P4, line 38: “maternal and child health hospital” needs to be defined. Is this a category of hospital in the local health system? Is it government or private? To my knowledge, government health services are either sub-centre, primary health centre, taluka hospital and district hospital. Is this a unit within a district hospital? Unclear. 

p.4, line 39“…to foster familiarity with the participants”: Clarify nature of activities/interaction in such visits
p.4, line 40: Site selection was done “after a discussion with the research team, in-charge of respective centres”. This is unclear. Such centres (as mentioned by authors) do not typically have research teams. So, who is being referred to? Clarify. 

p.4, line 45: “Participants unavailable…..”This sentence is redundant. How can participants unavailable be included in a study that uses interviews as a primary data collection method? Clarify need to say this. 

P.6, line 41: clarify if the “incentive” is over and above a salary/monthly honorarium? 1000 per month is below minimum wage for a full-time job. Later on it is mentioned that these are contractual appointments, so this needs to be clarified. 


Discussion mentions “corroboration” by earlier studies. Please reframe sentence to reflect that this study corroborates findings in earlier studies (not otherwise as is implied).
