Thank you for an opportunity to review this paper. The paper is an important contribution to better understanding and acting on structural drivers of inequities that in turn drive health inequities among Indigenous communities in Guatemala. Further, it is also an important contribution to global health literature because many of the inequity and social drivers that the authors examine in Guatemalan indigenous settings are also seen in many other Lat-Am & south/SE Asian contexts as well. This work has enormous benefit for practitioners of participatory methods in any context as well as to other teams working with indigenous communities elsewhere. 

The Introduction is very well written and makes a good justification for the study. Except for minor points raised in the Minor observations section below, this section provides a good foundation for the paper. The country context and history is rather new for me. In this sense, a suggestion to authors to consider adding a paragraph or cite other literature which gives a good overview of debates/discussions (beyond health) of indigenous collective action & its wider socio-political context in Guatemala. This will be useful for readers unfamiliar with Guatemalan/Lat-Am context as well. 

Was there any effort at seeking some community-level gatekeeping/consultation in addition to the ethics approval? Was this deemed useful (or not?) If not, authors could reflect a bit on whether the University-based ethics permission was deemed sufficient/or not. 

The conceptual framework description is good. In addition, perhaps the authors could add a few lines about their own position vis-a-vis the project and communities that they interact with respect to indigenous identity or other social identities that are relevant for this study? Further, a bit more on the idea of “defenders” either directly describing or via citations is needed. Perhaps my limited context familiarity limits my understanding of their social and economic position (paid? voluntary? community-appointed?), educational background, role and influence in the context they work in. Defender characteristics may also be useful in understanding how homogenous (or not) this group is, their own relative age, indigenous identity and/or other attributes & power positions and how this relates to their work and responsibilities….in general a little more detail about the history & nature of their role and identity is important….This is important for global health readership to adequately understand this paper in my assessment. See for eg. “Defenders used their positions in their communities…” 

Under data management, sufficient details are provided. Authors can consider some reflection on the nature of “discussion spaces” that they created. How inclusive were they? How were power dynamics between defenders/study team and participants managed/mitigated? Who got included and whom not? and how many of those who participated actually appeared to have a voice (representation without voice is often stated as a phenomenon in many participatory studies esp involving indigenous communities). I dont see major issues here but I believe this may help other practitioners understand how these were dealt with. 

I struggled a bit with understanding the process/pathways type of assertions in the Results. How is the reader to understand how these were arrived at? The last line in the analysis portion does provide clarity on this but I feel the authors could re-look at how they describe the process of developing/building assertions on explanatory processes/pathwyas they describe in Results. For eg. “Political learning about health and participation rights and strategies for claiming rights was the catalyst”, “the defenders made important advances in activating rural users of services and their leaders to help make health service problems visible.”, “Defenders used their positions in their communities…” ….


Minor observations

“defenders, local collaborators, and field staff who support them in three municipalities”
This phrase in the Abstract may need re-writing to make it understandable to a wider audience which may only read Abstract. Ideally the Abstract must be self-contained by itself in my opinion. For eg. What is meant by “defenders”? “local collaborators” of whom? and “field staff” of what entity/project/initaitive?

“Neither conventional development aid nor a string of national policies to combat poverty and hunger has been able to narrow the drastic disparities (9).”
I couldn’t access this publication cited. Please review if this framing faithfully reproduces this citation. 

“Inequities persist because micronutrient supplement programs and conditional cash transfers address their manifestations but fail to engage with their causes.”
Please review. Persistence of inequities are wider than this in my understanding albeit these are important contributors to the persistence in terms of many “solutions” leaning on supplementation & CCTs.
