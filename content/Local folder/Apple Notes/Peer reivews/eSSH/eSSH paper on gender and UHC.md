Thank you for the opportunity to review this paper. The paper is an important contribution to the growing literature on gender inequities in health. Then entire analysis of the paper is based on 71st round of the National Sample survey in 2014.

**Remarks to the editor**
The submission is largely based on an analysis of the 71st NSSO data. It seeks to view the data from a gender lens, but in the lack of a conceptual framework or at least a clear description of the hypotheses/questions that they sought to understand about gender and health. In the lack of this, the article largely is a summary of sex-differentials with respect to access, utilisation and healthcare impoverishment, with a discussion of these differences anchored within gender. 

**Remarks to the authors**
A gender-based analysis of nationwide data is an important endeavour and indeed contributes to our understanding of the inequity patterns with respect to gender. The paper attempts to present a gendered analysis of the 71st NSSO data and contribute to the knowledge-based towards improving UHC in India. My compliments to the authors in taking up this task. I have listed below possible revisions for the authors to consider, in order to improve the paper. 

**Major revisions**
1. **Sex differentials versus gender differentials**: The authors aim to present a gender lens of access to health and financial protection is not fully met by the extensive reporting of six differentials in access, utilisation, morbidity and other data presented from the 71st National Sample survey. I believe the article would be improved by the addition of a theoretical/conceptual framework on gender and health, or else clear articulation of the specific hypothesis that the authors seek to test or listing of questions that have driven their analysis. In the lack of this the gender component of the authors analysis appears only in discussion, and that too draws largely upon literature rather than the patterns seen in their analysis.
2. **What do we already know of the gender component of 71st NSSO:** There are a few articles including reports based on the 71st National Sample survey. It would be good for the discussion section to allude to these reports, as well as build upon what has already been reported in these publications. This will help clarify the *novelty and originality* of the submission. For example, beyond reporting of disaggregated gender analysis of various indicators, did the authors analysis reveal anything new either within/across state differences in gender, or else on how we understand gender related health inequities as a whole. Indeed, this should be an important part of the conclusions.
3. **Time trends missing:** The article also appears to be silent about any changes in gender related health in equities over time. I understand that this may not have been within the scope of the paper, but it may be useful to discuss broadly on whether these appear to be changing for the better or not.
4. **Inferences related to “why” and assertions/assumptions on** ***why*** **a particular pattern is seen in women with respect to health/healthcare:** In a few places particularly in the second paragraph of the introduction as well as in a few places in the discussion, the authors tend to make sweeping statements/assumptions related to the reasons driving particular decisions by women. For example in the second paragraph of the introduction section the authors say “women often do not acknowledge their own health problems“, and elsewhere racy “they also neglect their health because of the competing demands…“. I would suggest that the authors exercise caution in framing the possible reasons behind health care decisions especially when there attributed to all women. Especially when they use agency-loaded words such as neglect or acknowledge.
5. **Distinguishing NSSO methods from the methods in the paper clearly:** In the first two paragraphs of the method section, the authors have described the methodology adopted by the National Sample survey. Later on they have described the methodology adopted in their analysis. I would suggest to carefully re-write the method section to distinguish between the methodology adopted by the NSSO in obtaining the data (which is secondary data for the authors) and the methodology adopted in analysing this secondary data. Currently the method section is written as if the authors themselves were involved in the survey
6. In the fourth paragraph of the methods section the authors briefly describe what their analysis of the secondary data shall focus on. The authors say “Our analysis attempts to raise question as to who has access to what resources, who makes what decision when it comes to accessing or utilising healthcare services, which is also in line with how to do gender analysis”. Given that the authors only data is from the National Sample survey, which does not collect data on intra household decision-making or power relations, it is unclear as to how the authors could answer these questions.
7. The authors make a few inferential statements in the results that seek to explain the results. Some of these need to be carefully re-looked. For example in the first paragraph of the background information in the results section the authors attribute higher proportion of outpatient ambulatory care among women when compared to men, to the higher burden of morbidity in female population, without supporting this assertion with any explanation. Many such result patterns that they find could have multiple contributory causes which need to be acknowledged. Often these causes could very within and across households, as well as within and across several socio-economic population categories. Without a sophisticated statistical analysis, or else an in-depth qualitative inquiry, it may be difficult to choose one possible explanation over another while describing results of a cross-sectional survey. 
8. Many states have introduced insurance schemes in the last decade especially covering hospitalisation. These schemes appear to vary in the conditions they cover, the amount covered, as well as the coverage of the population groups. Given this diversity it is difficult to maintain some of the assertions made as if the RSBY (that the authors mention) is the only insurance scheme at play. In fact, in some states RSBY coverage is rather poor. I believe the authors should at least categorise states based on availability and/or coverage of such insurance schemes and correlate these with the results they see in terms of OOPE in their survey data. IN the lack of such attempts, the assertions related to OOPE and intra/inter state patterns related to financial protection need to be worded cautiously, accounting for these differences within states. 
9. The discussion section needs to be broken down into specific subsections to help the reader understand the various results that are being discussed. Also a general comment to limit the discussion section to discussing the results obtained in their paper. See for example assertions related to the poor quality of public health care systems in a country (a broad sweeping statement) or other statements related to the disadvantages of health-insurance model of financial protection, both of which were not investigated in the study, nor included as possible hypotheses examined. 
10. The authors need to carefully look into some of the assertions related to gender-based discrimination made in the discussion section. For example, in the second paragraph of the discussion section the authors say “it can be presumed that gender-based discrimination has decreased in the country and the survey is able to capture the full extent of the women’s illness and hospitalisation rate“, whereas later on in the fourth paragraph the authors say “gender-based discrimination is common and quite often family members ignore the health problems of the females…“. In addition to both these statements being unsupported by any data presented either by the authors or from elsewhere, there is also the incoherence with respect to the possible explanations for some of the result patterns that the authors observed. This incoherence needs to be corrected across the whole paper. See earlier remark about the need for hypotheses/questions or a conceptual framework
11. The authors framing of “lack of decision-making power among women“ needs careful re-examination to ensure that the meaning they intend on the lack of such a “power“, as if it were a biological construct.
12. Many assertions in the discussion section attributing some of the results to higher awareness about health in some states/populations needs bolstering with more evidence that what is presented in the results. 
13. The authors discuss limitations of the NSSO study itself, whereas they do not mention any limitation of their own analysis. This is an important oversight to correct. In fact, all through the paper, it is important to distinguish the survey data from teh analysis that the authors have done. A lot may be said with respect to gender and self-reporting, more so in its intersections with caste, socio-economic groups, regions etc. (which has not been said and is hence an opportunity!). 

**Minor essential revisions**
1. On page 2 in the introduction section, the authors say “UHC is designed…”. Conceptualised maybe a better word than design, as UHC appears to be a differently/diversely understood technical concepts rather than a specific design
2. On page 2, the authors begin the second paragraph within an assertion about the stratifications of Indian society. I suggest to reframe the sentence so that it does not appear that the stratifications that the authors list are the only ones upon which Indian society is stratified. There could be others.
3. In the second paragraph on page 2 as in a few other places the authors have used the singular *axis*, as opposed to the the plural *axes*. Further down in the same line the plural form *regards* is used in place of its singular (“in this regard”).
4. Please be consistent with In-text citations. For example in the second paragraph on page to the authors write “Witter et. al. 2017, but earlier in the first paragraph when they refer to the same publication they have listed all the authors.
5. At several places all through the manuscript, a fresh sentence is began immediately after a period without giving a space. This will need to be corrected
6. On page 3, second paragraph, the authors say “in the case of women belonging to poor in groups, costs and affordability are the two important factors…”. It is not clear how the authors distinguish between costs and affordability
7. On p.11, OOPE is synoymised with “capacity to pay”. These are not the same. The former is what people have paid either through debts or other means irrespective of whether they had the “capacity” to pay. 
8. On p. 11, the authors say “it is also observed that significantly lower amount of money is spent on women’s health…”. It is unclear where this observation is coming from. Is it referring to one of their results or to literature (that is not cited?). Or a general observation?
