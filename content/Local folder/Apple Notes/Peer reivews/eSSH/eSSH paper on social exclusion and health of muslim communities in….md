Thank you for the opportunity to review this paper. This is indeed a very relevant submission as far as the scope of the health inequalities supplement is concerned. Data and studies on health of religious minorities is indeed scarce as the authors claim.  Before a review of the paper, I note below a few general remarks directed more at the editor of the supplement. 

**Originality of submission**
As the authors note, the submission draws quite heavily from a report by CEHAT to the Government of Maharashtra on the health of muslims in the state that is publicly accessible here (https://mdd.maharashtra.gov.in/Site/Upload/Pdf/21-CEHAT-Health_of_Muslims_in_Maharashtra.pdf). The current submission is - to my reading - very identical to that and does not build upon that analysis, save for dropping some of the sections related to occupational hazards, mental health and recommendations. Most of the data presented including tables and graphs are identical. The qualitative findings too are almost similar to the ones presented in the report. While on one hand, it is important to have the authors findings in peer-reviewed format, on the other, the submission loses out on originality. 

**Major compulsory revisions**
1. **IS being a minority automatically leading to exclusion?** In multiple places (including the end of the first paragraph in the abstract), there appears to be an assumption that being a minority automatically translates into “social exclusion”. The paper does not carry enough arguments/data that allows for such a picture to be drawn about the muslims in Maharashtra. Indeed, as the authors point out, several aggregate health indicators are comparatively good (or at least there is a mixed picture). There needs to be a sharpening of the language in this respect. Is this coming from their background understanding of Muslims in Maharashtra, or is this something that they seek to demonstrate through their analysis. This appears unclear. 
2. **Objectives re-look?** The objective of the paper was to “identify the social determinants that *jeopardize* the health of the community” I wonder if the authors ought to make more explicit the lens through which they would like to analyse the secondary data they choose and perhaps that could help in framing the social exclusion better than to merely mention it in the background and then bring it up in the conclusion. The current framing of the objective leaves much to be desired. For eg. which were the social determinants that they screened for, which were not found to be relevant, and which were? Also, to meet the current objectives, authors will need to clarify their position on which of the results presented were pertaining to the “social determinants” (and which were not). 
3. **Details of variables analysed, hypotheses tested, as well as possibility of further disaggregation of a “monolithically constructed” muslim community** Authors use secondary NFHS, DLHS and NSSO data but what is not explained clearly is what was done with the data. Which variables were used, and what were the different hypotheses they explored. While the analysis and results are quite clear, what is not clear is which variables were extracted, how they were analysed and which of the data is being presented. This helps also build better explanations for the patterns seen. For e.g., did the data allow for any disaggregation within the Muslim community? Currently, urban/rural and male/female is the only disaggregation presented, whereas we know that the muslim community in the state, as the authors possibly agree, is quite a heterogeneous group. A greater disaggregation of the data may help move beyond the kind of stereotypes about the community that - the authors note in several places - persist both among health workers and in society. 
4. **Urban location of large Muslim population as an explanation:** The authors attribute urban location of a large proportion of Maharashtra’s muslims to the relatively better off aggregate health indicators. There is no specific analysis that they have done that supports this. At the same time, they point out the ghettoisation and the dismal sanitary conditions where most urban-dwelling Muslims live and they attribute the urban location sometimes to adverse health conditions too. Given the secondary data dependence, I am not sure if there is enough data available to better unpack these patterns, but in the lack of this, the framing of urban location as an advantage in one way and a disadvantage in another will need to be better represented. In fact, this could be a way of presentation if they choose, and will allow a sharper presentation of findings (the latter is only a suggestion). 
5. **Prejudice among health care providers as a Result of this study?** This is the only section that dwells into discriminatory processes and is based largely on a study by Khanday and Tanwar. Since there is no re-analysis being done on the data from Khanday and Tanwar, I am not sure if it is useful to include their findings under “Results”. It will need to be moved to “Discussion”. This is my understanding based on the reading of the paper, but I might be wrong. Further, the box showing experiences of women when seeking healthcare is again entirely sourced from the above study and may not be presented as results of the current submission, in my understanding. 
6. **Better connecting conclusions to results**: Social exclusion is one of the important themes explored in the paper. Throughout the paper, the authors make strong assertions about social exclusion of muslims in Maharashtra. In fact, they conclude “That a large percentage of Muslims in Maharashtra live in a strong context of deprivation, alienation and insecurity has been clearly established by this study.” While I agree that it is very likely that the patterns of data presented by the authors, especially the qualitative data on health worker prejudice, reveals processes through which discrimination may be at play in the provision of health services, I do not see clear arguments in the paper (either from quant/qual data) to support a strong assertion of social exclusion having been demonstrated by them. I would suggest the authors to limit the assertions in the conclusion to what has been shown by their own analysis. 

Minor essential revisions
1. Maharashtra has been stated to be a “well developed state” in the Introduction. Needs relook for more precise terminology. 
2. The second sentence under the section titled “Social exclusion and health” brings up “deprivation” after the previous sentence has just defined social exclusion. The next sentence gives back to the SEKN’s definition of social exclusion. Please re-look at the flow. Deprivation seems out of place here and ought to be better framed and incorporated (if at all). 
3. The “Indian context” is mentioned many times in the text. Do the authors just mean India? What additional explanation do they seek to bring in by using “Indian context”? Could be clarified. 
4. In the Findings section, sub-section titled socioeconomic context of muslims in maharashtra, the use of high and low is ambiguous with respect to unemployment. Please re-look. Some places where authors say “high” they are referring to employment, and elsewhere to unemployment. 
5. Figure 1 does not show time-trends. Revise the type of graph. 
6. Assertion of systematic neglect of muslim ghettoes by the state is made at the end of the section titled “Availability of public and private health care” in findings (p.7). However, as clarified earlier, the “Data” for this is from another paper (and not from the analysis of the authors), and the data presented does not offer a “comparison” with other nearby regions/communities in order to conclude that there is both a systematic phenomenon, and a neglect (together a systematic neglect). Would suggest to limit assertions in the findings section to the analysis of their own data. 
7. Apparent poor survival of urban muslim children is not straight-forward. Muslim urban girls U5MR (32 per 1000 LB) seems comparable to Hindu urban girls U5MR (31 per 1000 LB) albeit Muslim urban boys (73) is higher than Hindu urban boys (42), whereas the average U5MR of muslim urban children (54) is higher than the average U5MR of Hindu urban children (37). This needs a better explanation given that authors have elsewhere claimed urban location as an advantage in terms of health services access. How/why does this play out as an advantage in some instances (cf. immunisation) and a disadvantage in others (U5MR)?
8. In the discussion section, the authors invoke true deficit between state and the muslim community as a possible explanation for poor health seeking behaviour and poor utilisation. However, the data presented is rather mixed and does not universally show poor utilisation and poor health outcomes, albeit it does show unhealthy living conditions, neglect and health worker prejudice. Are there possible coping mechanisms or other phenomena at play that may need to be reflected upon? 
9. Conclusions of the authors in their assertions on “…a strong context of deprivation, alienation and insecurity” as being “clearly established in the present paper” may need to be more precisely worded. Similarly, claims on finding “inadequacies of the State and the health system” may also need to be better qualified based on the results presented.
