Thank you for inviting me to review this submission. The article seeks to understand the factors underlying the increased role of the private for-profit sector in healthcare delivery in Bangladesh. It does so using document analysis; details of how this analysis was done in terms of any analytical framework used or a systematic method of choosing documents to analyse are not mentioned. The final section titled “conclusion and policy implications”, while highlighting important issues regarding governance of health goes beyond the scope of the authors’ findings from the document analysis. Overall there is also limited grounding/framing of the analysis/conclusions within wider debates and/or theories around governance/stewardship in health. 

Based on the following main points, I feel that the article requires significant restructuring and/or even re-analysis to be able to adequately fulfil the aim the authors set out for themselves. 

I have organised my review observations in two sections: major and minor comments. 

Major comments
1. Conceptual gaps in framing and defining some of the results: There are several concepts that need to be developed carefully in order for them to come up prominently in the Results/Conclusion. For eg. the concept of stewardship has not been developed carefully enough for the Bangladesh setting. From the evidence presented, it is also possible to also assert that there has been an alignment in global and national agenda-setting for widespread private for-profit sector engagement in health along with political will at national level as well as systematic stewardship by the State in removing barriers for privatisation. So, how can the authors’ conclusions regarding lack of stewardship be supported? While I agree in principle with their conclusion, the arguments presented and the analytical process applied needs to be able to better support such a conclusion. Similar is the case of “blemish governance”, another result of the authors. 
2. Methods description gaps: Authors assert that their document analysis is “…based on both primary and secondary data”. Please clarify: the analysis is possibly performed ON documents and not “based on” document analysis. Further, the distinction between primary and secondary data is not very clear. For instance, government reports are included under primary data. This distinction is quite blurred in the kind of documents chosen in this paper. And finally, important details on the summary of the search results, criteria applied to identify relevant literature (after searching), and list of documents/summary of their numbers and type are not available to the reader. 
3. Unclear analysis procedures/tools/frameworks: Authors provide no details on how they analysed the data. They mention document analysis, but how were these documents analysed and how did they arrive at the results. What kind of frameworks/tools were used to arrive at the broad themes presented later as results. For example, the distinction between results of a document analysis and narrative review of literature blur in the lack of this and the article then begins to read as a critical commentary on the topic. Indeed, if the article is framed as a commentary on the topic drawing from various contemporary literature, the article could be more favourably considered in my opinion. 
4. Uncritical acceptance of literature in results: Authors need to distinguish where they are summarising results directly from literature and where they are able to make an abstraction based on their own position with respect to the research question. For instance, the abandonment of socialism (see p.4, line 34) seems to be an important finding that they report, but appears to be uncritically stated from a few papers they cite. How was this examined? There are similar issues with many other results: (a) “incredible progress in primary care…” (p.6, line 21), (b) “…delivery of public healthcare has usually been outputted (sic!) quite poorly…” (p.6, line 49), (c) “…Bangladeshi healthcare industry has many flaws such as conflict of interest and the lack of professional and business ethics…” (p.7, line 49), (d) “Despite a legacy of political dissonance…” (p.14, line 26); also dissonance is not defined/clarfied here. This brings up a situation where it becomes difficult to understand what aspect the document analysis added in addition to a narrative review of findings from cited literature. 
5. Qualifying adjectives in the conclusion: Several qualifiers in the conclusion are not carefully and critically assessed and seem not be based on evidence presented in the earlier results sections. See for eg. the “vigorous” role of international organisations, “dismal state of the public sector, “dearth of stewardship”. Conclusion talks about “budget constraints”, “communication gap between doctors and patients”, “deficient political will”, people “losing their confidence in the health system of Bangladesh” (which has not been sufficiently demonstrated in the results). Similarly, the authors talk of adopting a “comprehensive approach” (p.22, line 29), “appropriate response” (p.22, line 42) while not clearly defining what would characterise such an approach. 


Minor comments
1. There are minor grammatical errors in the abstract and in a few places in the main text as well. Use of has instead of have (in the purpose section of the abstract). 
2. P.2, line 6: Consider scarce/inadequite health workforce rather than “very limited” health workforce
3. The title should include Bangladesh
4. Reconsider use of word “endemic” at multiple instances in the paper. 
5. The article needs a clear navigation at the beginning of the results section that helps the reader understand how the results have been presented and organised. 
6. Companies Act (see p.15, line 32) was probably amended, not formulated in 1994. Pls check.
