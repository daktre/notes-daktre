**C1: Excellence**
The proposal carries an excellent and concise context analysis of treatment gaps in LMIC settings for mental health problems. The narrative demonstrates a good understanding of the settings in the countries chosen. The articulation of the complexity of the intervention is also appropriate. The proposal is rooted within LMIC local health systems and the objectives are very comprehensive in involving and intervening across macro-, meso- and micro-levels of LMIC country health systems and identifies the entry points appropriately. The intervention inputs are clealry identified. Table 2 is an excellent summary of how the project relates to the scope of the GACD call and identifies specific project activities in relation to elements mentioned in the call for proposals. Table 5 maps out the nature and extent of stakeholder involvement very well. Key alignments with national and international consensus on mental health as well as other cross-cutting issues is very well identified. Choice of methods is good. The project ambitions are very clearly specified and the manner in which these ambitions are incorporated/realised in the proposal are clearly explained in Table 5. Contextualisation to country context is well understood and incorporated in the proposal (WP2). 

The project seeks to approach chronic health problems using innovative and new developments (syndemics framework). They take up the complex task of integrating care for chronic conditions

**C2: Impact**
Projectimpacts are well identified but ambitiously framed (cf. Expected Impact 3 in Table 7), without sufficient explanation on how they will be measured in the corresponding WPs (WP 4 &5), although the proposed activities certainly contribute to these expected impacts. This appears to me to be more about inadequate explanation and detailing out of impact rather than an overstated impact. Similarly impacts stated at the level of quality of life and improved economic status (num 6 in Table 8) are again a similar pattern as the observation above. Dissemination plans appear well detailed out with good integration of social media channels in addition to more classical and academic ones. Commercial exploitation is mentioned without adequate details on why/how the proposed intervention is commercially viable and little identification of channels to do so. 
WP4 does not have enough details in terms of number of implementation sites or country contexts and nature of activities at these sites to make a good determination of the feasibility, but this is probably going to be developed in WP3 and in preparation during project implementation. Nonetheless, a rough idea on this would have allowed an assessment of the population that the intervention may benefit. 

**C3: Quality**
- LMIC country partners have strong partnerships at national and sub-national levels
- Strong publication record and involvement of young investigators and mentoring is a positive. 
- Data management plan and communication plans are well detailed out with elaborate and well planned communication strategies. 
- Very good detailing out of the project implementation in WP1 and WP6, including well described management structure and milestones and good details on the inter-linkages between WPs. 
- Very thorough identification of possible risks and appropriate mitigation strategies outlined (p.60)
- Very strong commitments of support by relevant heads from participating organisations showing their commitment. 

**Overall**
- Very thorough gender integration into implementation. 
- Researchers/project team have limited control over removing structural and economic barriers to MH care-seeking in the LMIC settings. Team also does not sufficiently include implementers themselves (or decision-makers/policymakers) that could have addressed this problem in their favour. While they do include activities related to protocoloising/manualizing their insights, the fact that implementers/policymakers will be recipients rather than more active participants is a possile hurdle to realising the full potential of their involvement. This hurdle is not sufficiently identified or addressed.
