**Excellence 3.0**
- The proposal addresses objectives relevant to the call and is detailed in identifying appropriate interventions for involving families in the care for mental illness. 
- Proposal was weak in demonstrating innovation. The implementation support programme described in the proposal in good detail, though comprehensive, was not considered particularly innovative. 
- Evidence-base provided for TFI was not sufficiently specific to the TFI intervention proposed and was rather on psycho-social interventions. The intervention proposed was rather the roll-out of clinical and policy level guidelines for TFI in the participating centres. 
- There are some shortcomings regarding the description of study design and methods. Sample size calculation assumes a homogenous study design, whereas the LMIC site has a pre-post study design as opposed to the CRCT in the HIC site. Duration of data collection in LMIC site is considered inadequate to establish pre-treatment trends and insufficient rationale for choice of classic pre-post design as opposed to other feasible study designs. Inclusion and exclusion criteria and diagnostic tools that will be used in patient recruitment are not clearly identified. 
- The participatory action research component described does not sufficiently demonstrate how a high degree of ownership and empowerment will be achieved, a defining principle of PAR. The description of the implementation of PAR does not demonstrate a critical action-reflection cycle process across stakeholders. 
- The proposal has a limited geographical scope in the LMIC, limited to small region. The targeting of vulnerable population in the HIC is considered insufficient. For instance, there is insufficient description of why the HIC patients targeted are more vulnerable than other mentally ill in that setting, or whether specific groups within mentally ill population in the HIC are being targeted. 

**Impact 3.0**
- The proposal is rigorous in formulating implementation outcomes at the level of patients, families and services. 
- The proposal is systematic in identifying various stakeholders, communication platforms and dissemination pathways. 
- The costing strategy is insufficient in detail; this could affect the cost-effectiveness analysis. 
- The empirical work in HIC is limited whereas the impact is considered across three HIC settings. 
- The proposal identifies outcomes at the level of creation of new communication platforms, website, network and stakeholder organisations and does not sufficiently indicate harnessing of or alignment with possible existing platforms. 

**Implementation 3.0**
- The description of recruitment, especially of families into the intervention is insufficient in its detail. 
- The proposal does not adequately account for risks in recruitment or drop-out of families from the intervention. 
- The proposal under-estimates the complexity of ensuring adherence to TFI guidelines across diverse groups across patients, families, providers and administrators, in diverse settings in HIC and LMIC. 
- The work packages describing qualitative data collection do not provide sufficient details. 
- The proposal could have been more efficient in its use of resources. For instance it over-estimates time and resources for preparatory work and literature review. 
- The risk assessment does not comprehensively address all possible risks beyond flagging ethical issues.
