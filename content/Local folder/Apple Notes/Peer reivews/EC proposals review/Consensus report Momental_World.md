**Excellence 3.5**
- The proposal is comprehensive and innovative in its approach, covers a heterogenous set of countries and responds to key priority areas of the call. For instance, the focus on patient’s contextual and socio-economic factors that affect care outcomes and the effort at reaching vulnerable and hard-to-reach patients is commendable. 
- The objectives are clearly stated and appear quite ambitious; key process, output and outcomes and corresponding indicators/measures for the same are clearly identified. Methods are described in sufficient detail and are state-of-the-art. 
- LMIC involvement limited to one hospital setting. 
- The feasibility of achieving a tool relevant across several geographic and cultural settings with the methods proposed is low. The proposed inputs into the model and the sample size for validation of the model is small. It was considered insufficient to achieve the generalisability and transferability laid out by the proposal’s early sections related to objectives and goals. Dropout rates estimated are optimistic and may further hinder the model’s ability to be useful across geographic and cultural settings as claimed by the proposal. 


**Impact 4.0**
- Expected impacts are in line with the activities and project outputs. Effort at quantification of impact is on specific aspects of care is noted. However, many of the impact statements were considered not possible to be achieved by the proposal. For instance, impacts claimed at the level of patient and informal caregiver empowerment in the respective contexts and on the transferability to other cultural, social and economic settings. 
- Potential effects on reducing socio-economic inequalities in care pathways are well identified. 
- Identifying specific target groups for dissemination is noted. 
- Impact and dissemination activities emphasise and identify more with European settings than in participating LMIC settings. 

**Implementation 3.5**
- Carefully planned management structure and relevant experience in participating organisations. 
- Well rounded skillset across consortium partners across various disciplinary backgrounds needed for this project 
- Risk assessment and mitigation strategies identified. Proposal identifies several “medium” risk events. Some of these were determined to be high enough to pose substantial risk to achieving key project objectives, for instance the possibility (medium risk in the proposal’s assessment) of the LMIC partner not receiving funds. The corresponding mitigation strategies/contingency measures (co-funding or public-private engagement) may not sufficiently address the risk. The need for partners dependence on co-funding was also determined to be a potential risk to implementation. 
- The implementation of many of the data collection involving both care providers and patients and across diverse socio-cultural and linguistic settings requiring significant resources and time was determined to be very challenging. For instance, the dependence on secondary literature and data extraction from these sources.
