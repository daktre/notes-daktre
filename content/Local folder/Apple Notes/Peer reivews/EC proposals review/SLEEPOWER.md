**C1: Excellence**
The proposal lists seven objectives that are well framed and specific. The “implementation” or action component does not come across readily on reading the objectives though. The “science” component of the research proposed is thorough and comes across well but the implementation/action component is not clarified in the objectives, though the methods and later sections clearly outline this component. Section 1.2 (Relation to the work programme) in fact is helpful in clarifying the overarching approach of the authors in terms of implementation. 

The concept proposed is sound and is based on extensive scientific work already done by many of the member institutions. Proposed methods are clearly described (biomarkers related bio-medical methods and population health/epidemiology related cohort study methods), although the specifics recruitment of study subjects by country/sites are not available. More such details would have helped appreciate the preparedness and the feasibility of the project. 

The proposal is an innovative study question and brings together disciplines that ought to work together to improve occupational health among nightshift workers. From the description, the technology/product of interest is not immediately apparent, but the promise of an occupational health model targeting a disadvantaged group of workers responds to a social/organisational need within many countries. 

Interdisciplinary and relevant stakeholder involvement has been taken care of. 

**C2: Impact**

Section 2.2 (Measures to maximise impact section) outlines several measures including peer-reviewed publications, conference level dissemination etc, which are not unexpected from a large scientific project as the one proposed. The table lists more specific impacts, for which pathways beyond dissemination to specific channels are not identified, but rather each of them mention “different stakeholders” or “relevant stakeholders”. There is no doubt as to the quality of information that will emerge from the results but more specific pathways to the impact would be needed to determine the likelihood and feasibility of such impact beyond the evolution of guidelines for night shift workers, based on the evidence generated from the project. 

While the mention of the website for the dissemination is a good idea, unclear as to sustainability of the website, nor is there information on how active it would be and whom it would engage, or if it will be a relatively passive distribution point for outputs of the project. 
Overall, the details presented on the expected impact and dissemination do not allow for a more specific determination of the likelihood of impact, whereas the “science” of the study is well described and likelihood of scientific outputs appears rather clearer than the impact processes. 

**C3: Quality**

Nature and characteristics of the team are in line with the inter-disciplinary work proposed. How the work packages coordinate with one another is not very clear (a PERT is provided but very little description is available on this figure). The foreseeing of potential delays due to ethics clearances and the different country contexts in member states is appreciated. Management structures are well described. Consortium expertise in the proposed work is good. Task allocation is as per strengths. 

Overall project objectives are not clearly linked to specific WP objectives. Description of activities within each WP is rather brief and is not clear and specific in the form of objectives. This hampers a good determination of how the WP level objectives align with the overall objectives.
