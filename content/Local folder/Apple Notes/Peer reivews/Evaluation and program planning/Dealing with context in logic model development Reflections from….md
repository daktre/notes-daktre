Thank you for the opportunity to review this paper. The paper seeks to systematically document reflections on the way in which logic model development within a realist evaluation was able to incorporate the context within which the program was being implemented. The paper is quite practice-oriented for evaluation practitioners and program planners. It aims to engage critically with the literature on the use of logic models within program evaluation, especially realist evaluation. This is the first time I am reading this paper. However, two reviewer reports have been provided and it appears that the current version of paper allocated to me for review is a version based on incorporating both reviewer suggestions. 

Insofar as the suggestions of both the reviewers are concerned, the authors appear to have incorporated the reviewer inputs by extensive revision and reorganisation of the manuscript, albeit I do not have access to the previous version. The current review applies only to this version of the manuscript. 

**Abstract**
Consider reframing “Among the lessons learned…” to “One of the most important findings is…”. 

**Backgrond**
Very well written and builds a strong case for what comes up next. However, the assertion that “few published RE studies share and reflect on their programme logic modelling processes” does not appear to be convincing (to me) without at least a citation to a narrative review or a commentary elsewhere that has systematically (or even anecdotally!) scanned the vast RE literature across disciplines. Within health for example, I am aware of at least three papers which provide thick descriptions of how programme theories and/or LMs were used (although the former is not entirely equalisable with the latter, LM and PT development and corresponding literature are pertinent to the authors’ assertion). 

**Synopsis of debate regarding incorporating context into logic models**  The section sets up the justification for what the authors do rather well rooting their work within the larger scientific debates. The distinction between program context and evaluation context appears (to me) rather artificial and I am not sure how it contributes towards their paper (I am not talking about the content in both these sections, which are all very good and well summarised, but about setting up this distinction itself). To my understanding the factors listed under programme context acquire significance only to the evaluators (or a critically reflecting implementer’s) eye and hence is also a part of hte evaluation context. Please treat this as a comment/observation only. 

**Lines 280-283**: Please review sentence grammar

**Identifying contextual factors**
While the process for developing LM is well described, what is useful in such a paper (that seeks to speak to practitioners as much as to researchers) is the actual way in which the contextual factors were identified. For eg. lines 357-359 mention that participants at the LM training meetings identified “potential contextual factors”. What was the process for this? Did they use a framework to organise the different factors? Was this an open brainstorm and all factors mentioned were listed? Going by the participatory nature of such meetings, a lot of discussion on context must have happened. How was this data processed? Given the layered nature of context, how “far” from the intervention did the researchers go? Were any distant/far-away factors or those at very high levels excluded?

**Implementer involvement**
From the steps described in developing the LM, it is not very clear who was involved beyond the research team (implementers of the intervention itself, CHWs etc.) in contributing towards LM. How were implementer perspectives brought in? Authors mention “consulting and engaging with local stakeholders…” But the details of such consultation are not clear (were they consulted over email or teleconferencing?) Later in line 534, authors mention the exchange between researchers and program stakeholders. Given that this is an important element of improving context-sensitiveness of the LM, it would be good to describe implementer involvement in greater detail. This also appears prominently in the Discussion. 

**Line 599:** Suggestion to review choice of word “verify”. Aren’t the researchers going to refine/validate these candidate theories?

The IWT examples are very useful in understanding how the LM development process helped improve the context sensitiveness of the researchers’ hypotheses. Researchers can consider making all the IWTs available as supplemental material in the interest of furthering LM and IWT development practice (assuming these are not part of future publications). 

**Strengthening the linkages between results reported and the discussion**
The discussion on points 7 and 8 particularly need to be better linked to the results. One way of doing this could be to improve the level of detail of reporting of results related to the discussion points. For eg., in the Discussion on the process of developing LM itself can facilitate closer links with stakeholders**:** This point and the related point in this discussion item about embedding the research within the policy and practice of the Ministry, while very pertinent, do not have enough data presented for readers to be able to appreciate this as a discussion point. For eg the paper did not really delve into the effectiveness of the teleconferencing for LM development, or how well the embedding worked (or didn’t). The reason for stating this is because the use of ICT and embedding have been spoken about before too in literature, but what makes this assertion valuable is if data or information on how specifically this was done that made them work is presented. Same point to be made on the discussion point number 8 on shared language. What is it about this intervention and what the researchers did that helped improve the shared language. Were there specific instances/stages in the program which benefitted from this? How did such a shared language manifest in terms of the IWTs chosen or contextual factors identified? Are there such examples?
