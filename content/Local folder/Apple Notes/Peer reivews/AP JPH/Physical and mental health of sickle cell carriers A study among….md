This is an important area of inquiry given that sickle cell disease is one of the neglected tropical diseases, specifically, aspects of lived experience, physical and mental health of SCD or SCT are under researched areas of inquiry. The focus on SCT among Scheduled Castes and Scheduled tribe communities from a region of the country with poor human development indicators is also a notable feature of this study. However there are critical gaps in the paper that diminish its scientific merit in its current form. 

The major issue is that the assertions in the abstract (in the sections titled *what we already know* and *what this article adds*), and in the Conclusion section need to be framed much more modestly, keeping in mind that this is a cross sectional study, which is reporting on statistical associations only and is not asserting causal links. Although the authors do not attribute causality they will need to carefully edit language to prevent assertions in the abstract that may not be supported by the study design and analysis that they have conducted. 

There are also minor grammatical errors which will need to be fixed in the section title what we already know. 

On p.2 in the Introduction, the assertion about SCT neither being a benign carrier state nor an actual disease entity has been frequently made in the abstract and later on in the Discussion. None of these are supported by good quality citations. Such assertions will require global reviews/evidence syntheses and not just papers that also just state these assertions. 

Authors claim that there is a vast literature on “physical and mental health of SCD patients”. This assertion too has not been supported by citations either to systematic reviews or any evidence synthesis on SCD. On the contrary, at least two recent papers published in BMJ Global Health report on the paucity of such evidence. 

In the introduction the authors cite a paper from *Journal of community genetics* to support assertions related to “socioeconomic disadvantage and medical deprivation” (it is another matter that authors may please examine more widely prevalent public health concepts such as limited healthcare access than call this medical deprivation). The citation to the journal of community genetics does not establish this point at all: it appears to be a knowledge awareness and attitude survey and does not seem to be the best citation on the socioeconomic and health care access in this district. And the cited paper does not provide any insight about the number of studies conducted in the state/district because the assertion being made here by the authors is that there is not a single study that reports on physical and mental health of people with sickle cell trait in this district. Once again, this kind of an assertion typically needs a systematic review or an evidence synthesis or at least a scoping review. Instead the cited paper is results of a survey that was done with the aim of establishing the prevalence and distribution of sickle cell trait in Koraput district of Orissa. Furthermore, seven sentences introducing the district are reproduced verbatim from this paper. Even if cited, verbatim reproduction will need to be put in closed quotations. 

There are also further problems with the way the paper conceptualizes physical health and lists several aspects of physical health in brackets. This ranges from systemic disorders like hypertension to biochemical parameters like glucose level and undefined or poorly defined characteristics like anemia (do they mean Hb status?), “obesity”  (do they mean body mass index?) and a clinical parameter like pulse rate. This range of aspects under physical health needs better conceptualization and some clarification on the underlying hypotheses being tested through this study. 

Under mental health, the author's study depression. Mental health is much wider. Authors will need to more modestly categorise what they study as depression rather than asserting it as a study of mental health. Indeed, over the last decade, mental health has also been conceptualized and defined in relation to wellness. See, for e.g., the positive mental health framework as opposed to not being depressed. 

Given the possibly regional or global readership of this article, the authors need to consider better defining Scheduled Castes and Scheduled tribe which are currently not properly defined in the manuscript. 

A major problem is the lack of details on how sample sizes were calculated and sampling strategy . 

There are no details of the location of the ethics committee vis-a-vis the respondents. Hopefully the IEC was located within Orissa in order to be able to provide reasonable oversight on the study. 

Authors describe in the results the statistical association between various physical and mental health characteristics of with and without sickle cell trait and the exposure variable being the carrier of sickle cell trait. Without robust multivariable analysis, it is impossible to account for various confounders which could have played a part in this association given that the modalities of sampling are unavailable or unreported, and given that sample size and articulation of hypothesis does not appear in the manuscript. It is difficult to assess the relevance of these associations beyond the fact that this specific study reported these associations. For instance, the occurrence of consanguinity itself could also be linked to socio-demographic and familial characteristics/social capital in unknown ways. And hence, it may be important to account for how consanguinity and consanguineous marriage which appears to be more among those with sickle cell trait influences through unknown pathways the outcomes beyond the fact that it drives higher probability of SCT through genetic pathways. Another example is the finding that blood transfusion incidences but sub optimal among those with SCT. These need to be better clarified. What was the reason that the authors expected there to be a higher level of blood transfusion? Were these for other comorbidities or for managing the SCT itself which will need further explanation? Many other interesting associations such as with lower systolic blood pressure, will need a more robust multivariable analysis and accounting for confounding variables and a more robust sampling design. In the lack of these, it may be difficult to posit these as possible implications of the current study. 

The discussion in its current format is long and goes far beyond the results discussed in the current study. There will be a need to carefully stick to the results of the current study only and to discuss those in the context of what is already known. 

The conclusions as with the abstract overstate the findings. For example, in terms of establishing the advantage of protection against overweight and obesity, which is unclear for me.
