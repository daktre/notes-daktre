Thank you for the opportunity to review this paper. The paper seeks to extend/broaden current practice of human centred design (HCD) from a design-oriented tool to being a tool for sustained participation and engagement of communities in complex health system intervention settings. 

Firstly, a general comment on the authors’ framing of the “how it works” as a pragmatic concern (see p.1 in the abstract for instance, but elsewhere as well). In the program evaluation literature as well as in the literature on theory-driven inequiry/realist evaluation literature, the “how” question (relating to interventions/programs/policies) is situated at the level of social mechanisms. Far from being a pragmatic concern, these are of immense intellectual interest. Indeed, in various social sciences, mechanisms are situated at the level of human agency and its interface with programs/policies. Given this, authors need to strongly clarify their conceptual position in relation to framing HCD’s “how it works” concern as being of pragmatic significance. 

Authors use a phrase “pieces of the health system” in the background. I believe this is not fully explained and may not be properly understood unless the thinking behind this is clearly laid out. 

The case for integrating theory into HCD is well laid out in the background. 

The idea of “traditional cultural context” also needs better definition. Is traditional being used in a relational way (to something that is less/not traditional)? The way top-down social structuring and shame-based norms are mapped onto a “traditional cultural context” needs better clarification for a global audience. For instance, the current framing implies that something which is not “traditional” may not have shame-based norms and will not be top-down. Even if agreeable, this needs sharper framing. The paper referred to Kumar et al.,2015 is supporting assertions about nature of innovations in such settings rather than helping readers understand the nature of what is termed “traditional cultural setting” for a wider audience. 

On p.7, authors apply the intrinsic/extrinsic motivations from organisational psychology to health systems settings. While giving examples from health systems settings for these motivations, they mention aspirations of end users as a possible example of extrinsic motivation. This entire paragraph needs some conceptual clarity. If the authors refer to potential examples of extrinsic motivations of individual health system actors, in my understanding, aspirations of beneficiaries/communities do not count within the framework of extrinsic motivation. Arguably if community is considered as being within the health system this does apply, but the conceptualisation of a health system needs to be stated here. Further, the lines could also be interpreted as being the collective motivation of the system itself (as an emergent construct), which I am guessing the authors do not imply here. For these two reasons, I would suggest to improve this paragraph conceptually. To clarify, I do not find any problems/errors here but only identify a need for conceptual clarity. 

Please quality “partners” (p.7). 


On p.10 SALT workshop is mentioned. SALT has been mentioned once earlier on p.9 as well. Unclear if it is an acronym, in which case, please expand. Currently the expansion appears on p.13. 

Discussion appears to begin twice: on p.20 & on p.21. 

While group 4’s drastic turn-around appears to be sufficiently explained by the trust framework, authors have not demonstrated in my assessment - as they claim on p.20 - sufficiently that it was indeed the “theory of trust building” that was crucial in securing the outcomes claimed (sustainability, mediation and frame measurement). While their observations do indicate this, there is insufficient empirical evidence presented in this paper (perhaps because it is not the primary purpose of this paper, but yet, this is important in making causal assertions in the results). For instance, some of the change could also be accounted for by leadership frameworks operating specifically within Group 4, or within specific contextual factors that operated within group 4. How could such alternative possibilities be ruled out within the material presented in this paper? That there was indeed sustainability, mediation and measurement is not contested but rather their attribution. 

While the integration of HCD approach to theory is indeed novel and has potential as the authors claim in their discussion, there needs to be a revision of the presentation of results or else a critical reflection in the discussion as to the limitations of the integration in this current case and how/what could improve this.
