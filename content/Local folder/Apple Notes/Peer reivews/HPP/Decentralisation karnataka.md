Thanks for the opportunity to review this paper. The paper builds upon existing research from Karantaka on the experiences with decentralisation in this context and identifies tensions between normative administrative approaches with political network-based approaches at village level. It is an important contribution towards better understanding decentralisation in relation to its effects on health/health services/systems. 

Major

1. Policy intent with respect to decentralisation in health systems
The central idea that decentralisation had an implicit/explicit intention of “…supporting people-centred, responsive health systems” is not very convincingly presented. Was there such an implicit/explict policy intent advancing specific areas of engagement within health systems (responsiveness/people-centredness?). The political goal (as stated by the authors) appears to be much more on autonomy rather than on specific health system attributes such as people-centredness/responsiveness in my reading. Strengthening documentation/referencing or presenting evidence for this would strengthen this assumption of the authors, that is central in their abstract and in their study. 

1. Framing decentralisation as a solution to healthcare access and social determinants
P.1, lines 44-50: Authors imply that decentralisation was advanced as a solution to address healthcare access inequalities and/or inadequate action on social determinants. However, decentralisation within health has been a sub-set of a wider push towards decentralisation with its origins outside of health. Acknowledging this wider statutory push (via Acts of legilslature and wider agenda-setting outside of health) may be important for a global audience. The current framing in the background is anchored within various global (Alma Ata, WB) discourses and does not appear to acknowledge the constitutional/statutory basis that actuals provided the rules/frameworks/structures for making it happen. This may be important policy context for understanding its effects within health. For instance, the documents referenced for World Bank’s push for decentralisation is for 1994, whereas some degree/form of decentralisation has existed administratively/legall via acts of legislature from late 50s reinforced in the 80s. 

1. Few studies on decentralisation and its effects on health outcomes
This assertion is made by authors on p.4, line 14-16. It would be useful to back this up with citations of review articles, if not systematic reviews. Do they mean there is scarce literature on decentralisation effects on health outcomes from LMICs/India? Only as an example, I mention this paper (Sumah, Baatiema & Abimbola 2016 in Health Policy) which is an example of one such review. There are others out there, so good to qualify/clarify this assertion. 

1. “Consensus” or lack thereof?
I wonder if consensus can be claimed on decentralisation being “largely unsuccessful.” Many papers caution about the role of local context. This is an observation only (and not an absolute comment on my part) to carefully relook at the way current literature is framed vis-a-vis success/lack of with respect to decentralisation

1. Decentralisation in terms of local government vs decentralisation in health services/systems
While the initial introduction is very clear in laying out the scope of this paper as being decentralisation/devolution of government authority to local bodies (so decentralisation as an organisational arrangement of government itself), in the section titled “Health system decentralisation…” on p.3, there might be a confusion about the scope. There is a lot of literature (especially from the HIV/AIDS domain) on decentralisation of healthcare/services/systems which mainly addresses the need to move more and more services to lower levels (akin to decentralisation within governments). There is some good quality evidence on the effectiveness and/or need for this in HIV, mental health, cancer care etc. Authors might want to clarify that this is out of scope of this paper or edit this section. 

1. Decentralisation through administrative fiat
Here, achieving decentralisation via administrative fiat is possibly used as a “straw man” argument? Clarify. Else, reconsider this. It assumes that decentralisation approaches rely on administrative fiat as a way of achieving this. 

1. Tools and Analysis
There are some issues that need to be addressed with respect to tools and analysis. 
a) Details on how the analysis was done of the various data collected (tools for the semi-structured and in-depth interviews, guides for the FGDs) to arrive at the results are not provided. Consider including these. For instacnce, how was convergence assessed? Were pre-defined codes used to assess some of the roles (for eg. “voice to the community”). 
b) In the lack of the tool details, it is tough to assess some of the results. For instance, when the ASHA did not mention VHSNC (given that she is the secretary), were there follow-up questions asked about this or only her knowledge was assessed? 
c) Similarly is the issue of framing results at the level of the whole group (for eg. PRI “…clearly see Health Department officials at a higher level as being much more critical to the availability, accessibility and quality of health services. They do not see CHWs as having much of a decision-making role on these issues.” How was this assessed at the PRI level? Via a thematic analysis or by looking for frequency of occurrence of particular codes or by structured choices/responses in a questionnaire). 
d) Some of the results presented under the action reserach/intervention indicate participant observation or data from field notes? This does not seem to have been included under the Methods? Please clarify. 
e) More details of number of FGDs, how they were constructed, nature of participants etc are needed. Same with how the respondents of the semi-structured interviews too. 

1. Design
It appears that the first phase of qualitative data was collected in Bangalore rural district. Here, the authors describe the disconnect/tension between how decentralisation is conceptualised by CHWs vis-a-vis PRI. But, the action research is implemented in another district (which may or may not have the same disconnect described from the previous district). In order for the analysis to apply to the whole research, it is important for the researchers to clarify a bit the relevant contextual conditions in these two districts. How comparable or not are they with respect to their objects of interest. Some clarificaiton/comments on this is needed. Also more information about these two districts would be helpful to better understand the settings where the two phases were implemented. 


1. Intervention details
Details of the intervention are scarce. For instance, what was included under “handholding of health functionaries”. How as this handholding achieved and what was the degree of participation in these? And who is included under “health functionaries” and how good was the participation? 

1. Themes as results or discussion; need to harmonise results and discussion sections
The discussion begins with results of a thematic analysis of “…themes emerging from “discussion” with the health….”. These discussions are from the semi-structured interview or from FGD? The three main analytical points in the discussion are analytical well abstracted out from the data and are indeed very insightful. However, further clarity on how this abstraction/analysis was done (see earlier points on further details of tools, analysis) would greatly improve the paper in my assessment. For instance, the viewing of the qualitative data from a power (or gender) lens is interesting and tends to explain the tension between political and programmatic elements in decentralisation. It would be good to understand where, at what stage and how this came to be examined (as this appears only late into discussion: earlier discussions on power all focus on devolution of “authority” to lower levels in the administrative machinery rather than unequal power dynamics between actors at the same level, as is being reported in the results and/or discussion). 

1. Improvements in response to capacity building
It would be good to provide more details to support “substantial improvements in VHSNC” in response to capacity building (this comes up in discussion; could include before/after comparisons of how this was assessed under results). Similarly, how was ability of VHSNC members to “…influence the larger context within which they operated” assessed? This comes up in discussion but is not sufficiently addressed in results.  



Minor/discretionary/suggestions/comments
1. Title needs to indicate that the paper looks at political and programmatic pressures in Karnataka state of India. Given the diversity even in the way decentralisation has/has not happened across Indian states, specifying this may be important for readers. 
2. The conclusions section is rather broad in making recommendations beyond the scope of the study results. 


**Revised paper review dated 4 June 2019**

Thanks for the opportunity to review the revision of this paper. I have noted the substantial revisions made by the authors in response to both reviews. There appear to be some unresolved issues. 

Conclusion as stated in the abstract
“Sustained capacity building of the VHSNC can contribute to strengthening decentralization, but only if ownership, resourcing and accountability are strengthened alongside.” It is unclear how these 3 are identified as essential conditions (“…only if…”). The results and discussion in the full paper do not convey this and rather end with the lack of quick-fixes. The abstract’s conclusion needs to summarise the results. 

In the background, authors trace decentralisation in the health sector as reflected in the various national health policy (1983 and then 2002), but do not reflect its position on the current national health policy (2017). Please clarify reasons (if any) for not considering the most recent national health policy that is now in place. 

Language used in the summary of the background needs to be sharpened/clarified. Some of the assertions dont appear to have been covered. For instance, “…arguments in favor of revitalizing local bodies and their role in governance have been a powerful part of political rhetoric”, “…political salience of decentralisation…”. 

The problematisation and framing of the basis of this paper has been clearly stated in the revised version under the section Research Focus. 

Although much clearer, there is still some need for clarity on how the results have been reported, both in terms of how they are framed as well as what kind of analysis led to the identification of the stated results. For instance, on the former (framing), there is the question of how the authors understand/conceptualise “architecture of VHSNCs”. This is important because they list “several structural weaknesses in the architecture of VHSNCs…” as part of results. But it is unclear how this was conceptualised. Perhaps the problem is in the introduction of the term architecture itself? Were they examining reporting relationships? formal and/or informal networks? What kind of framework was used to study the architecture (was document analysis of the VHSNC structuring done?). And secondly, when they report results  (i-iv), it is unclear if these are at the level of perceptions (of the participants?) or if these emerged as findings from an analysis (by the researchers). See for instance the finding they report that “…they (VHSNC members) have not been skilled to manage them (shortcomings) and move forward”). How was “skills” identified and not something else that may be pertinent (for example other structural factors in mitigating these shortcomings, such as resources, or formal/informal powers to them to act on these etc.). 

Some of the assertions in the results need further details. For instance, “…noticed some positive change in the operation of the VHSNCs”. How was this determined? Was this based on observations?
