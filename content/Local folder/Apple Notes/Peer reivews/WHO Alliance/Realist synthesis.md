Thank you for the opportunity to review this chapter on realist reviews. 

**Programmes and policies**
The introduction gives a good idea of the possible reasons to undertake realist review within health policy and systems research. Although not intended the introduction seeks to position the realist review only for health policies, where as realist reviews maybe more useful arguably for programmes/interventions. This could certainly be emphasised. In the LMIC context, the utility of realist reviews for synthesising evidence on how programs work in particular settings maybe quite important albeit there are very few realist reviews of LMIC-based interventions. 

**Realist evaluation and realist synthesis**
Perhaps there is a need to have a line or two about the relationship between a realist evaluation and realist review. In the same line then the section could also clarify on the nearly synonymous realist synthesis, given that these are all prevalent in literature. 

**Realist review scenarios**
The paragraph on what is the realist review is very well written and clarifies how really sleep you could be different from a systematic review. I wonder if it is possible to further provide scenarios or even examples where a realist view maybe more meaningful than a systematic one. This will really help those who are on the fence about whether to take up a realist review or not as well as clarify, as well as set right expectations from such a review. For eg. what kind of questions about policies/programmes/interventions do realist reviews answer as opposed to systematic reviews. 


**Realist philosophy of science**
The section on underpinning assumptions of realist reviews introduces the CMO configuration that is the hallmark of doing realist work. Although several versions of realism are alluded to in the first paragraph and the P&T version guiding the realist review is pointed out, I feel it may be useful to delve a bit into the “realist philosophy of science” and explain it. 

Further down in this section on underpinning assumptions, three points are made about realist analysis that are roughly as follows:
- Interventions dont cause outcomes, but people do
- CMO configurations and abstracted descriptions of why programmes are expected to work (programme theories) allow a realist review to explain how and why a context influences an outcome
- Outcomes of realist analyses are themselves testable empirically as they are framed as middle-range theories

In this section that ends just before the subsequent titled “undertaking realist reviews” two sections stand out:
- The one explaining what is the context in a realist enterprise, and
- the last paragraph on transferability

My suggestion is to consider pushing the section on context into the “undertaking realist reviews” section as it has a lot of operational application.
Regarding the section on transferability, I feel it may require a bit more expansion and wonder if it makes sense to have it further down the chapter. In this section, the example of optimism bias acting as a mechanism did not appear to be very helpful in making the point on transferability. If the argument is that transferability in the realist enterprise may be achieved through identifying particular context-mechanism relationships that can be reproduced elsewhere, I feel easier examples may be considered. What I usually find helpful is the emphasis in the realist view of individual human agency in particular contexts thorugh resources/shifts/changes introduced by the intervention. 

**Undertaking realist reviews**
- “think laterally” as an expression may need clarification. 
- Common pitfalls section is very nice. “Initial programme theory is….not realist in nature”. Useful to clarify what defining characteristics would a realist framing of an IPT have. 
- confusing intervention strategy with a  mechanism. Useful to clarify this with an example

**LMIC examples**
I understand that realist reviews are still scarce from LMICs, but I feel it is useful to have a mix of review examples from LMIC settings as well. 

Final suggestion: Author may want to relook at a conversational style ending in the conclusion (and in a few other places in the text), in line with how the other chapters.
