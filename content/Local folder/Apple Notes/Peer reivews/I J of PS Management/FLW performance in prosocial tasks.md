Thank you for the opportunity to review this paper. The authors take on an important public policy question with respect to FLWs and their performance with wide implications for organising policies and programs. While the analysis and design are robust, I find several conceptual/philosophical problems with the framing of the overly broad framing of the results. This - for me - is the main and important shortcoming (on which more below) in an otherwise well written paper. I must further indicate that I have not been able to evaluate the statistical analysis which needs to also be vetted but focus more on the aim, intent, framing and the claims/assertions based on the analysis/results. 

The task is overly simple and I find it very difficult to be able to attribute differences or lack thereof to phenomenon under study. In the lack of a theoretical framework/conceptual framework that links the performance of the task (outcome) exclusively to “motivation” (possible mechanism under study) that is related to the “exposure” (incentive scheme that they are currently under), it is difficult to attribute the outcome to the possible mechanism (motivation to do the task) as opposed to other contextual/environmental influences that could affect the observed outcome. An ideal experimental set-up would require that the outcome of interest is modified by agency (arising from motivation) of the FLW, but this is tough to establish. This needs to be substantively discussed/elaborated. 

Another relatively smaller shortcoming is the reason for 6-7 year delay in publishing this study. A good reason for this needs to be provided. 

There is no mention of ethical oversight and consent procedures for this study which too needs to be described/discussed. 

There is also no mention of the positionalilty of the reserchers/research team/observers vis-a-vis the FLWs and how this affects/did not affect the observations. 


Minor/discretionary comments
- On p.4 line 18, Anganwadi CENTERS (emphasis mine) are identified as FLWs of nutrition programs in India. They are in fact the centers where nutrition FLWs work.
