**Note to editor**

I was not able to find a guiding document that tracked how the reviewer comments have been addressed (I see the track-changes version of the paper but not a point-by-point response to the comments which would have made the review easier). In the lack of that, it was quite tedious to track and review the revised version. Kindly advice authors to provide this navigation. Currently, it is not possible to make out which of the comments of the reviewers were addressed, and which were not. See for eg. my first comment under the Results section on the “Geography” sub-section. I have noted that some of my comments have not been addressed, which I have included in my review report share with authors. However, these are relatively minor given the extensive revisions and need not come in the way of publishing the paper. That said, I would leave it to the Editor to take a call on whether s/he still deems them worthy of being addressed in the final published version. 


**revisions**

**Abstract**
**The abstract has been thoroughly revised and reads well.** 

**Introduction**
**The comment on nuanced explanations with citations for role of NSPs is well integrated.** 

**Methods**
**- Clarification on degree of impact evaluated is clear now.** 
**- With respect to my earlier comments on generalisability reproduced here: “Are the authors certain that their study results will be “generalisable”? When they justify the reasoning for using the Liu et. al. framework, they allude to “other studies” which had problems with validity and generalisability. If such an assertion is being made, it would be useful to provide some references to these studies and perhaps explain a bit more on how their methods have improved the generalisability and validity of the results. If I understand generalisability as meaning “external validity” to settings other than the one being studied, I am not sure if the author’s use of Liu et. al. framework itself could improve this(?) For eg. the Liu et. al framework as represented in Fig. 1 does not per se help understand how the findings could be generalisable (perhaps authors mean comprehensive in taking into account contextual factors at various levels, internal and external envt., behavioural factors etc?)”**

**I believe my comments have not yet been addressed. The revisions have clearly improved the reader’s understanding of how the Liu et. al framework is applied, but the text does not yet sufficiently explain how their application of the Liu et al framework (a) improves generalisability, as well as (b) how “other studies” (which have not been cited and hence not possible to assess this assertion) have been unable to address generalisability.** 

**-Sufficient details have now been provided for the qualitative data collection, ethics, triangulation and researcher positionality.** 

**Results**
**- I am unsure of how the following comment made earlier in the review was/was not addressed. "I think one of challenges is to capture the dynamic interaction between the elements of the framework. For eg. the findings under “Geography” are rather scarce (save for the fairly obvious inference that hard-to-reach areas are not preferred by NSPs). However, when seen in coordination/alignment with the institutional factors/goverance, if contracting mechanisms had features to incentivise/account for additional costs imposed by geography, would there have been a difference in NSP behaviour? Did this happen anywhere and hence was there a difference in NSP behaviour? If this didn’t happen, why did it not? Given the richness of qualitative data, if there are data that allows for understanding such interactions between the Liu et. al framework elements that show case-to-case (or across district) differences in how NSP behaviour has played out, it would add to the insights from this paper."** 

**- Perspectives and positionality issues have been clearly addressed.** 

**Discussion**
**All comments have been well addressed.**
