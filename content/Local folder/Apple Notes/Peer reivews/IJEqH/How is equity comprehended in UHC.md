Thank you for the opportunity to review this paper. The paper describes and critically analyses how the concept of equity has been used in global UHC policy documents of WHO and World Bank and in national government and national policy documents in two francophone countries. While the purpose of the paper is concise and well-defined, a deepening of the findings through other primary data may be needed to answer the question posed by the authors in their title (though this appears out of scope of what the authors set out to do in this paper). 

Manuscript had 2 line numbering schemes. I have followed the inner-numbers ranging from 1-25 or so rather than the outer number. This problem along with lack of page numbers makes this referencing of sentences in the review difficult and time-consuming. 

Equity as “fuzzy”
The authors characterise equity as a “fuzzy” concept in the abstract and later on in the methods and discussion as well. There is a need to explain why the authors take this position. In my assessment, the multiple meanings given to equity in different communities is being alluded to(?). In any case, better to explain this clearly then call it fuzzy. If authors choose to retain this, I would suggest to strengthen or develop this argument in greater detail in the paper. For instance, it first appears in the first line of Methods where it appears that the authors assume that the build-up presented in the background has resulted in an acceptance of the concept as “fuzzy”. I believe the authors imply by the use fuzzy that the concept of equity is vague or poorly understood. Rather their background indicates that the equity is understood “differently” (diversely?) rather than poorly? Please clarify. 

Title and scope of paper
Despite a global scope for the two documents reviewed, the in-country document assessment is limited  to two countries in East Africa. Consider limiting scope in title to these settings. Title ought to reflect that the paper responds to comprehension in “global and national UHC policy documents”. The current framing tends to give a wider scope than is responded to in the paper. Also the documents chosen are sectorally limited at both global (WHO and WB) and national (national health sector plans and government policy documents on UHC). Would widening of this scope affect their findings vis-a-vis comprehension? Perhaps not, but perhaps yes (depends on country setting). Consider including a limitation section that discusses this - among other methodological limitations (see below)


Optional/minor comments 

A careful thought may be needed to whether the authors are assessing “comprehension” or perhaps how “appears to be practiced” based oil a reading of formal policy documents. It’s a mild distinction. 

Last paragraph of Methods: grammatical error in the use of word “Similarly to the approach used by…”

Clarify the logic used for purposive sampling of UHC reports (line 4 under second page of methods)

Clarify: “….appraise the extent to which the documents were preoccupied…” How is this determined methodologically. May need refining. 

End methods with a line indicating how this led to the presentation of results for readers to understand what follows


IN the lack of primary data to gain a deeper understanding of the in-country perspectives or triangulation with historical paths that these countries have taken (which could explain some of the findings), there needs to be a limitation section explaining this. 

Discussion
“….other currents of literature” Clarify. 

Consider the body of literature around movements for “equal opportunity” which borrow from arguments of fairness/justice which appear to be related to concepts explored by authors under equity. Although their relevance to health equity is not direct, this needs to be discussed. 

The second and third arguments presented in discussion could be further grounded in their study findings. 



Review of revision

Thanks for the opportunity to review the revised paper. Authors appear to have comprehensively responded to the four reviews, three of which were extensive. Overall, the revision has clarified and improved the paper in my assessment. 

One overall comment that I believe the authors have not sufficiently clarified is their own view on how they believe equity *ought* to have been comprehended in the documents. While this is not essential to provide, their (critical) review appears to have an underlying “model” (view) on equity which does not appear explicitly. I suggest that the authors either frame/state their own view as to what they were looking for, or else re-assess their tone when they characterise current document contents as “rhetoric”, “merely political” etc. This is not to say that they ought not to say these things. Rather, they need to improve the argumentation to be able to make these assertions. More so, when they put some of these assertions in results (rather than in Discussion). Assertions in the results ought to be very close to the empirical work presented in the paper whereas wider abstractions either to literature or to theory ought to appear in Discussion (in my understanding). I have expanded a bit on this in the specific comments below. 

Once again (was already pointed out in the review of the original submission) there are two line numbering schemes in the paper I received that makes it confusing. I have used the numbering scheme of the authors (typically ranging from 1-25 on each page). 

Title and abstract
The revisions to the title and the abstract are in the right direction. Minor change suggestions in the abstract are as follows:
- Under methods, they say, they “manually searched” each document.  But what authors have done is more than merely searching. They read carefully and assess/critically analyse/describe/summarise. Consider minor revision to reflect this. 
- “…speak about “health inequities””. Reconsider use of “speak about”

Results
Table 1: I appreciate the revised Table 1. However, I am not sure it accomplishes what the authors claim. Authors state (p.7, lines 5-9) that the table is a “quantitative overview of the importance given to equity and the dimensions comprehended”. However, the table gives number of occurrences only and does not really give an overview of the importance. Table 1 appears to be rather a summary of use of terms that the authors scanned from the reports only; no more. Given that these are number of occurrences, it is also difficult to call it really a quantitative overview because one cannot really consider higher number as meaning anything different from a lower or an equal number for instance. 

Characterisation of equity treatment as “vague, rhetoric way”: On p.11, lines 3-9, authors share overall “interpretations” of how equity was understood in the documents. Among other things they consider the treatment as “vague, rhetoric way”. I feel there is insufficient analysis presented to be able to reach this conclusion. They may either consider explaining what they were looking for (how did the authors conceptualise equity and what was the framework used by them to assess/critique these country documents) or else at least explain what they consider “not vague”. In the lack of a comparator/standard, their assertions may - at best - be moved to discussion and not presented as “results”. 

Discussion
IN the second major point they make (p.15, line 18), authors raise questions about capacity of countries to translate global guidelines into practice in a context-specific way.  Based on the reading of their results, an equally likely question one could pose is the opposite: Shouldn’t global guidance be better presented in a context-specific way such that countries could benefit from them. Pls clarify. 

Conclusion
Please refer earlier to characterisation of equity as rhetoric. Perhaps clarifying this better earlier may allow retaining this in conclusion. Else reconsider. 
Similarly, use of equity in a “political” way comes up here in conclusion but has not been sufficiently developed in results or discussion. Use of equity in a “political way” per se is not problematic (authors assertion poses it as if it is a problem). As authors note earlier, UHC agenda is political. So, this is not clear and is out of place in conclusion in the current form. 

Authors bring up again that equity has not been “defined properly” (they do this twice: p.17, line 17 & again in line 19). They further state that policy documents do not propose “concrete ways to implement it”. While these are borne out by their data, in the lack of a comparator/standard definition/conceptualisation by the authors, this appears unwieldily in the conclusion. For instance, how do we assess “concrete ways of implementation” of equity if not thorough comparison with a standard? Please clarify. 



Minor observations
- p.7, line 3: “…explained more into the details”. Replace with explained in detail below. 
- p.8, line 19: Sentence starts with “First” and ends with “in the first place”. Reconsider framing
- p.11, line 9: Sentence starts with a hyphen for no reason
- p.11, line 23: “comprises for packages”. Replace with “comprises of”
- p.12, lines 14 & 15: Review grammar and spelling errors
- p.15, line 18: Sentence begins with “Second”. Not sure if I located a “First”
