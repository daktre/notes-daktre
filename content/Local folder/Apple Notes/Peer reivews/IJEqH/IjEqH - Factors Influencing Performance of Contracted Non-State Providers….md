**Note to editor**

Some of the claims about the methodology being “unique” need to be moderated. In my assessment, the authors study is more comprehensive than many others cited in utilising a framework and addressing various elements of CO (using the Liu et al framework) but the study falls short of describing how the elements of this framework have interacted in specific contexts to result 


**Major revisions**

**Abstract**
- Consider explaining the analysis in a widely understandable language in the abstract instead of just saying “…the Liu et al framework was followed”. Abstract is likely to be read by a wider interest group which may or may not be familiar with this framework. 
- Revise the line “This study confirms that effective input and out management results in strong outcomes”
- I wonder if the conclusion of the study could be framed in line with the stated objective. Currently, the first line under conclusion reads as if the study investigated the effectiveness of contracting NSPs “Contracting NSPs was a successful strategy”, whereas the aim appears to be rather looking at the factors influencing performance and perhaps generating explanations for why it worked (or didn’t). 

**Introduction**
- Authors seem to be making a case for the contracting to NSPs having been successful based on an analysis of the Balanced Scorecard reports. Additional file 1 shows the rather mixed picture of some provinces consistently doing well, whereas others going up and down during the (nearly) decade under study. I wonder if the Introduction could better reflect this in a more nuanced way than declare an overall assertion of effectiveness “The contracting out to NSPs mechanism has **generally been regarded** (*by whom? By the donor community, local communities, country system experts…- Reviewer comment*) as a very effective model for rapidly scaling up health services throughout Afghanistan - this is reflected in commensurate increases in health services coverage and impressive improvements in health systems performance indicators”. 



**Methods**
- The first few paragraphs of the Methods seem to position the study as also being an “impact evaluation” that will assess effectiveness. Authors need to carefully revise language in the “aim of the study” (Introduction and in the abstract) to include what they say about the scope of the study in the methods. 
- On generalisability: Are the authors certain that their study results will be “generalisable”? When they justify the reasoning for using the Liu et. al. framework, they allude to “other studies” which had problems with validity and generalisability. If such an assertion is being made, it would be useful to provide some references to these studies and perhaps explain a bit more on how their methods have improved the generalisability and validity of the results. If I understand generalisability as meaning “external validity” to settings other than the one being studied, I am not sure if the author’s use of Liu et. al. framework itself could improve this(?) For eg. the Liu et. al framework as represented in Fig. 1 does not *per se* help understand how the findings could be generalisable (perhaps authors mean comprehensive in taking into account contextual factors at various levels, internal and external envt., behavioural factors etc?)
- The details of qualitative data collection could be expanded in order to improve the reader’s understanding of the qualitative results. For eg. who conducted the interviews? how were the respondents identified? How was the stratification done? What were the characteristics of the 36 respondents? For example, were there patients/beneficiaries or only health workers? (See for eg. **line 15, p.13** where one could infer that patients/beneficiaries of services were interviewed, but it is not clear from the Methods though) How were they distributed across the districts, in terms of gender, in terms of positions in the system etc. Where were the interviews conducted (at the workplace?) Similarly for the focus groups, how were they convened? Who participated? Where were these conducted? 
- A section on ethical issues that the authors consider relevant to this study and how they have dealt with the data in terms of identity of the participants etc In case patients/beneficiaries were interviewed, how were they selected and what kind of ethical issues were foreseen and/or addressed. 
- In the triangulation, the authors state that “…we used multiple **perspectives or theories** to interpret the data”. This requires some clarification. When the authors state that they used multiple perspectives or theories, whose perspectives are they talking about? The respondents’ or the researchers’? And when they say “multiple theories”, are they referring to theories, as in, from wider body of knowledge, or theories, as in, theories built up from their own data. Considering the different ways in which these words are used, describing this in greater detail will help. Further, this section could benefit from understanding exactly how this triangulation was achieved in instances where there were inconsistancies in terms of perspectives. How did the authors decide how to represent this? Or perhaps all perspectives even if conflicting were represented?

**Results**
- I think one of challenges is to capture the dynamic interaction between the elements of the framework. For eg. the findings under “Geography” are rather scarce (save for the fairly obvious inference that hard-to-reach areas are not preferred by NSPs). However, when seen in coordination/alignment with the institutional factors/goverance, if contracting mechanisms had features to incentivise/account for additional costs imposed by geography, would there have been a difference in NSP behaviour? Did this happen anywhere and hence was there a difference in NSP behaviour? If this didn’t happen, why did it not? Given the richness of qualitative data, if there are data that allows for understanding such interactions between the Liu et. al framework elements that show case-to-case (or across district) differences in how NSP behaviour has played out, it would add to the insights from this paper. 
- Some of the assertions in the results, it is unclear as to whose perspective is being represented. For instance, the first assertion made under “Contract formality” section in p.14 “Abiding to all governmental laws and undergoing a rigorous selection process reduced uncertainty and improved trust and reliability” or for eg. “This role further improved the formality of the contracts” - improved trust and reliability for whom? And according to whom? For patients? For health workers? For MoPH? This kind of clarifying perspectives and/or researcher positionality is needed across the manuscript. To some extent this can be addressed by addressing the earlier comment in the Methods section of this review on the characteristics of the respondents. 
- Source of assertions: In some instances, there is a need to clarify if the assertions being made in the results are coming from desk analysis of reports/secondary data/gray literature or coming from perspectives of the respondents. For instance when the authors state 
	- “The process of contracting out of NSPs is well designed and clear.”, 
	- “Thanks to the detailed BPHS and EPHS documents the specification of services to be provided by the NSPs are clear.” 
	- or when they say “The longer an NSP remained in a geographical location the stronger is the trust between the NSP and the community” How do we know this? Is this being asserted based on respondent perspectives or based on secondary data? From a subsequent line, it becomes clearer that this may be a respondent perspective “ However, some other respondents disagreed and stated that the extension had a negative impact on service delivery by the NSP because it could affect the quality of services.” As a suggestion to the authors, if the data allows to relate the appearance of trust vs lack of trust with particular district context or NSP characteristics, once again, the earlier comment I made about interaction between elements of the framework allows for greater insight into what kind of internal environment in the district could trigger differing outcomes in terms of trust or lack thereof. 
- Participants vs respondents: IN the section on Contractee selection, a difference seems to be made between participants and respondents. Please clarify. 
- “The government, on the contrary, **would** prefer a reimbursable mechanism whereby more scrutiny would be focused on the NSPs and closer supervision could be exerted” Is this being offered as a conjecture? Or is this what respondents who were “government” said?
- Line 8, p.19: “The **only issue** is that this method has different connotations with various partners. Everyone has **its** own understanding of the lump-sum mechanism and that has created **some issues**.” Please carefully revise language to avoid ambiguity. 
- Line 20, p.19 “Regardless of the type of the contract, the **ultimate goal** is how this payment **trickle down** to the end of the payment system, payrolls.” Re-look at the use of possibly loaded words (highlighted)
- p.20-21 “For them the fixed salary is not so attractive but the Results Based Financing (RBF) seems to be more appealing. This is where they feel fairly compensated.” I presume that when such statements are being made this reflects perspectives of all of the health workers. Consider using verbatim quotations in instances where there appears to be this kind of single perspective emerging from (nearly?) all of the stakeholder groups. (?)
- Line 13, p. 27. “**None**-for-profit NSPs” Authors mean Non-profit or not-for-profit?

**Discussion**
- “The interaction of all factors in the framework finally results in a better health impact” This is one aspect the authors could improve the illustration of interactions between the factors of the framework. Currently, the results under each of the factors are represented in a rather atomised fashion. If the data does not allow for this, consider reframing this statement. Else consider adding this to the limitations section. 
- In Discussion, authors nuance the earlier stated scope of the study being an impact assessment, which in turn is not stated under the aim in the introduction. Please consider making this coherent across the manuscript
- **“…but the sum of all factors has shown some impact in general for the overall health.”** This sentence requires clarification and unpacking.
