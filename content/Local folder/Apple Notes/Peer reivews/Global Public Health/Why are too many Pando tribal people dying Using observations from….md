Thank you for the opportunity to review this paper. The paper is a case study that examines why there are avoidable (and possibly unfair) deaths among the Pando, an indigenous community in Central India. The article also aims to contribute to the limited literature (from India) on how socio-political & structural factors could be continuing to aggravate poor health and social outcomes among indigenous communities in India. 

The article is powerfully written but I believe there are some important gaps (as well as some minor/optional observations). 

- Many instances of the use of the word “typical”, “real-world” by the authors indicates an assumption on their parts which is not explicitly stated. For example, in teh abstract they say “real world” problems, but arguably, all contributions in public health examine what could be claimed as real world problems (even if the authors might disagree on which is and which is not). While I understand where the authors are coming from, I suggest a careful revision to examine language where they make distinctions between several such constructs (an non-exhaustive list: “typical disease control”, “biomedical approach”, “deeper inquiry”….). Further on page three, a suggestion to avoid casual phasing such as “drafting the usual recommendations of Disease Control and Prevention”. While I understand the emphasis that the authors want to place, the need for an inquiry into the social and political determinants of health, characterizing or diminishing the value of disease control does not achieve this purpose. If feasible, rephrase and provide any public details of these recommendations so that the reader can get an idea of what was done and what was missed? Similarly, characterizing their own inquiry as deeper without providing details is also better avoided. Later too, there are several allusions made to a typical biomedical approach. Some of these are better explained and outlined rather than assuming a shared understanding across an otherwise broad public health readership. 
- The initial phrasing in the abstract appears to indicate as if the designation of the cause as being scrub typhus is being contested. But it later emerges that the authors do not wish to contest this but rather distinguish this as representing only one of the several proximal and none of the possibly distal causes of these deaths. This needs to be better clarified. 
- Another major gap is to understand the overlaps/distinction between what the State asked for (an evaluation?) and the “evaluation” that the authors conducted (authors call this an evaluation with 7 objectives which is unclear whether these was the mandate provided by teh State or set by themselves). I believe the authors acted in an ethical imperative and dived into a dynamically evolving situation and provided public health support in the form of recommendations and action and very likely there may not have been a “method” as is often described in evaluations that are planned a priori at the desk. That said, the authors do appear to have an underlying multi-layered framework upon which they are describing the case study. However, that framework has not been explicitly described. For instance, they describe their results under “medical investigations” (but refer to biomedical response critically many times). Are these the same? Subsequently, they move to social inquiry where too there appears to be a systematic layered approach they take. But these have not been a priori outlined and explained. I believe the manuscript will benefit from a careful outlining of their collective framework that they have used in order to organise their results and insights into biomedical (medical investigations?), social inquiry, structural aspects and action. Authors will need to better establish how the social inquiry & the stuctural aspects and the action are connected in their framework. This will also help the reader better understand the inter-connectedness. For instance, the effect of the prevailing adverse stereotypy that is evident in the section titled “Meidcal investigations” could then possibly move to a subsequent layer that establishes how the structural aspects bear on biomedical decisions (neglect of diagnosis and overall general lack of surveillance of Pando population deaths). While such connects are evident upon their reading, these interconnections do not emerge strongly enough due to the way the manuscript is organised and because of a lack of an explicitly defined analytical framework (albeit authors use this implicitly).  
- Many assertions on page 11 and 12 which are the structural analysis of excess deaths require more details. For e.g., when the authors assert deaths were either unrecognized or deliberately neglected or that they were normalized. These are very important findings and assertions, but do not seem to have emerged from a detailed and methodologically robust qualitative inquiry (at least not clearly described herein). If it has indeed emerged through some kind of systematic interviewing and observational data that needs to be integrated into the manuscript. If not, this should probably go into discussion (there is no denying that these have contributed but if it is empirically established then only it belongs in the Results). 
- Some parts of the manuscript do not distinguish between state (administrative sub-unit of the country) and State (an institutional arrangement). I believe sometimes when they use this word referring to the former whereas sometimes for example, in page 14 in the discussion, they're referring to the latter. 
- There needs to be a more substantive discussion on the methodological limitations, especially to do with observational data, and the fact that this was retrospectively framed as a case study rather than a planned one (which is fine and which is important to learn from but with inherent limitations). 
- The acknowledgement mentions members of the public health department without details of which local government/state/national government.  

Minor/discretionary revisions
- In the abstract which is otherwise very well written it appears this assertion, “These have been limited to the clinics in most parts”, the word “These” is poorly defined and ambiguous. I believe that the authors intend to refer to the training of health workers in clinical aspects of care as opposed to the structural competency that they talk about in the previous sentence. The emphasis on the latter is unclear because of the phrasing. 
- A positionality statement reflecting on the representation of Pando within the authorship or on the indignenous/non-indigenous identities of the authors along with a reflection on this may be useful. 
- The abstract mentions the invitation to what is described as a group of public health and social medicine professionals, which were asked by the public health authorities of the state government to identify root causes of these deaths. If the author's would like to invoke the entire group as representing this paper, I hope that the entire group which was invited has also participated as authors in this paper. Were there additional people included as authors that were not invited, for instance? Better to clarify who was invited, who participated (in the government’s call for helP), who eventually joined the paper (these 3 may be diff with overlaps but not all the same?) 
- Note the use of plural of fevers on page three for any particular reason?
- For the global readership, it is useful to place this setting Chhattisgarh in the context of the wider geography of India. Although the map of Chattisgarh district is provided supplementary figure one will need to be located in India for the reader. 
- Please define “contractor” on p.2 for a global readership 
- Review the use of word “Britisher”.
