This appears to be very topical, innovative, carefully designed research project to generate good evidence on the use of photo voice to build resilience among youth with SUD in a very needy geographical region of India (Assam in Northeast India). As mentioned by the authors, recent assessments of mental health care needs in the country have shown that there is a huge need to build capacity within communities, civil societies and healthcare delivery systems in addressing several mental health problems. Among these SUD is most neglected as the government health services (the mainstay for many poor and vulnerable communities) does not yet provide care and support at primary and secondary levels for most mental health conditions including SUD. In this sense, their proposal is very well justified and makes use of and builds upon their own experience with the use of PV among youth with SUD. I have provided below a few comments for the researchers to consider to improve the effective implementation of their research as well as make it more relevant to the Indian policy and practice setting. 

**Transferability**
Although the team has gone into some detail on how they shall achieve transferability of their findings (one of their objectives), I find this rather weakly developed:
- Given that Indian states vary wides in terms of healthcare organisation, performance of health services, public-private mix of health services, civil society engagement and of course various other downstream factors related to financing of health and general socio-economic development, I am not sure how their specific experience with use of PV with very carefully chosen NGO POs will/will not lend itself to transferability in other settings. Specific outputs in this line are also lacking. 
- What will be the method employed to determine the factors that led to success in their own implementation site. I believe this has not been specifically planned, although I am sure they will be taking this into account. This will be important to be able to talk about transferability. For eg. what has been the lessons in terms of transferability of their experience in Nepal (which is not very far away geographically) to Assam. Do they have experience with in-country assessments of transferability? What has the experience with Nepal been?
In both the above instances, the objective of bringing up this criticism of transferability is rather to point out the ambitious nature of that enterprise than to say it cannot be done (or for that matter, that it has not been planned). I am very much in favour of this being included, but would be in favour of framing the outcomes related to this more narrowly. 

**Participants’ experience of risk, resilience and recovery**
Details of how these concepts (risk, resilience and recovery) will be dealt within among participants is lacking. While the details of the PV process is well described, how will participants reflect upon their resilience? What will be the tools for this? 

**Website**
Obj 2 on enhance voice appears somewhat weak, especially the focus on building website. Objective 2 c seems to be the most pertinent one to be able to enhance voice in the way the team has defined “enhancement”. However, I am not sure to what extent the website will be able to achieve this. I wonder if educational content/e-learning courses/blended training courses may be more useful than a stand-alone website. Also, the following considerations may need to be addressed:
- Sustainability of website beyond project period: how long, and will be managed and curated by whom?
- What kind of user engagement is planned for hte website? Any effort at building learning communities of PV practitioners?
- Language barriers for hte website: English only?

**Influencing policy and health services involvement**
The details provided in the pathways to impact are very good and show a careful planning by the team before taking on the proposed work. But, what is unclear is what specific aspects of the project will they be generating evidence on? Given that the project is being implemented by NGO partner organisations and given limited interface with the formal/government health services (either at primary care or at secondary or tertiary levels), how can we understand the nature of evidence that will be relevant for health services practice by the state. Of course, all evidence for SUD need not always address health services of the government, but given the dismal coverage of mental health care in India’s primary care system, or even at the district/higher levels (where care is limited to specialist care in large cities), what is the study team aiming for? Specifically, what is their “policy ask”? For eg. do they foresee expansion of PV-based programs within formal health services? Or else do they hope that the state shall identify NGO partners in all districts to do this? Or else is their policy ask at the level of designing other interventions that build resilience? These are some of hte questions that need to be reflected upon before embarking on the policy interface beyond the project. Very closely related to this point is the general lack of “government” or state implementers/policymakers at many levels of this research. 

**Academic beneficiaries**
**“**Psychology, Development Studies, Sociology, Social Anthropology, Social Policy, Youth Studies, International Health Policy, Psychiatry, Area Studies, & Participatory Arts. “
Minor comments about the possible list of academic beneficiaries being quite ambitious (which is not always a negative!). Given the inter-disciplinary nature of the team, I do hope the team could think of outputs across as many of these disciplines listed. 

**Authors mention “theoretical advances “**
I could not see specific details of what aspects of the project will be theorised. Perhaps the details are available with the study team. Would be good to *a priori* clarify these. 

**Advisory group**
Diverse advisory group, but limited representation of policymaker/implementer (government health servcies) perspective. Regional mix and gender mix is well represented. 

**Ethics approval costing**
800 GBP costed for ethics applications in India. This appears a bit steep. 

**Interface with the healthcare delivery system**
The main challenge of what is proposed is that the entry points to influence overall healthcare delivery system either in Assam or country-wide are few or are very challenging, given that:
- Mental healthcare provision at primary health care is limited in India; DMHP evaluations show that very few districts even have reasonable capacity to provide mental health care for severe MH disorders
- Cessation clinics are few. SUD is largely addressed by civil society/NGOs or private sector
Given this context, how can the project interface with the system?

**Other comments**
1. Methodogical innovation in participatory film-making is very innovative
2. HOPE foundation appears to be a very new organisation (mid 2017) and does not yet have a website. 
3. The choice of SUD-affected youth is appreciated and the focus on building resilience is also appreciated.
