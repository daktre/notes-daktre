Comments on importance of the proposed research area *

Household air pollution and effects of second-hand smoke is a much needed area of research in many southern/LMIC settings. In addition to the importance of second-hand smoke and its effects within houses, the social dimension of studying how gender and power relationships interact with the knowledge of potential harm from second-hand smoke is contextual and important. There are limited interventions available in LMICs for this and hence the proposed topic is not only of scientific importance but also has policy and practice implications for improving public health. There is relatively limited scope for discovery in a classical biomedical sense, but higher scope for understanding implementation and its effects and high likelihood of positive effects on public health policy and practice. Applicants also make a strong case for equity considerations of the proposed research given that the people affected by indoor exposure to second-hand smoke are more likely to be poor, women and children.  

Comments on the deliverability of the proposed programme of research *

Overall, the proposal appears to build on existing multiple strands of work by the applicants and many of the outputs identified are very likely to be effectively delivered in my assessment. In some instances, I believe the ability of the applicants to predict impact at scale (for eg on shifting social norms on indoor smoking beyond the population that they shall intervene upon…which is relatively small for each country) appear overly ambitious (for eg. high level aim 1 on p.3). The potential pathways to impact on such scales is not available in the proposal. That said, given the complexity of policy processes, these might indeed be achievable given the multi-disciplinary team that is coming together within each country. Previous working relationships between the UK partner one-on-one with LMIC partners has been demonstrated and supervisory/PhD links too exist, both of which are likely to improve the deliverability of the programme of research. More south-south linkages though have not been demonstrated. 

A relatively minor point: Some research questions (4 & 5) appear to be framed as yes/no questions although their methods and tools clearly will go into possible pathways of such change/explanations rather than answering yes or no to these questions. Among their 6 over-arching objectives, 1-3 appear most feasible whereas 5 & 6 appear least feasible given the policy and social complexities likely involved and hence appear overstated (see also anticipated impacts on p.18 for eg impact bullet 2 and 3; or bullet 5 & 6 on p.19 on “change in social norms relating to smoking….”!). Another pertinent point is the applicants’ apparent assumption that south & south-east Asia could be one entity where lessons from such interventions could be easily scaled; this assumption may not apply to all countries and regions within S & SE Asia and could affect some of their potential outcomes. Proposal is likely to strengthen the scarce network of people and body of literature on household air pollution and second-hand smoke in the participating countries. 

Social science expertise in terms of studying structural drivers (power, gender, norms for eg.) and the ability to steer community engagement and co-production/participatory approaches are crucial in achieving success. While the team has demonstrated evidence of experience in this among the UK institutions, there is insufficient clarity on this from among the southern partners. The PhD programmes and the networking around that could potentially address this but this may be a potential challenge that may hamper deliverability. The involvement of non-research stakeholders is good and may facilitate this albeit not the academic outputs. 

Comments on the overall quality of the application

The proposal is of high quality and brings together experienced researchers spanning various disciplines: public health, social marketing, informatics and  NGOs as well as potential government/community actors. There is a good emphasis on social science approaches, intervention development and co-production. Intra-household power relationships and gender are sufficiently taken into consideration especially given that these aspects are likely to be very different even among the participating LMIC/LIC contexts, let alone differences from the HIC contexts in which such evidence exists to some extent. The overlap of the program over existing population cohorts is also noted with interest. There is also a good diversity of LMIC and LDC and UMIC contexts that could enrich the scientific and policy outputs. An important limitation that may apply in some/all settings where the applicants plan their interventions is the likelihood of information leading to any transformative change in household circumstances given that capabilities to make these changes are likely gendered. The applicants appear to recognise this but this could be a serious limitation and the ability of the intervention to trigger neighbourhood and/or household level action (as opposed to individual action; see for eg background and rationale section in pp 2-3) is likely to be important. There appears to be scarce detail on how such complexities will be navigated, but the team appears capable of being able to resolve this in my assessment. Grounding in theoretical framework is adequately done with involvement of Co-I with necessary expertise. The team appears to have a strong track-record of work in the topics proposed with strengthening of existing research and capacity-building relationships. Policy engagement relationships appear more clear in the UK team than in the LMIC teams. Costing and budget appear adequate for the outputs proposed. 

Questions for the applicants
1. Based on the programme of work proposed, could you point out specific pathways through which your proposal might impact social norms and achieve outputs that you identify at scale (outside of your intervention groups) within countries?  How predictable/controllable are these given the complexity associated with them and how will you manage this?
2. What are the ways in which your proposal could strengthen exchange and linkages between/across southern partners?
