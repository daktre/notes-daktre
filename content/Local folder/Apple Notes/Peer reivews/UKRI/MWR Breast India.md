Project summary provides insufficient background and mentions various numbers and “study” which are unclear in terms of their geographical scope and are unsourced. For instance, “The study found….” (which study?), “….incidence of cancer in in Indian population is lower than in Europe” (reporting systems and data availability issues pose limitations in making such assertions currently); “…observe the state after cancer.” (unclear language)
Summary of business model also lacks clarity. 
Very well written public description that captures their aim very well. 

ODA compliance, impacts, benefits and partnerships (unscored): Many of the assertions are not precise enough and claim improvements at a rather high level (quality of care, social upliftment). Also see for instance “…safe to use unlike another device…”. Another instance of lack of clarity/precision is the statement that it will give a fair chance for men to take control of breast cancer in men” (unclear what prevents it currently)

Gender equality and social inclusion (unscored): poor clarity again. Gender inclusion reduced to widening this to “men”. Inclusion of cancer survivors (? unclear who they speak about) as possible brand ambassadors is a very positive inclusive step.

Project motivation
Blanket statements about private practitioners lacking any medical qualifications. This is untrue. While there are many who do practice without proper qualifications there are many others who are qualified as well. Need for project is well established. Poorly defined assertions: “In the majority of cases, reactive interventions are undertaken only when a body has more than <u>one million cancer cells</u>” (how do we know this?)
