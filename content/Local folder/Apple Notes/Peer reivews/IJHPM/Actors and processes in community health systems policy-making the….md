Thank you for the opportunity to review this article. The article aims to critically analyse the policy-making process for Zambia’s community health strategy using a case study approach. The article is well written and the analysis is presented appropriately. In my assessment, the article needs to address some minor shortcomings which could improve its fit for the journal readership. As far as its methodological and technical content is concerned there are no major concerns according to me. Perhaps the only relatively important consideration is the lack of voices from various excluded stakeholder groups which have not found a place in this manuscript. I am assuming that this was either infeasible or outside the scope of the current research. Nonetheless this needs to be critically reflected upon or else addressed as a study limitation at least. More on this later.

1. In the abstract the authors identify the need to pay attention to CHS policy development process. This can be sharpened by stating precisely what aspect of the process needs the attention rather than framing it generically.
2. In the background the authors in their first line itself talk of the growing interest for CHS as being due to its potential to “leverage different community resources“. The authors could expand the range of probable potential that is possible with CHS and not limit to merely leveraging of community resources? I am aware that the rest of the paragraph addresses this but felt that their opening line in the background frames this issue rather in a limited way.
3. The authors end their background positioning why their current paper is of interest to the policy research readership. However in positioning it I believe they identify a generic reason for undertaking the research when they assert that The purpose of this paper is to illustrate the political nature of the policy process. Is this not widely acknowledged and known in the research community working on health policy? Could the authors perhaps make this more precise in fit to their approach and findings? 
4. On page 4 line number 24, suggest to review the characterisation of case studies as being “great“ for exploring questions in health policy.
5. At several places in the manuscript short forms have been used which have not been defined before. For example MOH online 27 on page 4, and numerous acronyms for international organisations. Please check if these need to be introduced.
6. Regarding the purposive selection of 21 participants from various government departments under the MOH, a little more details on how many were chosen, of which how many were invited, of which how many agreed/declined, would be useful. Particularly useful would be reflections on which kind of people were included and which kind not, and any patterns with respect to those who tended to decline if there were any.
7. The data analysis section is well written and allows for a good understanding of how the analysis was done. Online 29 on page 5 with respect to the request from the respondents for some information do not be included due to its “sensitive“ nature, authors could consider reflecting in discussion if there was a pattern with respect to the nature of information that was considered sensitive. Of course this has to happen keeping in mind ethical considerations with respect to safeguarding respondent requests.
8. On page 6 line 9, authors use the word grapple and deal in the same line. Please review usage.
9. On page 6 the mandated process for policy development in Zambia is described. Either in results or in discussion it is useful to state or cite articles on the extent to which policy processes in Zambia adhere to this process generally speaking even outside of the CHS agenda setting. This will help the international readership. 
10. On page 6 line number 29 the word strategy has been misspelt
11. On page 11 line 23 in the discussion section the authors characterise the process of community health strategy development as complex. In my assessment the authors could be sharper/more precise in characterising this process. What aspect of complexity do they identify? Or perhaps what in their assessment makes it complex? I believe that the material presented allows for this to be discussed.
12. Both in the abstract and in the discussion the authors place the word partners in single quotes. The reason for this is unclear. Do they mean that these actors are not really partners? It would be useful to deconstruct the assumptions underlying the single quotation marks.
13. In the discussion section on page 12 lines 124 the authors identify the absence of actors key to implementation especially at the lower levels of the system. The even suggest that the process has been marginalising for these actors. However the research exercise too has not been able to include their voices in terms of the community health strategy process. Authors could consider discussing this or declaring it as a limitation to their study as the inclusion of their perspectives would be important for example did they all also feel excluded? Was there any effort made by them to try and join this process? If yes where are these efforts rebuffed?
