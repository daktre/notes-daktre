Thank you Dr. Deepa. We have created 8 work-packages which are each led by two Co-PIs/institutions. 

The first 4 work-packages encompass the 2 cross-cutting themes, i.e. the realist implementation research action lab and setting up of a birth cohort among the Soliga tribal population, and the 2 vertical themes i.e. setting up of a population haemoglobinopathy register in the district and studying socio-behavioural aspects of substance use among the Soliga people. 

The other 4 work-packages encompass important centre-wide activities related to capacity-building, public & policy engagement, research management and M&E. 

2 Co-PIs will lead each work-package. We have also identified specific capacity-building roles for PhD, postdoctoral and CRTP fellows in line with the research outcomes planned at the centre. 
CTRITH’s activities are planned such that there will be adequate mobility of these staff across institutions and also between the field-settings in Chamarajanagar and the respective head offices of the 3 institutions in Mysuru and Bengaluru. 

Indeed, one Co-PI & a senior team member will be based in the field for the entire project period, which we believe will facilitate field-level mentorship and the embedded nature of the centre.

# Slide 5
We will use state-of-the-art research methods in social sciences called health policy and systems research methods that are increasingly becoming popular. While we have chosen diverse methods spanning both quantitative and qualitative domains, we have ensured that these methods carefully & meaningfully interact, keeping tribal health at the centre. 

For instance in vertical 2 on studying harmful substance use among the Soliga people we will use oral histories, narratives and ethnographic approaches. These will form the basis for conceptual frameworks and case studies in the cross-cutting theme CC1. In CC1 we will use realist approaches and implementation research methods. Scoping review/realist synthesis inputs on one hand and rich contextual information from the vertical will be brought together in participatory action-reflection cycles with local communities for intervention design. 

This was piloted already in my ongoing THETA research in the region where a small-scale intervention using sports activities for youth is providing an arena to investigate its effects on substance use and wellness among tribal youth. 
The second cross-cutting theme on setting up a birth cohort will allow us to further examine individual and household level psychosocial and parenting environments within tribal households and their impact on health and development of children under 3, and help us identify potential anchors to design interventions. 

Methodologically, the participating Co-PIs and the institutions have extensive experience as evidenced through their publications using these methods.
