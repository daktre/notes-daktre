I am pleased to write a letter in support of VGKK for setting up an Innovation and Learning Center for comprehensive primary health care for indigenous/tribal populations in Chamarajanagar district, Karnataka. VGKK has been working on tribal health for several decades and has a strong rapport with the Adivasi community-based organisations as well as with research organisations. We have had a long-term collaborative MoU focusing on tribal health research and action since 2012. Initial collaborations have focused on community health programs and joint research programs on tribal health. For instance, one of the earlier programs from 2014 to 2016 funded by the WHO Alliance for Health Policy & Systems Research, Geneva was to conduct implementation research using participatory methods with tribal community-based organisation. The intervention was instrumental in design of a unique Adivasi-led health navigation intervention to improve acceptance of referral to higher hospitals by Soliga communities. Based on this work, IPH proposed a long-term tribal health research program, “Towards HEalth Equity & Transformative Action on tribal health” with the DBT/Wellcome Trust India Alliance in which VGKK has been an important partner. Since 2017, VGKK has facilitated the setting up of a public health research field station in BR Hills, the first of its kind where a public health research organisation commits to long-term embedded research with senior researchers working full-time from the tribal area.  IPH’s work with VGKK has expanded to involve various research programs on tribal health, mental health and zoonoses/OneHealth research. More recenlty IPH was awarded a grant by DBT/Wellcome Trust India Alliance to set up a Centre for Research in Tribal Health, part of which will focus on identifying and innovating around community-based healthcare and engagement models in tobacco control and sickle cell diseases within tribal communities in the region. 

VGKK has set up administrative and financial procedures in line with several other NGOs in Karnataka. Although they are not a research organisation they have worked with IPH and other research organisations in fulfilling public health and community health research projects and fellowships. Based on my experience, I am sure VGKK will be able to fulfil the requirements of leading this ILC in close partnership with other relevant community health and research organisations.
