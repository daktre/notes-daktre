Sir/Madam,

I hereby submit my application for a PhD scholarship under the XXXX program of The George Institute of Global Health. 

I am a medical doctor with a post-graduate degree in community medicine from Christian Medical College, Vellore. My training at CMC Vellore is well known for its rigor in training doctors to deal with rural and/or limited resource settings that prevail in large parts of the country. After my post-graduation, I was involved in setting up a department of community medicine and facilitating various community health activities in a rural healthcare setting in Nagaland. I have also worked closely with health systems strengthening interventions at the district level in my initial engagement with the Institute of Public Health, Bangalore. 

In 2011, I began my research career with a research project on social exclusion in a nation-wide health financing scheme that was targeting people below poverty line. Since then, I have been drawn to the idea of marshalling research towards addressing the huge inequities in health in India. Over the years, I have gained experience in working both with quantitative datasets, as well as with qualitative data in my collaboration with social science researchers. 

Over the last few years, I have developed a keen interest in applying research knowledge to problems related to health inequities. I have been closely associated with a project funded by IDRC and led by a premier research Institute in Kerala, that looked at the gaps in research with respect to health inequities in India. While periodic nationwide population health surveys have established serious inequities along gender, socio-economic status and caste, very little reserach has been done on developing interventions or delving deeper into the underlying socio-political or health services/systms drivers of these inequities. The result is that most health services in the country rely on a one-size fits all kind of a healthcare delivery pattern that tends to either actively or passively exclude several population groups and individuals. 

Among the population groups that have had the most difficulties in accessing healthcare and/or gaining from recent population health improvements in India are our indigenous people. Many of India’s indigenous population are closely associated with forested regions and my work with one such community called the Soligas in southern India has shown me that there is a need for a special focus and the use of participatory and community-based research methods in order to achieve any meaningful change. Hence, I have begun in earnest to apply implementation reserch and participatory action reserch methods in my work with the Soliga people over the last few years. My work has been closely allied with an ongoing health equity initiative that seeks to build a resource centre on tribal health that could ensure wider sharing of the reserch benefits beyond the Soliga people to other indigenous communities nearby and elsewhere in India. 

For this purpose, I have helped set up a field research station in the forested regions of southern Karnataka (one of the states in southern India) in close association with the community-based organisation of the Soliga people and a reputed NGO that has been recognised for its work with the Soliga community for decades. In partnership with them, I hope to contribute to a long-term tribal health researcher-civil society-community collaboration that will engage scientifically and ethically with this community and hopefully bring the benefits of good research into transformative action on ground. If I get the opportunity for this fellowship, I hope to bring my experience with the community into defining a way of setting up a participatory health observatory on tribal health that will allow the community and researcher to work together and identify research questions that are both scientifically and socially relevant. I hope that my research will embrace and advance the currently scarce body of knowledge on addressing social determinants of tribal health in these parts. 

I would like to apply for this fellowship because I am at a stage in my research career where I would like to systematically invest in taking my research and community health experience to the next level. I would like to conceive and implement a research project on tribal health and go through a rigorous training and supervision that a PhD program provides. My work over the last 4 years of being embedded within a forested area and working closely with a community-based organisation of the Soliga tribal people has given me a unique perspective of the research needs in this population. If I am successful, I hope that I will be able to conceive a research plan that will be locally relevant as well as scientifically sound, so that it could advance the health equity agenda for indigenous people. 

I have provided all the necessary information in the application form and am happy to provide any additional clarifications, if needed.
