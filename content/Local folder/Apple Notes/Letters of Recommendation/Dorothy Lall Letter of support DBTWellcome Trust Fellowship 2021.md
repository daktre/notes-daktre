To 
The selection Committee 
DBT/Wellcome Trust India Alliance Fellowship Program in Clinical/Public Health


Re: Application of Dorothy Lall for CPH Intermediate Fellowship announced by the DBT/Wellcome Trust India Alliance Grant with fellowship project titled *Quality of care for persons living with chronic health conditions: fostering effective, equitable and patient/person centered primary care systems in rural India.* 

Grant Ref no: 19625

Dear Committee members,

I am writing this letter in support of the fellowship application by Dr. Dorothy Lall for an intermediate public health research fellowship. This is based on (a) my 7 year working experience with Dorothy as her colleague at Institute of Public Health Bengaluru (IPH), (b) my experience in being involved as a collaborator in her PhD work focusing on improving non-communicable disease care at primary health care in southern Karnataka, (c) my working experience with her co-leading the setting up of a unique capacity-building program in health policy and systems research program, the India-HPSR fellowship program which Dorothy and I jointly set up in collaboration with key international partners including the WHO Alliance for Health Policy & Systems Research, Geneva, and (d) her strong proposal to advance our understanding of NCD care in rural primary healthcare settings which I have had the opportunity to help her with. I describe these four in detail below. 

Dorothy joined IPH in 2014 aiming for a PhD in public health. She successfully designed an implementation research program that studied how government primary health center teams respond to initiatives to improve quality of care for rural poor with Diabetes and Hypertension. Her work involved closely working with health workers and doctors in government PHCs, but she also closely interacted with various health services actors at district and taluka levels in course of her work. Having a solid grounding already in epidemiology and other quantitative methods through her work at CMC, Vellore and subsequently at the Centre for Chronic Disease Control, she chose to use mixed methods with a particular focus on developing a theory of change to understand how change comes about in rural PHC settings. Her PhD outputs - four first-author international peer-reviewed publications - are among the few publications from India which produce small-scale yet context-specific lessons on how we could improve care for NCDs in primary healthcare settings. Apart from this, as a senior colleague at IPH, she was instrumental in strengthening research conduct at IPH through contributions to design and implementation of research conduct guidelines for all researchers as well as the strengthening of the SOPs for the institutional ethics committee, for which she has been the member-secretary for several years. 

In course of her PhD, given my expertise in mixed methods research and evaluation, she invited me to join as a collaborator to help develop a theory of change. In course of this, I worked closely with Dorothy and have observed her handwork and dedication, rigour in pursuing research and handling of data as well as her perseverance. I believe she is an excellent researcher who is capable of handling research projects independently. Dorothy is also a very compassionate and caring researcher and has nurtured and mentored early-career researchers. I particularly mention one researcher who joined IPH from nursing background whom Dorothy has mentored and helped with enrolment into a PhD program. 

Dorothy joined me in setting up a unique fellowship, the India Health Policy & Systems Research Fellowship program, for which we held a series of national and internationall consultations with experts for curriculum development. Her attention to detail, breadth of knowledge of various qualitative and quantitative research methods and yet a careful and measured application have stood out. She has extensive experience in setting up face-to-face and virtual learning programs which shall be an asset to the mentoring and capacity-building components of the proposed fellowship. 

If this fellowship is funded, Dorothy shall set up a long term body of work which will further our understanding of how to achieve good quality non-communicable disease care at primary healthcare settings for which good quality implementation research has been scarce. If this advances further, I hope to join as a collaborator in helping with the application of appropriate health policy and systems research methods to study the nature of change through the interventions. Given her long-term stint at IPH, she already has access to colleagues of mine at the chronic conditions cluster who too will be able to provide support as and when needed. 

Overall, I believe the work proposed is work that is important in strengthening health systems response to NCDs particularly in rural PHC settings. Further, I believe Dorothy is at the right time in her professional career to be able to respond to this need through the collaborative proposal that she has put up. I strongly recommend her for the fellowship and am willing to provide technical support and collaboration from our cluster at IPH as needed. 

Yours sincerely,


Dr. Prashanth N Srinivas, MBBS, MPH, PhD
Faculty & DBT/Wellcome Trust India Alliance Fellow (2017-22)
Health equity cluster lead
Assistant Director (Research)
