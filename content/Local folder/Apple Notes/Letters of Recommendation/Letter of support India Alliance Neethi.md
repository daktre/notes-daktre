<p style="text-align:justify;margin:0">I am pleased to write a letter in support of Dr. Neethi V Rao’s application to the India Alliance Early Career Fellowship program. 


I have known Neethi when she joined IPH as a researcher in xxxx. At IPH, in a short period of time, Neethi demonstrated a keen interest in health policy issues backed up with a good understanding of health systems in India. During her stint at IPH, she was an important member of the policy research team, where she worked closely with a senior colleague of mine in research and advocacy on policy issues related to governance and regulation in health, particularly on tobacco control, non-communicable diseases and later on private sector regulation. Her keen interest in research has helped her pose intelligent and relevant research questions to improve our understanding of how policies are conceived, implemented and analysed. Despite her research training in biomedical sciences, Neethi’s passion for contributing to policy analysis has steered her towards a career in health policy and systems research.  

As an early career researcher, Neethi was an active participant at research seminars at IPH as well as in other regional institutions whenever there are topics related to health system regulation and governance. She made multiple presentations at national and international forums and conferences. She has demonstrated a strong foundation on basics of research methods, data collection, analysis and writing. She has contributed as co-author to publications from IPH and has also recently led an independent publication on industry interference in health policymaking, a field that is very poorly understood in low- and middle-income countries such as India. She also took initiative in institutional activities including convening a temporary committee formed for organizational development, where she demonstrated exceptional leadership and clarity of vision working with a diverse team in a relatively short timeframe.

<b>Paragraph about her stint at national level</b>

In spite of a large number of medical schools and healthcare organisations, various assessments have shown a very poor culture of critical analysis and research in many LMICs, including India. Given her previous training and her capacity to think and pose questions independently, I think Neethi’s research career in public health and advocacy is very promising. Although, she is not (yet) an independent researcher who has led large research grants on her own, I find in her, a great potential to transform into a successful public health researcher and a leader who can contribute to evidence-based policy and stronger health systems in the region. I believe that the PH_Leader program has the right elements to help her transform into an independent researcher and leader in public health and I strongly support her selection in the program. 
</p>
