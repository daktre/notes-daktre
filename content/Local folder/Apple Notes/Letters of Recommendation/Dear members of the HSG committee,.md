On behalf of Dr. Pragati Hebbar & myself from Institute of Public Health Bengaluru, we are delighted to nominate Ms. Uma Mahadevan for the Health Systems Global Sam Adjei Distinguished Public Service Award for 2024. 

Uma Mahadevan has been in public service for nearly three decades now, being a member of the civil services and serving in various positions of leadership in the Government of Karnataka in southern India . She has been a champion for creating, nurturing and strengthening public spaces in all the areas where she has served. Her work in hugely expanding the number, quality and visibility of public libraries across rural Karnataka is now widely recognised. Over the years, she has championed for the transforming government libraries into vibrant spaces especially for adolescents, elderly and women. This has taken the form of both advocating for these within governments and making financial provisions for these as well as championing them publicly among citizens in order to create a movement in favour of protecting public libraries. Through her leadership in this, Karnataka has achieved a large network of public libraries in rural and remote areas.  

Based on high quality evidence on the importance of establishing creches to cover children in <3 years age group, she was instrumental in initiating *Koosina Mane* (home for the infant). This initiative is unique because it is largely delivered through local governments (Panchayats) and is implemented in a bottom-up and need-based approach. The initiative builds upon the evidence produced for these among Adivasi and rural communities in Central India. 

She has also been a strong advocate for women’s empowerment through livelihoods and greater social and political engagement of women thorugh various initiatives that she spearheaded in her leadership role in Women & Child Development department. In her current role as the adminsitrative head of the Rural Development & Panchayat Raj department, she plays an important role in facilitating civil society engagement with policy and programs, while allowing spaces for criticism & debate about public policies and programs - a unique characteristic that is at the heart of good governance. This was crucial in her role during the COVID-19 pandemic where she was the focal point for ensuring coordination across academia and civil society in the response.   Her body of work over decades has demonstrated a strong commitment for local government and decentralisation as a governance principle. Her continued engagement with academia and civil society is noteworthy.
