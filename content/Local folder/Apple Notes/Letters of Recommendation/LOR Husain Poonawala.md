I am delighted to write a letter of recommendation for Husain Poonawala in support of his application for a Masters in public health program at the Harvard School of Public Health. I know Husain for nearly 10 years now. I got to know Husain through his keen interest in public health when he finished his medical degree in 2008. Even though his professional journey has taken him deeper into clinical medicine and subsequently into microbiology and infectious diseases, Husain has maintained a deep passion for public health. Having obtained a medical degree from one of the good schools in southern India, and later having done his internship clinical rotations at another hospital, Husain is very aware of the health situation in India. Despite having continued his clinical studies in the US, he has carefully followed healthcare reforms and developments in India and has often taken time off to participate in interesting and challenging assignments here. In 2011, he visited a very remote Lisu tribal village deep inside hard-to-reach forests in Northeast India to help with community health activities and build capacity of local health workers there as a part of a larger collaborative effort by conservation scientists in that region. Subsequently, I have seen that he has deepened his interest in public health, carefully choosing inspiring community health and development initiatives in different parts of India and embedding himself whenever possible in these initiatives to learn as well as contribute to them. HIs visits and assignments he took on with Jan Swasthya Sahyog in rural regions of Chhattisgarh, BR Hills in southern India (where I work) and his work with Aarohi in Uttarakhand stand out among many others. 

In the last decade, he has visited me and my colleagues at the Institute of Public Health in Bangalore often. During these visits, we were happy to see his progress from a young and inquisitive medical graduate to a critical thinking and research-minded and caring doctor who wished to widen the scope of science and innovation to the widest possible populations. His journey into public health has matured from an undefined passion to a focused path to make a difference in Tuberculosis management and prevention at the population level. HIs recent seminar at IPH in fact focused on sharing how the advances in infectious diseases microbiology can be harnessed to improve TB case detection and management. I was impressed with his grasp of a wide body of literature on the subject, his keen observations after having spent a year within the TB management eco-system in India and his ability to put these together into a coherent presentation to a public health audience. 

I find Husain to be an intelligent, hard-working and committed doctor. His conceptual grasp over infectious diseases microbiology, epidemiology and his keen interest and experience in clinical and community health settings in India make him a good candidate to benefit from formal public health training. I believe that the public health program at the Harvard School of Public Health is very well suited for him at this point in his career and will allow him to consolidate his ideas and experiences into a coherent scientific and societally relevant enterprise. I strongly recommend him for this course.
