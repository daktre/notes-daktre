Social inequalities and social determinants are often driving inequitable effects of zoonotic diseases burden and morbidity, particularly among communities living in and around forest areas, such as Adivasi communities. These communities are also socio-geographically disadvantaged and potential solutions require a range of participatory approaches that are inter-sectoral and inter-disciplinary. Using examples of ongoing work in zoonotic diseases, that use  participatory and health policy and systems research approaches, I will discuss ways of advancing the science of zoonotic diseases control using social science research methods.
