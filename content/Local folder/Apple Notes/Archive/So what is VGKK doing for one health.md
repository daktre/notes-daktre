VGKK is involved in multiple research and Health Systems initiatives that use the Onehealth framework. VGKK is working on improving surveillance and decision-making with respect to zoonotic diseases. Earlier in a project led by the Indian Council of medical research and the state health and family welfare department, VGKK collaborated with Institute of Public Health, Bangalore and other partners to create a decision making support tool for early warning and prediction of zoonotic disease called Kyasanur forest disease in the Western Ghats region of Karnataka. Kyasanur Forest disease earlier localised to specific locations in Karnataka has been spreading in recent years to neighbouring states. So VGKK was involved in this project for the Health Systems strengthening component wherein an early warning tool was deployed for supporting surveillance and decision-making within the state health and family welfare services. 

Based on this project more recently Dr Tanya Seshadri (from VGKK) is Co-investigator in an international project titled IndoZoorisk+. This is a multi-institutional interdisciplinary collaborative research project funded by the UK research and innovation, the government of United Kingdom‘s research funding agency. Other partners include Institute of Public Health, Bangalore, Indian Council of medical research, atree and various research organisations. This research project is ongoing and involves social scientists and community health/public health researchers working towards understanding disease dynamics and health seeking behaviour for three zoonotic diseases which are leptospirosis, scrub typhus and KFD among Forest dwelling and forest associated communities in Karnataka and Maharashtra.

Apart from these initiatives Dr Tanya Seshadri has been involved in various teaching programmes panel discussions and public engagement events related to the use of inter-sectoral frameworks such as one health.

In November 2021, Dr Tanya Seshadri from VGKK was Co-awarded the annual TN Khoshoo Memorial Award, which recognises achievements in sustainable development and conservation areas.

IPH collaboration with VGKK
The Institute of Public health Bangalore has established a public health research field station in B R Hills with the support and collaboration of VGKK.

Various oneHealth Research  Intiative‘s are being supported here. IPH has been involved with other partners in supporting the policy level engagement and technical assistance to the state and national governments in bringing one health work into practice. 

Researchers from IPH have been involved with providing inputs to the national Mission on biodiversity and human well-being, an initiative of the Ministry of environment and Forest, government of India. 

At the state level too, IPH health equity cluster has been providing inputs in responding and strengthening zoonotic diseases surveillance and management. 

IPH researchers have been involved in publishing both epidemiological and social science research related to onehealth practice based on fieldwork conducted in various parts of Karnataka.
