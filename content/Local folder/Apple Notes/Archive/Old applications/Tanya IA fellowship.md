*How the proposed project would allow you to create your own niche and provide a competitive edge over the others working in the same field?* 

There is scarce health research focusing on Adivasi populations in southern Karnataka. The few papers available on this population is limited to anecdotal descriptions of poor access to care and hospital-based studies of haemoglobinopathies. The proposed research will lay the foundations for future epidemiological research on this community. By the end of the fellowship, I foresee the ability to collaborate with other researchers to build various inter-disciplinary research building upon the proposed work ranging from cohort studies on tribal populations to implementation research studies partnering with implementers and policymakers. 

*What would be your individual contribution to the proposed study?*
I will take overall responsibility for implementing activities under this project. Currently, I am based in BR Hills, a village in Chamarajanagar district of southern Karnataka at a public health research field station. I will be implementing all the activities foreseen under this project from this field station. 

I will begin in year 1 with preparatory activities including obtaining necessary permissions, MoUs with proposed partners and applying for an ethics clearance. Subsequently, I will be designing and piloting a workflow to ensure effective reporting of all deaths in tribal settlements across the district. My ongoing rapport with the district health services and the state government’s tribal welfare directorate, as well as active engagement with the Zilla Budakattu Girijana Abhivruddhi Sangha (community-based organisation of the Soliga people) will help ensure thorough coverage. I will also be creating a network of ASHA workers (community health worker at village level) in tribal areas. After setting up the reporting mechanism, I aim to be overall in charge of recruiting taluka level teams and organising medical audits for 1000 deaths. 

Later in the second year, I will adapt available social audit tools to conduct social audits for priority health conditions and finally towards the end of hte project I will be in charge of organising consultation workshops and engaging stakeholders in designing and piloting a health services intervention.

I began working on tribal health research and action about five years back with relatively small grants and have developed collaborations with researchers and institutes since then. My first grant from WHO Alliance for Health Policy and Systems Research was an implementation research grant that was selected in an open global competitive call. It allowed me to forge collaborations with the Chamarajanagar district health services and with a reputed local NGO (VGKK). In this grant, I established collaborations with local stakeholders including district health services, local NGO and the Soliga community-based organisation. The study results are to be published soon in an international peer-reviewed journal and were presented at the 2018 WHO and health systems global symposium at Liverpool. My current project (on which I am a co-investigator) supported by ICMR on health workers in tribal areas also requires extensive fieldwork and qualitative interviews with various stakeholders in the region. Under this project, I supervise a research associate and work closely with a team of researchers from health equity cluster of IPH Bangalore. 

*Research outline*

Despite overall improvements in average lifespan in India over the past decades (TS to indicate), these improvements have not been uniform. The most recent sample survey data indicates that this difference may be a staggering 17 years between an average scheduled tribe (ST) when compared to upper caste populations. Moreover, the category ST in Karnatkaka (and in many other states) includes both forest-dwelling communities (variously called *Adivasi* or *Moolanivasi*) as well as other caste groups included under the ST list. Among the ST group, the Adivasi communities are relatively disadvantaged with respect to access to health services and other adverse social determinants of health due to their remote location in and around forested areas. Several advantages of their location in clean environment and access to natural food sources have been hypothesised. However, access to healthcare and rapid changes in lifestyle and food may also be pre-disposing them to non-communicable diseases. Most of our data from nationwide surveys, however, is at the ST level; there is scarce information on the *Adivasi* communities in Karnataka such as the Soliga, Jenu Kuruba, Yerava. Their morbidity and mortality profile could be different from overall ST and other non-ST populations, but these differences have not been systematically characterised due to remote location as well as relatively small population sizes. Nonetheless, in order to achieve overall human development goals and universal health coverage, there needs to be a thorough understanding of the morbidity and mortality patterns of Adivasi communities in order to identify and act on causes of preventable deaths and design public health interventions to improve their health. 
In this study, we will characterise the mortality profile of Adivasi communities in southern Karnataka and analyse the underlying individual, systemic and social causes for these deaths. In doing so, we shall particularly seek to document possible health service gaps that could have avoided these deaths. Further, we shall involve local implementers including health system staff (PHC doctors, ASHAs and ANMs catering to tribal populations) as well as Adivasi community-based organisations and NGOs in a social dialogue on what kind of community organisation and action could contribute to mitigating the gaps. 
The objectives are:
1) To characterise causes of mortality in indigenous populations of southern Karnataka with respect to age, gender and illness 
2) To identify priority population sub-groups (by age/gender/illness) among indigenous communities based on higher sub-group mortality (when compared to non-adivasi ST and non-ST) and explore relative contributions of social, health services and biomedical factors
3) To design and implement a pilot intervention at a PHC/taluka to address major health service gap identified in objective 2 in partnership with relevant stakeholders
In relation to these objectives, the research questions and underlying hypotheses that will be tested are as follows:
1) What are the major causes of deaths of indigenous tribal communities in southern Karnataka? Is the mortality profile of these communities significantly different from other ST and non-ST communities in the state? Hypothesis: Lower average lifespan among Adivasi communties and comparable or worse-off cardiovascular/non-communicable disease deaths among Adivasis; significant differences in leading causes of deaths of women Adivasis
2) How can we explain the differential survival in a select group (by age/gender/health problems/illnesses) in indigenous people? What were the relative contributions of health servcies, social (including geographical and lifestyle characteristics) and biomedical components in explaining the difference? Hypothesis: Higher contributions of health services acceess and social determinants in explaining differnces among Adivasi deaths; social inquiry to examine cultural and other factors relating to accessing modern health services
3) What are the priority age groups/gender groups/illness profiles that could be addressed by PHC/taluka level health services intervention? What are the characteristics of an intervention that could address this gap based on the experience of a pilot intervention at taluka/PHC level in southern Karnataka? 
Methodology
For objective 1, we will set a system for tracking deaths in Adivasi communities and adapt existing tools for conducting medical and social autopsy. A team consisting of a social worker and a counsellor trained in conducting verbal autopsy shall visit the household reporting death and adminster the tool. Over a period of 40 months, we aim to complete a 1000 verbal autopsy of tribal deaths. Summary statistics and analysis of cause of deaths will be computed and comparisons with similar secondary datasets drawing from the million death study and India burden of disease study shall be done to report on differences in mortality patterns of Adivasi populations. (including preparation, 48 months) 
For objective 2, we will identify priority conditions based on differences in age/gender/illness specific mortality among study population. Among households reporting deaths due to these priority conditions, we will conduct a detailed social autopsy on 100 households (could vary depending on the specific mortality among the chosen sub-group). A mixed method approach (including summary statistics and qualitative inquiry using case study approach) will be used to identify relative contributions at multiple levels: biomedical, social (including geography), access to health services and other determinants. (24 months through years 2 and 3) 
For objective 3, based on conditions for which health services had a higher relative contribution, we will design a pilot intervention in partnership with the district health services, tribal welfare directorate at state government and local NGO/CBOs. We will implement the pilot intervention for a period of 12 months and document lessons learnt. A refined model will be developed for dissemination at the end of the pilot. (15 months including design and implementation starting y 3.5) 
Expected outcomes
For the first time, mortality profile for the Adivasi communities in southern Karnataka will be abailable along with an evidence-base for possible intervention to address priority condition. 

What considerations led you to choose this institution for your research? How does your proposed research relate to existing research at this institution?

I have been associated with IPH in various capacities in my research career. After completion of MD Community Medicine, I worked in their district health systems strengthening intervention as a Faculty and research associate. Later on (after a stint in Nagaland in Northeast India), I returned to IPH to lead a survey involving 6000 households across four districts in different parts of Karnataka as a part of a multi-country research project studying social exclusion in health financing with N Devadasan (current sponsor). Subsequently, I have continued my association as an Adjunct Faculty and continue to be engaged in research seminars, academic events and supporting IPH as a member of their ethics committee.  
In 2014 December, I set up a field station in BR Hills in collaboration with the health equity cluster of IPH. After completion of my project, the field station was handed over to the health equity cluster and currently houses THETA project (India Alliance Fellowship to Dr. Prashanth NS). Given my familiarity with policies and processes of IPH, its work culture and quality of research, and the access to field station and collaborators, I believe IPH to be best suited to host the proposed research.
