Thank you for the opportunity to review this manuscript. This study is of high public health systems significance and does a good job at systematically documenting the various barriers to access to medicines and healthcare at primary care level in India. I have few comments which must be addressed to further improve the presentation of their research.
In general the authors followed a rigorous methodology to assess various component of local healthcare delivery system, based on the well-accepted Access to Medicines in Health Systems framework by M. Bigdeli et al. In “study setting” section, the authors explain how Karnataka state has 30 districts and provide the range of population sizes. They could make it clear that Tumkar is a ‘district’ with 10 talukas, of which three were studied in this research. Also please mention that talukas are “administrative divisions with a district” to make it clear to the international readership of journal.
Please define “cluster” in Page 4 lines 51-53. Also it is unclear how the 107 clusters was identified.
The authors, on page 4, mention that 107 clusters were identified and 10 households were surveyed in each cluster. This should lead to 1070 surveyed households in total, instead of 1069 households as mentioned on Page 5 line 1. Similarly, authors mention in methods section (line 6-7, page 5) that a total of 1154 patients were included in analysis. This is different from the total patients mentioned in results section is 1149 (line 40, Page 7). Please clarify.
On Page 8 lines 12-14 and elsewhere, I would suggest replacing words “government medicines” with “medicines supplied in public-sector”. Similarly the word “combination medicines” be replaced with “fixed dose combinations”, as appropriate (for instance, last line in page 8).
On Page 8 Lines 18-19, the authors state “Medicines for secondary prevention…were not procured/supplied to PHC, BUT are…widely prescribed in the private sector”. This implies that those medicines are “not prescribed” in PHC, is that true?
On page 8 lines 22-24, authors presents their findings on availability of functional diagnostics test. The authors could provide the list of surveyed diagnostic tests in a supplementary appendix with break-down of availability of each diagnostic test across the PHC/public sector?
On page 9 lines 41-42, the authors present an important finding on “perceptions seemed to be influenced by an expectation of continuous care”. In my opinion, continuum of care and following patients over time is a need of Indian healthcare system. These aspect could be discussed in discussion section by presenting recommendations on how patients and healthcare utilization can be tracked for continuum of care, by exploring existing platforms like such as Adhaar card. For reference, see: http://www.thelancet.com/pdfs/journals/landia/PIIS2213-8587(16)30054-7.pdf
On page 11 lines 47-49, the authors state that combination medication do not have a rational basis in medical literature. This seems to be an over-speculation and is not based on any literature review done by the authors. In fact, while the utility of combination medicines is a subject of debate, there exist literature demonstrating benefits of fixed dose combinations. Please revise.
In table 5, the authors can – in additional rows - report the ‘mean percentage availability’ of diabetes medicines, CVD medicines and overall medicines, as usually done in WHO/HAI surveys.


The discussion section is well written and mentions some important solutions/approaches needed to improve the healthcare delivery at public-sector primary care level. The authors also discuss various barriers to improved public sector delivery i.e. private market forces, inadequate healthcare human resources, patients’ dependence on private sector. In this regard, I would urge authors to discuss and discuss on some previous India studies and recommendations like empowering non-physician healthcare for comprehensive delivery (see: http://bit.ly/2wsayjs , http://bit.ly/2ig8kho), controlling on financial incentives and influence of private market forces in health and medicine service delivery (see: http://gh.bmj.com/content/1/3/e000112 , http://www.thelancet.com/journals/laninf/article/PIIS1473-3099(16)30215-8/fulltext ), and improving role of cooperation between private and public sector in improving service delivery (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/26976803)  

The manuscript in its current standing is very lengthy and readers may find it hard to go through. In this regard, the authors could shorten the introduction and results section. Authors should take out some quotes and/or shorten the quotes to reduce manuscripts word count. The authors should also take out word like “sir”, “madam” etc. from these quotes and just keep the text necessary to communicate the precise point. Some quotes just not clear and need to be restructured (perhaps by including much needed commas and by providing additional text in parenthesis). For instance, the quote on Pahe 16 lines 54-58 is hard to understand. On Page 18 lines 44-56, the quote is much lengthy than need, please edit. On Page 15 lines 56-58, the word “sister” in the quote should be followed by “(nurse)” so as to make sure that international readership understand what ‘sister’ refers to here. Please review and revise all the quote in the paper for clarity.
