Thank you for the opportunity to review this proposal. The proposal seeks to apply the important scientific advancements in gene editing towards building a model for possible treatment of patients with Sickle Cell Anaemia (SCA). SCA pre-dominantly affects tribal communities, but not exclusively so. Nonetheless, given that it is among one of the neglected diseases with respect to research, it is an important priority especially towards research that moves these advancements towards therapeutics. 



(1) Are the objectives proposed relevant for the problem and the sector proposed and what is their priority for accelerating the development of the subject population?
The objectives are clearly stated and logically breakdown the steps needed to proceed from current status with respect to the CRISPR tools towards developing and testing therapeutics for SCA. The objectives while well-formulated and translated into clear work-packages, the final stage of human trials, which is a vital component has been relatively neglected with respect to details. There are for example scarce details on how this will be planned and conducted. The requisite experience and expertise in the team for patient recruitment, ethics requirements and expertise and most importantly proximity and trust with tribal communities is lacking. An important requirement would be for instance for the benefits of this proposal (if funded) to be made available to the most vulnerable tribal communities. However, this may take a backseat unless specifically stated and acted upon earlier. Typically, the most accessible patients in larger centres get priority over the most vulnerable and remotely located tribal communities. I see that there is a Co-I from Sickle Cell Institute in Central Indian region who is probably the main lead on this component (no details provided for his/her role though). This apart, the biomedical and laboratory component of the proposal are well detailed and supported by strong publications in top journals (albeit I am not an expert in these disciplines, so this may require independent assessment by disciplinary experts). 
 
(2) Do the Investigators (PI and Co-I) have the necessary expertise to undertake the project? Kindly comment on the work done by the PI and also on the strengths of the organization based on your knowledge/ information.
The biomedical component is strong, but the translation component that is expected to end with clinical trial in a small group of patients is poorly detailed out in the proposal. For instance, who shall be involved in the recruitment? What is the sample size. If it is going to be in clinical trial format, what are the ethics considerations and who shall ensure appropriate consent processes, given that this is possibly vulnerable study participants. These kind of important details are lacking. 

(3) Is the proposal well formulated and is the information provided adequate?
See response to q2 with respect to the human study participants component. That apart, the rest of the proposals and annexes provide sufficient details., 

(4) Is the methodology of the project clearly spelt out in a phased manner giving adequate details of the technical programme?
All phases clearly spelt out except for the last one involving human study participants. 

(5) What alterations and improvements would you recommend/suggest in the objectives, methodology, etc., to make the interventions more effective/eficient?
I have the following suggestions, soem of which are major enough to impact on the science and the societal relevance while others are discretionary:
- Clearer protocolisation of the human study participants component with clear outlining of sampling, consent and ethics considerations
- More specific details of the recruitment strategy for the last phase including how tribal/Adivasi communities shall be included. And if so, what kind of community-based organisations may be involved in gatekeeping and ensuring good quality consent and participation
- Last phase requires meaningful involvement of people with training/expertise in community medicine/public health, social sciences (including qualitative methods to assess receptiveness to such therapy among tribal/Adivasi communities). Preferably, NGOs with good track-record of work with tribal populations should be involved if feasible, if not at least the involvement of tribal CBOs in some capacity. 
(6) Are the milestones of progress realistic and achievable during the proposed duration of the project?
Although ambitious, team has made good effort at spelling out the steps involved. 

(7) Names of specialists/institutions, whom you consider would be able to improve the proposal, assist in monitoring, benefit from this action research etc. You may also suggest the type of linkages with any of the scientists/institutions for strengthening the S&T component of the proposal.
- There is almost no particiaptory/action research component unfortunately. The last phase is the only one that can have more participatory component, but does not appear to have any. I would suggest partnership with any leading community health/public health research institution. 
- See earlier comments too about involvement of CBOs/NGOs in areas with SCD

(8) Comment on the estimated cost of the project and the techno economic viability of the project.
- I am unable to assess the equipment due to limited expertise but the HR costs are in line with the needs of the project
(9) Please give an overall rating of the proposal on a scale of 1 to 10 with 10 being outstanding
8

(10) Final Recommendation: Recommended for Support/Recommended with minor revision/Revision/Not Recommended (please select one)
Recommended with revisions related to the last work-package especially to include greater details or even a separate section separating the direct and indirect benefits to tribal communities. Currently, the entire proposal is written as if there is only indirect benefit. Since this funding is via TSP, there will have to be more direct benefits accruing to tribal populations.
