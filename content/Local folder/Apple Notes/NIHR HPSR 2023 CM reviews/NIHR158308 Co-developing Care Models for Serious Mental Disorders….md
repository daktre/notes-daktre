Major comments

- Strong focus on participatory methods and engaging with patient/people/community perspectives in designing contextualised interventions
- Well justified argument for complexity & well argued in favour of integrating the structural/social inequalities & ongoing shocks in healthcare program design in multiple settings
- Focus on 2 countries, one LDC & LMIC is a strength
- Strong track-record of evidence generation by the team in mental health and evidence synthesis on relevant topics
- WP2 appears overly simple like a geo-spatial resource mapping activity while not sufficiently addressing the more “software” aspects of household/neighborhood/community/local govt level social networks & resources that may exist outside of facilities/buildings/NGOs which can be mapped; given their deeper engagement in both sites pre-dating the current application, this WP ideally needs more intense local engagement 
- Strong track-record of ongoing/previous work by applicants at both sites
- It is unclear if the co-produced/designed models in WP3 & 4 will have sufficient buy-in from decision-makers in the local/district health system; if not these remain hypothetical diminishing the utility of the co-production process; overall the “multi-stakeholder” component appears to under-involve decision-makers at district/state/policy levels which appears to be crucial.
- Well designed cross-cutting themes that run across WPs with a welcome focus on capacity-building


Minor comments
- Framing of the aim appears to indicate that their main interest is in theorising/explaining the *how* of multi-stakeholder engagement whereas upon reading the application, focus is much more on contextual health system design, co-production rather than theorising/explainign the former.
- Numbering the objectives & research questions is useful in review process
- The justification for the utility of simulation models in the context of two localised/contextualised solutions is not compelling to be at WP-level albeit it could be a contribution to wider/global literature on this from India/Uganda where such methods are applied less than in HIC settings.  

Overall
On Relevance of the proposed research, the research is relevant for strengthening mental healthcare in two diverse district settings in two countries, with a strong focus on co-production of health services/systems design, but with relatively limited engagement of decision-makers, potentially undermining systemic integration of the co-produced outputs. 

On Research quality and excellence, appropriate methods chosen and well conceptualised cross-cutting themes cutting across WPs; good coherence across objectives, WPs, CC themes & expected outputs. 

On the Strength of the research team, good track-record of work at both sites and leveraging non-HPSR work that’s already done/ongoing. 

On Impact and sustainability, risk of remaining an innovative solution that may not get integrated.
