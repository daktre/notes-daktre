Major comments
- Overall strong justification provided for adapting guidelines for digital health into a specific Lagos context; improving access to mental health care as per mhGAP appears to be an arena within which the team aims to contextualise digital health interventions, but this is often unclear/ambigious (for instance, where the application lists mental healthcare treatment gaps as the problem being addressed)
- Application takes a systematic approach towards assessing fit of the GRDI-HSS framework to the Lagos context and aims to contextualise it and identify f&b for its implementation through a pilot project. There is good coherence across the reserch questions and incrementally building up from RQ1 to RQ4. 
- Use of PAR approach is appropriate. However, there is insufficient detail on the iterative action-reflection cycles that they plan overall in the co-production process. Only details on how they shall ensure participation and whose participation are provided. 
- Community engagement is foreseen but with limited details on offer in terms of who shall be engaged and how
- The overall method of input, process, output, impact is being taken up with limited integration of complexity & systems thinking (which is indeed well integrated in the planning of the workshops). 
- Project, if successful will generate some practice-based evidence on this topic for Lagos albeit this may not be easily contextualised to all of Nigerian setting. May provide useful templates and inspire local adoption, but limited details on how they plan to ensure sustainability and wider use (dissemination channels are identified). 
- Some of the anticipated impact appears to be overly state and needs to be more coherently linked to the pilot intervention (improved quality of services etc) 
Minor comments
- Success criteria are too generic and some are not very well chosen (MoU with policy makers as a success criterion)
- Risk in terms of potential exclusion of non-mobile owners/people who face exclusion from digital health for other reasons not mentioned/integrated
- The involvement of statisticians and economists (mentioned in the proposal) appears confusing in terms of methodological choices. 

Overall
On Relevance of the proposed research, this proposal addresses an important opportunity presented for improving/expanding access to mental healthcare using digital technologies through aiming to adapt global guidance to the specific Lagos context in Nigeria. There is a systematic and incrementally organised research questions which end in a pilot intervention. 
 
On Research quality and excellence, PAR approach is appropriate although with insufficient detail on how the iterative process of building insights/reflectiosn will work through the project. Evaluation of the pilot intervention appears to be fair but may need a more robust framework to be used to integrate systemic learnings.  

On the Strength of the research team, there is a good skill-mix across the members with involvement of decision-makers, academia and practitioners. 

On Impact and sustainability, given early involvement of decision-makers and engagement with various health system actors, there is likely to be good ownership. However, given the methodological shortcomings (see above) in deriving lessons from the pilot, the major outputs of the project shall be the recommendations/contextualisation that occurs. If successful, more could be leveraged from the project pilot than is currently envisioned.
