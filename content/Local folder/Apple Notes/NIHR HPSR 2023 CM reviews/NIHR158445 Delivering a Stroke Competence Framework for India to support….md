Major comments
- A team that brings together a diverse range of healthcare actors for action on improving competency frameworks for stroke management in India 
- inclusion of patient groups, health worker professional body staff, clinicians (doctors, nursing professionals and other allied health worker professionals) is a strength
- Focused heavily on in-hospital care which is the tip of the iceberg; the lack of social care, caregiver support and community-based long-term care at primary health care levels, limited engagement with community health workers (ASHAs, ANMs, but also new staff such as the community health officers under Ayushman Bharat)
- Community engagement component is well defined; limited however only to participation in PCRGs and sharing inputs into guidelines/evidence
- Was well defined and work is well partitioned; WPs esp 1, 3, 4 & 5 are largely desk exercises with limited inquiry into the social determinants underpinning stroke care in community/primary healthcare settings which might result in under-representation of these components in the final outputs of the proposed work. 

Minor comments
- Success criteria are generic; more specific criteria could help the team in case assess success if this application is successful. 

Overall
 On Relevance of the proposed research, the project is relevant and of high importance given the rising incidence o stroke, more so among poor and disadvantaged communities in India and the lack of a robust framework to guide stroke care in hospital as well as community settings. 
 
On Research quality and excellence, the project is heavily focused on building evidence-based guidelines and a practice framework that integrates patient views and views of multiple healthcare professionals. There is a focus on in-hospital care which is important but with relative neglect of post-hospital care in community/PHC settings. Wider health system building blocks integration is weak and potentially impacting on sustainability as well albeit professional body invovmenet will facilitate wider acceptance by healthcare professionals. Community engagement while well defined is limited to involvement in evidence synthesis inputs. 

On the Strength of the research team, an excellent multi-stakeholder and multi-organisational team with complementary expertise

On Impact and sustainability, see above. If successful likely to be well disseminated among professional bodies and associations.
