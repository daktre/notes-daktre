Major comments

- Team is relatively experienced in clinical/biomedical components with comparatively limited HPSR/social science expertise
- Despite its focus on implementing a new technology platform in primary health care settings, there is relatively weak engagement with wider system building blocks as well as health system actors (for instance, PHC health worker involvement is limited to sharing views/perceptions on acceptability in obj 1; obj 2 and 3 have a strong biomedical & econometric focus while obj 4 is the only one on community building)
- Justification and argumetns presented in the Background and rationale section do not sufficiently examine the wider systemic issues pertinent to poor achievement of VL elimination targets, while appropriately describing the VL-specific service delivery challenges in Ethiopia and globally. 
- The goal of “strengthening primary health care for VL elimination” is distilled down to deploying “cost-effective technology” and related acceptability/feasibility issues of local staff.
- Aims and objectives state a clear hypothesis that appears to be suited for what appears to be a well-designed diagnostics efficacy trial thus only weakly falling within remit of this call. 
- Good alignment between WPs and objectives 


Minor comments
- Research is primarily identified by the team as a Evidence synthesis (see scope & call)
- Succes criterion 3 is poorly defined (“health system approach for VL treatment”)
- Strong and favourable supporting letters from institutions with good biomedical expertise for undertaking this work 
Overall
On Relevance of the proposed research, the proposal addresses an important concern for Ethiopia and for VL control globally but with relatively limited contextualisation and adaptation to Ethiopian primary health care settings. 

On Research quality and excellence, the methodological approach is better suited for a new technology feasibility trial at PHC settings (with qualitative component examining acceptability with limited involvement/engagement of PHC staff; limited engagement with decision-makers and other health system actors), and despite a strong biomedical component lacking a robust-enough health systems expertise/components.  

On the Strength of the research team, the team is multi-disciplined but with a strong econometric & biomedical focus. 

On Impact and sustainability, insufficient detail as wider health financing and governance issues are under-explored and could influence impact and sustainability.
