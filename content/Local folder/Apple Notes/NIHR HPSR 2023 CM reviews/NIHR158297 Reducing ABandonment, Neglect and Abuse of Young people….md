Major comments
- Strong regional focus on 3 country settings across LDC & LMICs in N. Africa
- Strong justification provided for focus on ACEs in this region
- Well justified intent to collaborate across the NA region with involvement of health professionals, policymakers across disciplines/sectors
- Equity focus on a region with relatively scarce coverage in evidence-base in public health/health systems
- WP2 provides limited detail on how the c/b-participatory approaches will actually be developed/tested in these multiple settings in terms of approaches, modalities and methods
- WP3 provides details on the tools (as opposed to methodological approach) to be taken in course of development and testing of evaluation methods. 
- Well described modalities for managing the consortium
- Strong track-record of previous work & leveraging that; well described focus on equitable partnership building

Minor comments
- Risk of tensions across country priorities set in WP1 being different within and across is under-considered and needs to be discussed/reflected upon on how this can be managed or in fact leveraged to produce a more comprehensive coverage of various aspects of ACE across the region
- Matched funding commitment from HIC University is a strength
- Well defined multi-lingual outputs
- Good focus on research capacity building (5 higher degrees)

Overall
On Relevance of the proposed research, the proposal is very contextually relevant and addresses an important component of human development (with its focus on addressing child rights) as well as its focus on a geographical & social setting with relatively limited engagement in health systems research. 

On Research quality and excellence, while the overall conceptualisation in terms of WPs is coherent, insufficient detail especially on the co-production (WP2) & on evaluation approach (WP3) indicating potential shortcoming in terms of conceptualising how to evaluate a multi-site participatory intervention (in terms of methodological approach and not only tool development); also underdeveloped conceptual framework to be able to evaluate/learn from this multi-site complex participatory intervention. 

On the Strength of the research team, while indeed representing multiple professions and sectors (Which is a strength), there is limited expertise in undertaking evaluation of this complex intervention and/or co-production processes proposed in multiple sites. 

On Impact and sustainability, well argued impact on people directly involved and a few indirect recipients of dissemination strategy identified but with limited details on potential pathway to policy engagement at any of the sites.
