Major comments
- Focus on youth mental health is well justified with implications for prevention, promotion in addition to early detection
- Team has experience implementing a youth-focused mental health intervention with early results but with limited details of this provided
- Multisectorality is a strength of their previous intervention: Involvement of multiple government departments; also involvement of non-health sectors is a strength
- The overall design of involving teachers, youth leaders and community health workers is good and ensures wider availability of their services. However, there are limited details on how those who are trained shall roll out their *built capacities*. A weakness of the intervention is the limited details on the systemic integration (even if not services integration) of these capacities within school curricula and/or health systems/services. 
- The strength of the proposal is the focus on early detection and improved resilience within schools that the intervention could result in, but the weakness is the limited engagement with the wider referral/care eco-system around these schools/locaitons where the intervention is rolled out. 
- Project, if funded could result in several champions of young people’s mental health wellness in Cambodia & Vietnam even if a critical mass at country level may not be met, they could inspire localised thinking on potential for such interventions in schools/communities


Minor comments
- Success criteria are heavily project activity focused
- 3 co-applicants from QMU & 3 from Cambodia & Vietnam together; with 1 of the co-applicants from QMUL at 45% FTE with limited details on partnership implications of relatively high FTEs from HIC partner 

Overall
On Relevance of the proposed research, the case for a focus on young people’s mental health in Cambodia & Vietnam is well made. Contextualisation to these two country settings is well justified and linked to existing implementation experience of the team in one country. 

On Research quality and excellence, well thought out objectives and research plan; limited systems and policy engagement despite mentioning a framework that illustrates this intent; the strength of the proposal is the focus on multi-sectoral action and especially on non-health sectors, but with relatively weak integration with health policy and systems. 

On the Strength of the research team, the team is well skilled to be able to implement this proposal if funded
  
On Impact and sustainability, see comment on systems & policy integration. There is a risk of an excellent intervention remaining so due to limited engagement/integraiton with the system/policy related to long people’s mental health care.
