Major comments
- Systematic effort at understanding demand and supply side issues using appropriate phenomenological methods and using that both for theory-building & for co-production
- A clear plan exists to involve and engage people with MHC and their caregivers
- Project leverages and builds upon existing expertise in Somaliland through previous projects
- While the descriptions are clear and the proposal is conceptually strong and backed by a good team, there are very little details on the possible nature of the co-designed intervention. Even if this cannot be described at this stage given participatory approach, the aspects of MHC that the intervention might build/achor/influence could help better understand the potential impact of the proposal
- Capcity-buildnig focus on early-career research capacity in Somaliland is a strength
- Strong equity focus in terms of catering to vulnerable population and vulnerable geography


Minor comments
- Shared ownership/responsibility for WPs across HIC/LMIC
- Relatively under-utilisation of capacity/skills from Ethiopian institution/partner

Overall
On Relevance of the proposed research, the proposal is of high relevance and appropriately contextualised to the Somaliland setting. 
On Research quality and excellence, there is a systematic thinking through of the WPs and the steps starting from mapping and listening/understanding to the later steps on action. PAR and phenomenological approaches are appropriately used. 

On the Strength of the research team, multi-country team with complementary skill-set and good focus on capacity-building in Somaliland.  
  
On Impact and sustainability, given the socio-economic and political situation in the context, huge challenges remain in terms of sustainability. Directly impact to several people who are likely to be involved is well demonstrated but system impact appears to be quite challenging even if this is outside the control of the team, these will affect sustainability.
