*What ails India’s COVID-19 rural response? A call for action from Chamarajanagar* 

The COVID-19 second wave has aggravated pre-existing social vulnerabilities that were always plaguing access to healthcare for rural communities.  


The second wave of COVID-19 has had an unprecedented impact on India. While English social media is overwhelmed with SOS messages for ICUs and ventilators, rural India’s gasp has often been crowded out. Only the most horrifying of tragedies have received focus while everyday difficulties in accessing basic care for COVID-19 is often not interesting enough to capture space in the fast-paced COVID-19 coverage. Chamarajanagar which had attained fame during the first wave as being *evergreen* (in terms of total COVID-19 cases) saw the tragic death of at least 28 people, which a Government report held was due to “……”. The incident brought to the fore the nature of the pandemic’s impact in rural areas, where decades of neglect have resulted poor access to modern healthcare. But more importantly, the overall trust in public services was already so low that many people often prefer catastrophic and often irrational out-of-pocket payments to unregulated private sector in rural areas, to on-paper free treatment at district and taluka hospitals. While the pandemic’s horrible devastation brings rural district hospitals albeit for a day or two to front-page national dailies, this is the time to pause and examine if these are merely due to the overwhelming of our rural facilities or, as we contend here, the expected outcomes of neglect and apathy towards strengthening publicly funded primary health care and hospitals in rural areas.
