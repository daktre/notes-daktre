# Video Resource: Prioritisation of KFD Risk Factors

**Context**: Educational Video Development / Research Output
**Affiliation**: Institute of Tropical Medicine (ITM), Antwerp / Institute of Public Health (IPH)
**Topic**: Kyasanur Forest Disease (KFD) - a tick-borne hemorrhagic fever

## Context
This resource is linked to Dr. Srinivas's deep research into **Kyasanur Forest Disease (KFD)**, also known as Monkey Fever. The video/presentation focuses on a "One Health" approach, trying to identify and prioritize which factors most contribute to the spread of this disease in the Western Ghats.

## Core Arguments & Key Takeaways

### 1. Landscape & Land Use Changes (Top Risk Factor)
*   **Argument**: The primary driver of KFD isn't just the virus, but how humans change the land.
*   **Key Finding**: **Forest loss and degradation** are the highest-ranked risk factors. "Mosaic" landscapes—where you have a mix of forest, paddy fields, and scrub around villages—create the perfect interface for ticks to transfer from animals to humans.

### 2. The Role of Cattle
*   **Argument**: Cattle are a critical link in the amplification of tick populations.
*   **Key Finding**: Grazing cattle inside forests brings them into contact with ticks, which they then carry back to human habitations. This management of livestock is a key intervention point.

### 3. Knowledge Co-Production
*   **Methodological Argument**: You cannot solve this with just doctors or virologists. You need "knowledge co-production" involving forest dwellers, veterinarians, and local administration.
*   **Takeaway**: Effective disease prediction models must include **local knowledge** and behavioral factors (like who goes into the forest and why), not just biological data.

### 4. Systemic Deficiencies
*   **Argument**: The risk isn't just biological; it's systemic.
*   **Takeaway**: Lack of awareness, low vaccination acceptance, and under-reporting of monkey deaths (a warning sign) are "public health system" risk factors that are just as dangerous as the virus itself.
