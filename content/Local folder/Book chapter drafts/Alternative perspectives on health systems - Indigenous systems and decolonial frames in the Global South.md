Alternative title: _Indigenous health systems as political orders: epistemic justice, cultural safety, and governance beyond the health(care) services

This chapter reframes alternative health systems as distinct epistemic, political, and relational orders often rendered invisible through institutional exclusion and epistemic marginalisation. Viewing *alternatives* as marginal conceptualisations or add-ons/options to formal state systems or market systems is insufficient to be able to overcome these phenomena rooted in historical and social phenomena which lead to their current configuraiotns as *alternative*. Building on Indigenous and decolonial scholarship, it argues that health systems research in the Global South must attend to land, sovereignty, cultural safety and dignity, and cognitive justice as constitutive of health and care. Empirically, the chapter uses South Indian Adivasi experiences (e.g., Soliga and Jenukuruba contexts) to illustrate how Indigenous systems of wellbeing operate through household practices, social networks, benevolence and informal kindness, and community authority, and how their influences often contests with public systems through fear, mistrust, and punitive governance. The chapter then proposes a research agenda and methodological guidance which is rooted in dialogic, practitioner-based, and community-governed processes so that health systems research can more credibly study and engage these alternative orders.

## **6. Research & methods: methodological guidance you can later expand**

  

(Directly consistent with your brainstorm and the volume guidance.)   

  

### **6.1 Methodological stance (placeholder)**

- **Dialogic / relational** designs (community authority as co-theorist, not “informant”)
    
- **Practitioner-based** inquiry (work with healers, elders, community health actors)
    
- **Ethics beyond IRB**: governance, ownership, controlled access, benefit sharing
    
- **Reflexivity on translation**: avoid flattening concepts into biomedical proxies
    
- **Community-governed outputs**: bilingual outputs, local archiving, reciprocal utility
    

  

### **6.2 Candidate methods (menu)**

- Ethnography + institutional ethnography of interfaces (PHC–community; FD–community)
    
- Participatory action research; community advisory councils with decision rights
    
- Oral history and narrative methods (especially for displacement/violence histories)
    
- Systems mapping **from within** communities (emic system maps of care pathways)
    
- “Rapid” but respectful approaches for emergencies (avoid extractive outbreak anthropology)
